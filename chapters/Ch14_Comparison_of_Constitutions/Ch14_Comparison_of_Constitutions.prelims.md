## CHAPTER 03
PARLIAMENTARY VS PRESIDENTIAL
THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter
1. REPEATS

#### A. The Core Distinction
- Parliamentary government means the executive is responsible to the legislature and removable by it [2020].
- India qualifies because the Council of Ministers answers to the Lok Sabha [2015].
- The stated advantage is continued responsibility, not independence of organs [2017].
- The Cabinet form rests on collective responsibility through parliamentary democracy [2017].

#### B. Britain Compared with India
- The British Parliament is sovereign; Indian law making is limited by the Constitution [2021].
- Amendment validity goes to a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court [2021].
- India has an elected President and a written Constitution; Britain has a hereditary monarch.
- India has no genuine shadow Cabinet and no legal responsibility by countersignature.

#### C. The Presidential Model and Borrowings
- One directly elected office combines Head of State and Head of Government.
- That executive sits outside the legislature, holds fixed tenure and faces no dissolution.
- Removal is only by impeachment; ministers answer to the head, not the legislature.
- India took Fundamental Rights, judicial review and impeachment from the US Constitution.
2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED
- 2015 to 2020: single answer definitions of parliamentary and Cabinet government [2015, 2017, 2020]
- 2021: a two statement comparison of the British and Indian models [2021]
3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW
- No question since 2021; the live angle is comparison, parliamentary sovereignty in Britain against limited legislative
power in India [2021]

1. CORE DISTINCTION
The dividing line is the relationship between the executive and the legislature. In the parliamentary system the executive is
drawn from and answerable to the legislature; in the presidential system the two are separately elected and mutually
independent.

Responsibility
[PRELIMS
2020,2017,2015]
- Collective and individual
responsibility to the legislature.
- Non responsible to the legislature, answerable directly
to the electorate. The legislature cannot remove it for
policy failure.
Membership - Double membership: ministers sit
and vote in the legislature.
- Single membership: Secretaries cannot be legislators.
Tenure - No fixed tenure, removable any day
on losing confidence.
- Fixed tenure, removable only by impeachment for a
defined constitutional offence.
Dissolution - Lower house can be dissolved
before its term
- Lower house cannot be dissolved by the executive
Powers - India has only a functional, not a
rigid, separation.
- MERIT: Harmony between the
executive and the legislature, so a
deadlock is structurally unlikely.
- India, Separation of Powers is part
of the Basic Structure (Indira
Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain, 1975).
- Hard Separation of powers with checks and balances.
- DEMERIT: conflict. Separate mandates invite friction
and divided governments can produce paralysis.
Political
homogeneity
- Usually present, ministry drawn
from one party or coalition.
- May not exist. Divided government is normal.
- DEMERIT: Narrow representation. The President
appoints a team of his own choosing, answerable only
to him.
