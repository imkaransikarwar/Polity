| Aspect | Indian Secularism | U.S. Secularism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model Type | Positive secularism - Equal respect to all religions | Neutral secularism - Strict separation of Church and State |
| Constitutional Mention | Explicitly mentioned in the Preamble (added by 42nd Amendment, 1976) | Not in Preamble, but derived from the First Amendment |
| State-Religion Relationship | State can intervene in religion to ensure equality and reform (Eg. temple entry, untouchability) | State maintains complete non-interference with religion |
| Religious Freedom | Article 25-28 guarantee freedom of religion with reasonable restrictions | First Amendment guarantees absolute religious freedom and bars state religion |
| State Funding/Support | State can fund or regulate religious institutions for social reform | No government aid to religious institutions allowed |
| Religious Symbols in Public Institutions | Permissible in some contexts (Eg. festivals, public holidays) | Generally not allowed in public/government institutions |

### Similarities

- **1.** Dual Polity Structure: India has 28 states and 8 Union Territories, while the US has 50 states.

- **2.** Written and Supreme Constitution: reflected in Article VI of the US Constitution (Supremacy Clause) and the "Basic Structure" doctrine in India.

- **3.** Independent Judiciary that acts as the final arbiter of constitutional disputes between the center and states.

- **4.** Bicameralism: India's Rajya Sabha and the US Senate both serve as "Houses of States".

- **5.** Division of Powers: India uses the Seventh Schedule, while the US uses the Enumerated Powers (Article I, Section 8).

- **6.** Constitutional Amendment Procedure: In India, federal amendments require ratification by 50% of state legislatures (Article 368); in the US, they require three-fourths of states.

| Indian Federation | US Federation |
