| Indian Federation | US Federation |
| --- | --- |
| Vested in the Centre. (Entry 97) | Reserved for the States or the People. |
| Single Constitution for all states. | Each state has its own separate Constitution. |
| Representation in Upper House - Population-based Representation. | Equal Representation of States. |
| Under Articles 352 & 356, Centre can assume control over states. | US President cannot dismiss a state government or legislature. |
| Integrated Judiciary & Services. | Dual Judiciary & Separate Services. |

| India | USA |
| --- | --- |
| President can be impeached only for "Violation of the Constitution" under Article 61, which is not clearly defined. | President can be removed for "Treason, Bribery, or other high Crimes and Misdemeanors", allowing wider criminal and ethical grounds. |
| Charges can be initiated by either House of Parliament (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha). | Impeachment can be initiated only by the House of Representatives (Lower House). |
| Motion must be signed by at least 1/4th (25%) of total membership of the initiating House. | Requires only a simple majority in the House of Representatives to impeach. |
| The other House of Parliament investigates the charges and acts as a quasi-judicial body. | The Senate acts as the sole court for impeachment trial. |
| Regular presiding officer (Speaker/Chairman) manages proceedings; no judicial involvement. | The Chief Justice of the Supreme Court presides over the Senate trial. |
| Requires 2/3rd majority of total membership of each House, making removal very difficult. | Requires 2/3rd majority of members present in the Senate, which may be comparatively lower. |
| President has a constitutional right to appear and be represented during investigation. | President is usually represented by legal counsel, rarely appears personally. |
| Removed President simply vacates office. | Senate may also vote to permanently disqualify the person from holding future federal office. |
| No Indian President has ever been impeached. | Three Presidents impeached by house (Johnson, Clinton, Trump twice), but none convicted by Senate. |
