### Judiciary

### Similarities

- **1.** Judicial Review:

  - **a.** India: Kesavananda Bharati (1973) - "Basic Structure" of the Constitution.

  - **b.** US: Marbury v. Madison (1803) - Supreme Court can invalidate acts of Congress.

- **2.** Independence of Judiciary:

  - **a.** India: Salary and allowances of judges are charged to the Consolidated Fund.

  - **b.** US: Article III - judges' salaries cannot be diminished during their time in office.

- **3.** Guardian of Rights:

  - **a.** India: Article 32 allows citizens to move the SC directly for FR violations

  - **b.** US: The "Bill of Rights" - basis for the Court to protect FR.

- **4.** Adversarial Model: Eg. In high-profile criminal trials in both nations, the burden of proof lies with the prosecution to prove guilt "beyond a reasonable doubt."

- **5.** Interpretive Supremacy:

  - **a.** India: Article 141 states that the law declared by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts within India.

  - **b.** US: The "Supremacy Clause" (Article VI) ensures that federal judicial decisions override conflicting state laws.

- **6.** Writ Jurisdiction: Eg. Both courts frequently issue Habeas Corpus to secure the release of persons illegally detained by the state.

- **7.** Common Law Roots: Over 80% of legal terminology and foundational principles in India (Eg. Tort, Equity) mirror those used in the US federal courts.
