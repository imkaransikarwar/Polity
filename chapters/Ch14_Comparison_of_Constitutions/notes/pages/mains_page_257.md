### Executive

### Similarities

- **1.** Both governments continue to derive authority from the Constitution. Eg. Article II (US) and Articles 52-78 (India).

- **2.** President as Commander-in-Chief: Article II, Sec 2 (US) and Article 53 (India).

- **3.** Pardoning Powers: Both executives hold the judicial power to grant pardons, reprieves, or remissions of punishment.

- **4.** Veto Powers: Both have the power to withhold assent to bills passed by the legislature.

- **5.** Impeachment Process: The Indian President can be impeached for "Violation of the Constitution," while the US President faces it for "Treason, Bribery, or High Crimes".

| Indian Executive (Parliamentary) | US Executive (Presidential) |
| --- | --- |
| Dual Head - President is the Head of State (titular); Prime Minister is the Head of Government (real executive). | Single Head - The President is both Head of State and Head of Government. |
| Part of Legislature - The PM and Council of Ministers must be members of Parliament. | Strict Separation - The President and Cabinet cannot be members of Congress. |
| Collective Responsibility - The Cabinet is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. | Individual Responsibility - The President is not responsible to the legislature for executive actions. |
| Indirect Election - The President is elected by an Electoral College of MPs and MLAs. | Popular + Electoral College - The President is elected through popular vote leading to Electoral College selection. |
| 5 Years; No Term Limit - Can be re-elected any number of times. | 4 Years; Two-Term Limit - Restricted by the 22nd Amendment. |
| Parliamentary Cabinet - Ministers are peers/colleagues of the PM within the legislature. | "Kitchen Cabinet" Style - Members are often experts/advisors, responsible only to the President. |
| Suspensive Veto - If Parliament passes the returned bill again, the President must sign. | Qualified Veto - Requires 2/3 majority in both Houses of Congress to override. |
