### Legislature

### Similarities

- **1.** Bicameralism: India has the LS and RS; the US has the House of Representatives and Senate.

- **2.** Representative Democracy: direct elections for their lower houses based on adult suffrage.

- **3.** Privileges: Legislators enjoy freedom of speech within the house to ensure robust debate. Eg. Article 105 in India and the Speech or Debate Clause (Article I) in the US Constitution.

- **4.** Role of the Vice President: serves as the presiding officer of the Upper House.

- **5.** Legislative Autonomy: Indian courts cannot inquire into proceedings of Parliament under Article 122, similar to the US "Political Question" doctrine.

- **6.** Committees System: Both utilize Standing Committees and Select Committees to handle the heavy volume of legislative work.

- **7.** Joint Sittings/Conferences: India uses Joint Sittings (Article 108), while the US uses Conference Committees to align differing versions of a bill.

- **8.** Impeachment Powers: Both legislatures hold the power to remove the Head of State and high judicial officials.

| Indian Parliament | US Congress |
| --- | --- |
| President is an integral part of Parliament | The President is not a part of Congress. |
| Fused System - The Executive (Cabinet) must be members of Parliament. | Separated System - No member of the Executive can be a member of Congress. |
| Rajya Sabha has limited powers over Money Bills and in joint sittings. | The Senate is often considered one of the most powerful upper houses globally. |
| Population-based Representation in RS | Equal Representation - Each state gets exactly 2 Senators |
| The President can dissolve the Lok Sabha on the advice of the Prime Minister. | The President cannot dissolve either house of Congress. |
| Lok Sabha - 5 years; Rajya Sabha - Permanent body with 6-year terms. | House of Representatives - 2 years; Senate - 6 years. |
| Only Parliament can initiate a constitutional amendment. | Amendments can be initiated by Congress or the States. |
| Residuary powers - Vested in the Union Parliament. | Vested in the States under the 10th Amendment. |
