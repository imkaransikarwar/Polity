- Two routes at once: Bribing voters or booth capturing is a corrupt practice under Sec 123 (civil route, 8A) and an electoral offence under the BNS or Sec 135A (criminal route, 8); the two proceed independently.

- The integrity route involves no wrong: it is the statutory cousin of the constitutional office of profit bar in Arts 102(1)(a), 191(1)(a), which is why the Returning Officer, not a court, decides it at nomination. Disclosure and criminal antecedents

- Affidavit (Form 26, Sec 33A): Pending cases with charges framed for offences punishable with 2 years or more, past convictions, assets, liabilities and education. The duty flows from Union of India v ADR (2002) and PUCL (2003), which struck down the provision enacted to undo ADR.

- Parties must publish candidates’ criminal antecedents in newspapers, on TV and on their website, with reasons for selection (Public Interest Foundation, 2018; 2020 contempt order), enforced through ECI directions under Art 324.

- Special courts try cases against sitting and former legislators, on the Supreme Court’s directions. Traps

- The disclosure duty rests on the party as well as the candidate.

### POLITICAL PARTIES, FUNDING AND EXPENDITURE

Registration (Sec 29A, RPA 1951)

- Application to the ECI within 30 days of formation; the memorandum must pledge true faith and allegiance to the Constitution, to socialism, secularism and democracy, and to the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.

- The same Part governs who may donate, and disclosure. Traps

- Registration is not recognition.

- The ECI has no general power to deregister (INC(I) v Institute of Social Welfare, 2002), except for fraud, a party ceasing to be lawful, or renouncing allegiance to the Constitution.

- Delisting inactive Registered Unrecognised Political Parties (RUPPs) that have not contested for six years is not deregistration. Recognition and symbols (Symbols Order, 1968)

- Issued by the ECI under Art 324, not an Act of Parliament (Sadiq Ali v ECI, 1972); governs recognition, symbol allotment and disputes on splits and mergers (Para 15).

- State party, any one of:

- 3% of Assembly seats (minimum 3);

- 1 Lok Sabha seat for every 25 allotted to the State;

- 6% of votes plus 1 Lok Sabha seat or 2 Assembly seats;

- 8% of votes in the State, whatever the seats.

- National party, any one of:

- 2% of Lok Sabha seats (11) from at least 3 States;

- 6% of votes in 4 States plus 4 Lok Sabha seats;

- Recognised as a State party in 4 States.

- Benefits: A reserved symbol, one proposer, free AIR and Doordarshan time, and up to 40 star campaigners (20 for other parties). Traps

- Registration is statutory; recognition is not, since the Symbols Order is an ECI order.

- Since 2016, recognition is reviewed every two general elections. Expenditure ceiling

- Fixed by the Central Government by rules under the RPA, 1951, last revised in 2022: Lok Sabha ₹95 lakh (₹75 lakh in smaller States and UTs); Assembly ₹40 lakh (₹28 lakh).

- The account is lodged with the DEO within 30 days of the result; failure brings 3 years’ disqualification. Traps
