### THE TWO REPRESENTATION OF THE PEOPLE ACTS

How to tell the two Acts apart

- The RPA, 1950 builds the stage: who may vote, the electoral roll, and how many seats each State has.

- The RPA, 1951 runs the play: qualifications and disqualifications, party registration, nomination, polling, corrupt practices, results and election petitions.

- Where an item is constitutional, the Act supplies the machinery.

- Party registration entered the law only in 1989, through the RPA, 1951; the RPA, 1950 nowhere mentions political parties. Trap: The Model Code of Conduct, party symbols, NOTA, electoral bonds belong to neither Act.

### SEATS AND DELIMITATION

Allocation of seats (RPA, 1950)

- State wise seats in the Lok Sabha (First Schedule), Assemblies (Second Schedule) and Councils (Third Schedule), with SC/ST reserved seats and the manner of filling Council seats.

- The ceilings come from the Constitution (Art 81 for the Lok Sabha, Art 170 for Assemblies); the distribution among States comes from the Act. Traps

- Rajya Sabha seats are allotted in the Fourth Schedule of the Constitution, not the RPA. Delimitation Commission (Delimitation Act, 2002)

- Members: A serving or retired Supreme Court Judge (Chairperson, appointed by the Centre); the CEC or an EC nominated by him; and the State Election Commissioner of the State concerned (both ex officio).

- Associate members (MPs and MLAs of the State) assist but have no vote and sign no decision.

- Orders are published in the Gazette and laid before the Lok Sabha and the Assembly concerned (Sec 10); they apply from the next general election. The ECI keeps them updated for minor corrections under the RPA, 1950. Traps

- The Commission is statutory, unlike the ECI and the Finance Commission.

- Orders have the force of law: legislatures cannot modify them and courts cannot question them (Art 329(a); Meghraj Kothari, 1967), except where delimitation is manifestly arbitrary (Kishorchandra Rathod, 2024). Delimitation exercises so far [PRELIMS 2024]

- Four Commissions: 1952, 1963, 1973 and 2002 (under the Acts of 1952, 1962, 1972 and 2002).

- The 2002 Commission (Justice Kuldip Singh) finalised its orders in 2008, leaving out J&K, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur and Nagaland.

- Two later exercises, not Commissions under Art 82:

- J&K: Under the J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019; 90 Assembly seats (43 Jammu, 47 Kashmir).

- Assam: By the ECI under the RPA, 1950; 126 Assembly and 14 Lok Sabha seats kept. Traps

- The count stays at four; the J&K and Assam exercises do not add to it. Women’s reservation (106th Amendment, 2023)

- Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam: Inserted Arts 330A, 332A and a proviso to Art 239AA(2)(b); one third of seats in the Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and Delhi Assembly, including within SC/ST reserved seats, for 15 years.

- In force from 16 April 2026, but reservation applies only after delimitation following the first census taken after commencement. Traps

- Constitutional but not yet operational; the 131st Amendment Bill, 2026, which sought to advance it, was defeated.

- Rajya Sabha and Councils are excluded; there is no OBC sub quota.
