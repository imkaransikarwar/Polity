### CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Part XV at a glance (Arts 324 to 329)

- Art 324: Election Commission of India; superintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures and the offices of President and Vice President.

- Art 325: One general electoral roll per constituency; no one excluded only on grounds of religion, race, caste or sex; no separate communal electorates.

- Art 326: Adult suffrage; every citizen aged 18+ who is not disqualified may be registered. Age lowered from 21 to 18 by the 61st Amendment, 1988.

- Arts 327, 328: Parliament legislates on all election matters (used for the RPA, 1950 and RPA, 1951); the State Legislature only where Parliament has not. Both are “subject to the provisions of this Constitution”.

- Art 329: Bar on courts: (a) laws on delimitation and seat allotment; (b) elections questioned only by election petition. Traps

- Part XV has no article on political parties. Parties got constitutional mention only in the Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985), which defines “legislature party” and “original political party” but not “political party”.

- Local body elections are outside Part XV; State Election Commissions run them (Arts 243K, 243ZA).

- Art 325 lists only religion, race, caste and sex; place of birth, descent and residence are not in it, and the RPA, 1950 requires ordinary residence.

- Art 326 applies only to the Lok Sabha and Assemblies, not to indirectly elected bodies (Rajya Sabha, Councils, President, Vice President). Vote at 18, contest at 25.

- The voting age came by the 61st, not the 62nd Amendment (which extended SC/ST and Anglo-Indian reservation).

- State Assemblies to formulate election laws only in so far as Parliament has not already made a provision on the matter. Election Commission of India (Art 324)

- Composition: The CEC and such number of Election Commissioners as the President fixes; three members at present, but the Constitution fixes no number.

- Appointment: By the President on the recommendation of a Selection Committee of the Prime Minister, a Union Cabinet Minister and the Leader of the Opposition (CEC and other ECs Act, 2023).

- Removal: The CEC only like a Supreme Court Judge; other ECs only on the CEC’s recommendation (Art 324(5)).

- Reservoir of power: Acts where the law is silent (Mohinder Singh Gill, 1978): by elections, symbols, party recognition, Model Code of Conduct, observers, countermanding a poll.

- Limit: Cannot act contrary to existing law. In A.C. Jose v Sivan Pillai (1984), EVMs used without statutory backing were set aside, so Parliament had to legislate. Traps

- Outside the ECI: panchayat and municipal polls (SECs), the election of the Speaker/Deputy Speaker, and nominated Rajya Sabha members.

- The Ministry of Home Affairs has no role in fixing the election schedule; it is the ECI alone. ECI versus State Election Commission (Arts 243K, 243ZA)

- SEC: Created by the 73rd and 74th Amendments, 1992; appointed by the Governor; conducts panchayat and municipal rolls and elections.

- Protection: The SEC is removable only like a High Court Judge, and his service conditions cannot be varied to his disadvantage; the CEC is protected like a Supreme Court Judge. Traps

- The SEC is wholly independent of the ECI; the ECI has no supervisory or appellate role over it.

- The State Election Commissioner sits ex officio on the Delimitation Commission for his State (Delimitation Act, 2002).
