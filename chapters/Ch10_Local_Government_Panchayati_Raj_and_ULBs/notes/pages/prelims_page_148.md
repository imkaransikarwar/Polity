| --- | --- | --- |
| **Governing body, nature of powers and Union control** *(Contd.)* | **3.** The Governor reports annually, or when required, to the President on their administration (Para 3).<br>**4.** The Union's executive power extends only to giving directions to the State; no takeover of administration. | — |
| Declaration and alteration of areas [PRELIMS 2023] | **1.** The President declares an area a Scheduled Area by order and may increase, decrease, rename or rescind it (Para 6).<br>**2.** Done in consultation with the Governor. | **1.** The Governor creates, increases, decreases, renames and dissolves autonomous districts, on a Commission's recommendation.<br>**2.** No Presidential role. |
| Composition of the advisory or governing body | **1.** Tribes Advisory Council of up to 20 members, three-fourths of them representatives of the Scheduled Tribes in the State Legislative Assembly (Para 4).<br>**2.** It advises the Governor, who rules on its membership and mode of appointment. | **1.** Autonomous District Council of not more than 30 members - up to 4 nominated by the Governor, 26 elected, for a five-year term.<br>**2.** Exception: the Bodoland Territorial Council may have 46 members - 40 elected, 6 nominated. |
| Legislative powers | **1.** No direct law making power vests in the tribal population; the State Legislature legislates for a Scheduled Area in the ordinary way.<br>**2.** The Governor may make regulations for the peace and good government of the area, with the President's assent (Para 5).<br>**3.** Such a regulation may repeal or amend an Act of Parliament or of the State legislature in its application to the area. | **1.** The Councils have real law making power, exercised through Bills passed by the Council (Para 3).<br>**2.** Subjects: land other than reserved forest, management of forests other than reserved forest, shifting cultivation, village and town administration including village police and public health, inheritance of property, marriage and divorce, social customs.<br>**3.** Every such law takes effect only on the Governor's assent (Para 3). |
| Governor's role: regulation making and protective safeguards [PRELIMS 2022,2019] | **1.** The Governor may make regulations, requiring the President's assent, to restrict transfer of land by or among Scheduled Tribes and to regulate money lending (Para 5).<br>**2.** Source of the power to declare a transfer of tribal land, including for mining, null and void.<br>**3.** The role is dominant: the Governor is the custodian of Scheduled Areas and his powers here are wider than anywhere else in the Constitution. | **1.** Protection works through the Councils' own legislative and judicial powers, not gubernatorial regulations.<br>**2.** The Governor's main role is to give assent to Council laws and regulations (Para 3).<br>**3.** The Governor may alter or dissolve a Council, but day to day powers rest with the Council. |
| Applicability of Union and State laws | **1.** An Act of Parliament or the State legislature applies by default.<br>**2.** The Governor may direct that it shall not apply, or apply with modifications (Para 5). | **1.** On matters within the Councils' competence, Acts of Parliament or the State legislature do not apply of their own force.<br>**2.** For other matters the Governor may direct non-application or modified application. |
| Judicial powers | **1.** None. The regular State judiciary and the ordinary civil and criminal courts operate in Scheduled Areas.<br>**2.** Customary law is protected, if at all, through the Governor's regulations and not through tribal courts. | **1.** The Councils may constitute village councils and courts to try suits and cases between parties all of whom belong to Scheduled Tribes of the area (Para 4).<br>**2.** They apply customary law; the Governor may confer wider civil and criminal jurisdiction on them.<br>**3.** Appeals lie to the District Council and thereafter to the High Court as the Governor specifies. |
