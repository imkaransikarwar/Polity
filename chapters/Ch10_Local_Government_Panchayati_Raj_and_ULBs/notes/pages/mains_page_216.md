- **2.** Functional Challenges

  - **a.** Parastatal bodies control key functions like water, and transport leading to "responsibility without power" syndrome for ULBs. Eg. Delhi Jal Board

  - **b.** Schemes like JNNURM (2005) and Smart Cities Mission (2015) implemented via SPVs. SPVs, led by bureaucrats, indicate "procedural distrust" of local democracy.

  - **c.** Incomplete devolution: Most States haven't transferred all 18 functions under the 12th Schedule.

  - **d.** Inactive District Planning Committees (DPCs): Non-functional in 9 States.

  - **e.** Urban planning remains reactive and fragmented rather than long-term and integrated with transit.

  - **f.** 65% of Indian cities still operate without updated Master Plans. (NITI Aayog)

- **3.** Functionary Challenges

  - **a.** Election delays: Eg. BBMP (Bengaluru) vacant since 2020.

  - **b.** Proxy representation: "Corporator Pati" syndrome weakens women's empowerment.

  - **c.** State-appointed Municipal Commissioners overshadow elected mayors.

  - **d.** Staff shortage: 42% of posts vacant (2022), leading to poor service delivery.

  - **e.** Absence of a dedicated Municipal Administrative Service in many states.

- **4.** Functionality Challenges

  - **a.** Neo-liberal urban policy bias: Focus on "competitive cities" and investment-driven growth has sidelined democratic decentralisation. (RBI)

  - **b.** Weak Citizen Participation Structures - Ward committees and area sabhas are non-functional or absent in most states. Only 5 out of 35 states have legally mandated participatory bodies.

  - **c.** Rapid urbanisation strains water, housing, and sanitation systems. Eg. Bengaluru floods (2022) and Mumbai water shortages show planning failures.

  - **d.** 17% of urban households live in slums; 11 million homes vacant (ORF, 2023).

  - **e.** Ceremonial Mayors: In most Indian cities, the Mayor has no executive power, which instead rests with the state-appointed Commissioner.

  - **f.** Audit Lags: Delayed and non-transparent audits lead to poor financial credibility and loss of performance grants. g. Legal Constraints: ULBs lack the freedom to set their own tax rates or borrow without stringent state approvals. Greater Bengaluru Governance Bill (2024):

- Creates Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) headed by the Chief Minister and Chief Commissioner (bureaucrat).

- Empowers GBA to alter ULB boundaries and override elected councils.

- This effectively removes even the symbolic layer of decentralisation.
