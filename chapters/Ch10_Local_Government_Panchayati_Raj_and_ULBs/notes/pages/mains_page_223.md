### Constitutional Provisions

- **1.** Article 244(2): Specifically provides that the provisions of the Sixth Schedule shall apply to the administration of the tribal areas in the four North-Eastern states.

- **2.** Article 275(1): Mandates special Grants-in-aid from the Consolidated Fund of India to these areas for development and administration.

- **3.** Governor's Role: The Governor has the power to create, reorganize, or change the names/boundaries of autonomous districts.

### Benefits of the Sixth Schedule

- **1.** Cultural Preservation: Protects the unique "civilizational" identity of North-Eastern tribes

- **2.** Prevents the alienation of tribal land by prohibiting its transfer to non-tribals.

- **3.** Recognizes traditional dispute resolution systems for tribal disputes.

- **4.** Legislative Self-Rule: Empowers councils to make laws on subjects like shifting cultivation, watercourses, and local social customs.

- **5.** Fiscal Empowerment: collect land revenue and taxes on professions, vehicles, and trades.

- **6.** Resource Management: Allows tribal communities to manage community forests (excluding reserved forests) and local water resources.

- **7.** Protection from Exploitation: Regulates money-lending and trading by non-tribals to prevent debt traps and economic marginalization.

- **8.** Political Representation: Ensures that minority tribal groups within a state have a dedicated platform for political expression.

- **9.** Countering Insurgency: Eg. The creation of the Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR) ended decades of militancy through constitutional accommodation.

- **10.** Customary Law Recognition: Safeguards the traditional social fabric by making Governor's assent mandatory for laws affecting tribal customs.

### Major Issues in Sixth Schedule Areas

- **1.** Overlapping jurisdictions lead to frequent power struggles between the state legislature and the councils.

- **2.** No mandatory reservation for women. Women's representation in most ADCs below 5%

- **3.** Absence of Anti-Defection Law, leading to instability and "horse-trading."

- **4.** Financial Dependency: Despite taxing powers, most councils remain heavily dependent on discretionary state grants, limiting actual autonomy.

- **5.** Bureaucratic Hegemony: Eg. In Assam, the "Principal Secretary" to the council is an IAS officer reporting to the state.

- **6.** Incomplete Power Transfer: Eg. Departments like PWD and Irrigation in Tripura's ADC often remain under state control in practice.

- **7.** Power is often concentrated among a few "Tribal Elites."

- **8.** Corruption cases in the Chakma Council (2025) highlighted the misappropriation of funds by high-ranking members.

- **9.** Non-tribal residents lack basic voting rights and land ownership, causing social friction.

- **10.** There is no strict constitutional time limit for holding fresh elections if a council is dissolved.
