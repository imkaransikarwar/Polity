### Steps Taken by Government

- **1.** AMRUT: Centre provides an incentive of ₹13 crore for every ₹100 crore raised via bonds.

- **2.** Credit Repayment Guarantee Fund (CRGSS)

- **3.** SEBI has simplified the "Listing of Municipal Debt Securities" norms and introduced a "Municipal Bond Information Repository."

- **4.** GST Compensation Integration: Allowing states to use a portion of GST compensation as a "Sinking Fund" to guarantee bond repayments.

### Way Forward

- **1.** Municipal Recovery Fund to act as a backstop in case of temporary repayment delays (Global Practice: USA's Bond Insurance).

- **2.** Scale up Pooled Finance Development Fund (PFDF) model where several small ULBs "pool" their requirements to issue a single, large-value bond.

- **3.** Complete 100% GIS-based property tax mapping to double municipal revenue (recommendation of HPEC on Urban Infrastructure).

- **4.** Create a specialized "Municipal Administrative Service" to provide professional financial managers to ULBs.

- **5.** Grant "Tax-Free" status to bonds used for water and sanitation projects to attract retail investors.

### Direct Election of Mayor

The 74th Constitutional Amendment Act (1992) provides for urban local bodies but does not prescribe the mode of election of mayors. Hence, the method of mayoral election is decided by State legislatures under Article 243R of the Constitution.

- **1.** Active Direct Election: Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Telangana, and Uttarakhand.

- **2.** Fluctuating Systems: States like Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu have frequently switched between direct and indirect systems depending on the political party in power.

### Benefits of Direct Election of Mayor

- **1.** Fixed Tenure and Stability

- **2.** Direct Accountability to Citizens

- **3.** City-Wide Vision: Directly elected mayors represent the entire city, not just a single ward, reducing "parochial" ward-level politics.

- **4.** Stronger Mandate for Reform: Popular support provides the political capital needed to implement tough reforms like increased property taxes.

- **5.** Checks on Bureaucracy

- **6.** Reduced Horse-Trading: Eliminates the "resort politics" seen during indirect elections

- **7.** Faster Decision Making

- **8.** Professionalization of Politics: Attracts high-profile candidates who can run a city like a CEO.

- **9.** Enhanced City Branding: helps in attracting global investments and sister-city partnerships.
