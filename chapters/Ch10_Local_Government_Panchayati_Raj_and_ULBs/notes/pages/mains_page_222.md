### Way Forward

- **1.** Strict Adherence to "Kishan Singh Tomar" Case: Enforcement of the SC ruling that administrative excuses like delimitation are not valid for delaying polls.

- **2.** Permanent OBC Commission to avoid last-minute "Triple Test" hurdles before every election.

- **3.** Financial Independence of SECs

- **4.** Explore the possibility of "One Nation, One Election" extending down to the local level.

- **5.** The Finance Commission should strictly bar any "Untied Grants" to states where local body elections are overdue by more than six months. Reasons behind reluctance of states in empowering local bodies in India

- Preservation of Political Patronage: States fear that empowering local bodies will create a parallel center of power.

- Rivalry with Emerging Leadership: State-level politicians (MLAs/MPs) view strong local leaders as potential political competitors for their own legislative seats.

- States use "grant dependency" as a tool to keep local bodies politically aligned; independent local revenue would break this leverage.

- Bureaucratic Comfort with Centralization: The state bureaucracy (IAS/PCS) prefers reporting to a single State Minister rather than being accountable to a decentralized "Mayor-in-Council."

- Unitary Bias in Governance Philosophy: Many state leaders still view "extreme localism" as a threat to state-level administrative unity and unified policy implementation. Fear of Fractionalization: States argue that local bodies are "caste-ridden" or "inefficient," using these social complexities as a pretext to deny them real power.
