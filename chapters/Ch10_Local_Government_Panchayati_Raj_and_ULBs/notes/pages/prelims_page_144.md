| FEATURE / PARAMETER | RURAL LOCAL SELF GOVERNMENT (73RD AMENDMENT) | URBAN LOCAL SELF GOVERNMENT (74TH AMENDMENT) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Devolution of powers and responsibilities** *(Contd.)* | **2.** This covers plans for economic development and social justice, including the 29 Eleventh Schedule matters.<br>**3.** Enabling, not self executing: the Schedule transfers nothing by itself.<br>**4.** Real devolution needs the 3 Fs (functions, funds, functionaries) through State law and orders; so it is uneven, and activity mapping is the main tool. | The State may also empower Wards Committees and other committees. Note: Parastatals (water and sewerage boards, development authorities, housing boards, power and transport bodies) still hold many of these functions, so Municipalities often plan for functions they do not control. |
| Eleventh Schedule (29 matters) vs Twelfth Schedule (18 matters) | **1.** Agriculture and extension; land improvement, land reforms, consolidation, soil conservation; minor irrigation, water management, watershed development.<br>**2.** Animal husbandry, dairying, poultry; fisheries; social and farm forestry; minor forest produce.<br>**3.** Small scale industries including food processing; khadi, village and cottage industries.<br>**4.** Rural housing; drinking water; fuel and fodder; roads, culverts, bridges, ferries, waterways; rural electrification and non-conventional energy.<br>**5.** Poverty alleviation; education (primary, secondary, technical, vocational, adult, non-formal); libraries; culture.<br>**6.** Markets and fairs; health and sanitation (hospitals, PHCs, dispensaries); family welfare; women and child development; social welfare including the handicapped.<br>**7.** Welfare of weaker sections, especially SCs and STs; public distribution system; community assets. | **1.** Urban planning including town planning; land-use and building regulation; planning for economic and social development.<br>**2.** Roads and bridges; water supply (domestic, industrial, commercial); public health, sanitation, conservancy, solid waste management; fire services.<br>**3.** Urban forestry, environment and ecology; interests of weaker sections including the handicapped.<br>**4.** Slum improvement and upgradation; urban poverty alleviation; amenities - parks, gardens, playgrounds; cultural, educational and aesthetic aspects.<br>**5.** Burials, burial grounds, cremations, electric crematoriums; cattle pounds and prevention of cruelty to animals; slaughter houses and tanneries.<br>**6.** Vital statistics (births and deaths registration); public amenities - street lighting, parking lots, bus stops, conveniences. |
| Sources of finance and taxation | **1.** Discretionary: The State may let a Panchayat levy, collect and keep taxes, duties, tolls and fees; assign it State taxes; give grants in aid; and create Panchayat Funds (Art 243H).<br>**2.** Second ARC, five streams: Union grants on the Central FC’s advice (Art 280(3)(bb)); State devolution on the State FC’s advice; State loans and grants; Centrally Sponsored Schemes and Additional Central Assistance; own revenue.<br>**3.** Village levies: house or property tax, profession tax, land cess, vehicle tax and tolls, entertainment tax, licence fees, water rate, lighting tax, sanitation tax, education cess, fairs and festivals tax.<br>**4.** Intermediate and district Panchayats have almost no tax base; they rely on ferries, markets, a cess on stamp duty and non tax sources.<br>**5.** ARC diagnosis: weak own revenue, dependence on tied grants, and a mismatch of duties and resources. | Same principle governs. With Municipal Funds (Art 243X). A stronger own revenue base: property tax (the mainstay), advertisement and profession tax, water and sewerage charges, user and development charges, building permission fees. Octroi, once the largest municipal levy, is abolished in almost all States and subsumed in GST, increasing dependence on State transfers. |
| Finance Commissions, accounts and audit [PRELIMS 2010] | **1.** Mandatory: The Governor sets up a State Finance Commission every five years to review Panchayat finances (Art 243I).<br>**2.** Discretionary: Its composition, qualifications and selection (State law). | Same principle governs. The same SFC reviews municipal finances (Art 243Y); accounts and audit under State law (Art 243Z); the Central FC’s parallel duty (Art 280(3)(c)). |
