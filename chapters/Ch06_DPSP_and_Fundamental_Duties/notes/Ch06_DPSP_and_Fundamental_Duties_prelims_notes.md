## CHAPTER 08: DPSP AND FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES

### THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter

#### 1. REPEATS

#### A. The Three Way Comparison Frame

- Part III with Articles 12 to 35, Part IV with 36 to 51, Part IVA with 51A

- Sources, the United States for rights, Ireland for directives, the former USSR for duties

- Rights are negative limits on the State, directives are positive obligations on it

- Duties bind citizens, added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 on the Swaran Singh Committee report

#### B. Justiciability and Sanction

- Article 37, directives fundamental in governance yet not enforceable by any court [2015]

- Directives limit neither the legislative nor the executive function, they only guide [2017]

- Sanction is judicial for rights, political and moral for directives; a law against a right is void, one departing from a directive survives

#### C. What Each Part Secures

- Rights secure political democracy and the individual; directives secure socio economic democracy and the community [2015]

- Article 31C precedence settled through Kesavananda Bharati and Minerva Mills

#### D. Where a Provision Lives

- Welfare State and economic justice sit in the directives (economic justice also in the Preamble) [2013, 2015, 2020]

- Concentration of wealth offends Art 39(c), a directive, not a Fundamental Right [2021]

- Separation of judiciary (Art 50) and international peace (Art 51) are directives [2014, 2020]

- Gandhian directives: village panchayats (Art 40), cottage industries (Art 43); prohibition (Art 47) [2008, 2012]

- Schemes such as the NSAP implement directives [2010]; the 42nd Amendment added workers' participation in management (Art 43A) [2017]

#### E. Fundamental Duties

- No legislative process enforces duties and they are not correlative to legal duties, yet rights are correlative with duties [2017]

- Sovereignty, unity and integrity is a duty [2015]; voting is not [2011]. Composite culture, scientific temper and excellence are duties [2012, 2025]

#### 2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED

- 2008 to 2015: single answer location questions, which Part holds a value [2008, 2013, 2014, 2015]

- 2017 to 2025: two statement sets on justiciability and duties [2017, 2020], then a three pair "how many" set [2025]

#### 3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW

- Placement across Parts III, IV and IVA in one stem [2025]. Specific directive Articles: 39(c) concentration of wealth [2021], Art 50 separation of judiciary [2020]

| PARAMETER | FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS | DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES OF STATE POLICY | FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Part | Part III | Part IV | Part IV-A |
| Articles | Article 12 to 35 | Article 36 to 51 | Article 51A |
| Source of inspiration | Constitution of the United States. | Constitution of Ireland, which had itself borrowed from the Spanish Constitution. | Constitution of the erstwhile Soviet Union (USSR). |
| Concept | Supreme human rights recognised to keep the individual away from the oppression of a transient majority. | Social and economic policies to be pursued by the Government. | A constant reminder to citizens for observing certain basic norms while they enjoy Fundamental Rights. |
| Example | Article 21: Right to life is a Fundamental Right. | Article 42: Just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief. | Article 51A(a): To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem. |
| Type of democracy secured [PRELIMS 2015] | Political democracy. | Economic and social democracy; the Directive Principles spell out socio-economic democracy in the country. | Not a democracy-securing device; a civic obligation supporting both. |
| Nature | Negative, as they prohibit the State from doing certain things. | Positive, as they require the State to do certain things. | Mixed: partly positive obligations (to develop the scientific temper, to strive towards excellence) and partly prohibitions (to abjure violence, to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women). |
| Whose welfare | Promote the welfare of the individual; hence | Promote the welfare of the community; hence societarian and socialistic. | Regulate the conduct of the individual citizen in the interest of the community. |
