| PARAMETER | FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS | DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES OF STATE POLICY | FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Whose welfare** *(Contd.)* | personal and individualistic. | — | — |
| Enforcement [PRELIMS 2017] | Justiciable: legally enforceable by the courts in case of violation. | Non-justiciable: not legally enforceable by the courts for their violation. | **1.** Non-justiciable. Part IV-A provides no legal sanction and no machinery for direct enforcement: a citizen cannot be punished by a court merely for breaching Article 51A, and no writ lies to enforce a duty.<br>**2.** The duties are not directly enforceable; they work indirectly:<br>**(a)** Parliament can enforce a duty through ordinary law, for example the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971.<br>**(b)** Courts use Art 51A to interpret ambiguous statutes and to judge the reasonableness of restrictions on Fundamental Rights. |
| Sanction behind them | Legal sanctions. | Moral and political sanctions: public opinion, the electorate and the opposition. | Moral and civic sanctions, backed indirectly by pre-existing statutes. |
| Need for legislation | They are automatically enforced; no legislation required. | Require legislation for their implementation; they are not automatically enforced. | Require ordinary legislation to acquire teeth; Article 51A by itself creates no offence. |
| Effect on a conflicting law | The courts are bound to declare a law violative of any Fundamental Right unconstitutional and invalid. | The courts cannot declare a law unconstitutional merely for violating a Directive Principle. They can, however, uphold the validity of a law on the ground that it was enacted to give effect to a directive. | No law can be struck down for conflicting with a Fundamental Duty; the duties serve as an aid to interpretation. |
| Whom they bind | Addressed to the State as defined in Article 12; several rights are available to any person, citizen or foreigner. | Addressed to the State only, Article 36 adopting the same definition as Article 12. Part IV creates no obligation on private persons and confers no right on any citizen to compel the State to act. Its beneficiary is the community, its addressee the State in all its organs. | Addressed only to citizens. Non-citizens and foreigners owe no Fundamental Duties, in contrast with certain Fundamental Rights available to any person. The duties bind the individual, not the State. |
