## Chapter 6: Directive Principles Of State Policy

DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES OF STATE POLICY

- **1.** Discuss the possible factors that inhibit India from enacting for its citizens a uniform civil code as provided for in the Directive Principles of State Policy. (2015) Directive Principles of State Policy are enumerated in Part IV of the Constitution from Articles 36 to 51. Dr B.R. Ambedkar described these principles as 'novel features' of the Indian Constitution. Granville Austin has described the Directive Principles and the Fundamental Rights as the 'Conscience of the Constitution'. It promotes the principles of social and economic democracy and envisages establishing a welfare state. Directive Principles together with the Fundamental Rights contain the "philosophy of the Constitution" and are known as the "soul of the Constitution".

### Reason why DPSP are non-justiciable

- **1.** Fiscal Constraints - Eg. Providing a universal "Right to Work" (Article 41) would have bankrupted the state treasury in 1950.

- **2.** Administrative Limitations - The state machinery was initially insufficient to manage complex social reforms like universal education or health infrastructure.

- **3.** Vast geographical and social diversity made uniform application of certain principles difficult and potentially socially disruptive. Eg. "Uniform Civil Code" (Article 44)

- **4.** Political Sanction vs. Judicial Sanction - Dr. Ambedkar argued that the real sanction behind DPSPs is "public opinion" and the "ballot box," not the courtroom.

- **5.** Flexibility for Governance - Non-justiciability allows the government to prioritize goals based on the prevailing socio-economic climate and urgent national needs.

- **6.** Potential for Institutional Conflict - Making DPSPs justiciable would have led to constant friction between the Judiciary and the Executive over policy-making priorities.

- **7.** Evolutionary Nature of Rights - Many DPSP goals were intended to "mature" into Fundamental Rights as the nation progressed and resources grew. Eg. "Right to Education"

- **8.** DPSPs serve as a "Moral Code" for the state, guiding the conscience of legislators rather than imposing rigid legal chains

- **9.** Preventive Legal Overload on judiciary

### Amendments in DPSP

42nd Amendment Act, 1976

- **1.** Article 39(f): opportunity for children to develop in a healthy manner and protection against exploitation.

- **2.** Article 39A: Free Legal Aid to the poor

- **3.** Article 43A: steps to secure Worker Participation in Management of industries.

- **4.** Article 48A: protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests & wildlife
