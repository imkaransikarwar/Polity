### Criticism of Fundamental Duties

- **1.** Non-Justiciable: without direct judicial enforcement, they remain mere codes of conduct

- **2.** Vague Terminology: Terms like "composite culture" and "scientific temper" are subjective and difficult for commoners to understand.

- **3.** Incomplete List: Major duties like "paying taxes" or "voting" were excluded despite Swaran Singh Committee recommendations.

- **4.** Placement in Constitution: Placed in Part IV-A (after DPSP) instead of Part III, reducing their perceived importance.

- **5.** Potential for State Misuse: Governments may use "Duty toward the State" to suppress legitimate dissent or free speech.

- **6.** Historical Baggage: Their introduction during the Internal Emergency (1976) leads to associations with authoritarian state control.

- **7.** Lack of Awareness: Despite 50 years, a majority of the population is unaware of the specific 11 duties.

### Important Judgments

| Javed v State of Haryana 2003 | Supported a law limiting the number of children one can have for eligibility in local elections. |

### Enforceability of Fundamental Duties

Arguments in Favor (Pro-Enforceability)

- **1.** Deterrence Against Vandalism: Making safeguarding public property enforceable would provide a direct legal basis to recover damages from rioters and vandals.

- **2.** Balancing Rights and Responsibility: It prevents the "Rights-Only" culture. If citizens benefit from state-provided rights, they must be legally accountable for their civic contributions.

- **3.** National Integration: Legally mandating respect for national symbols and the promotion of brotherhood could act as a check on rising communal and divisive tendencies.

- **4.** Effective Policy Implementation: It would provide the State with a stronger constitutional shield when passing laws that restrict certain freedoms for the sake of "public duty."
