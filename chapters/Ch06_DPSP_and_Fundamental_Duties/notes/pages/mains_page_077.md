### Conflict Between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles of State

### Policy

- **1.** Champakam Dorairajan Case (1951): The SC ruled that Fundamental Rights (FR) are superior to DPSP. If they conflict, FR would prevail, and DPSP must run as a "subsidiary" to them.

- **2.** Golaknath Case (1967): Held that FRs are "sacrosanct" and cannot be amended by Parliament even to implement the Directive Principles.

- **3.** Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973): Overruled Golaknath. It held that Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution (including FRs) to implement DPSP, provided the Basic Structure is not destroyed.

- **4.** Minerva Mills Case (1980): A landmark ruling that established the "Doctrine of Harmony." The Court held that the Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between FRs and DPSPs. Neither has absolute supremacy over the other.

- **5.** Unni Krishnan Case (1993): The SC famously bridged the gap by reading Article 45 (Education) into Article 21, demonstrating that DPSP can be used to expand FRs.

- **6.** Present Position - Fundamental Rights enjoy supremacy over DPSP except for Articles 39 (b) and (c) which enjoy supremacy over Articles 14 and 19.

### India's achievements

Economic Justice & Livelihood (Articles 38, 39, 41)

- **1.** MGNREGA: Provides a legal "Right to Work" (Article 41), supporting nearly 15 crore households annually.

- **2.** Financial Inclusion (PM Jan Dhan Yojana): Brought over 55 crore citizens into the formal banking system, preventing the "concentration of wealth" (Article 39c).

- **3.** PM-KISAN: provides direct income support to 11 crore farmers (Article 38). Social Justice & Legal Aid (Article 39A, 46)

- **1.** NALSA & Lok Adalats: fulfill Article 39A by providing free legal aid. In 2025, Lok Adalats settled over 1.2 crore cases in a single day.

- **2.** Scholarships for SC/ST: In the 2025-26, over ₹7,200 crore was released to 61 lakh SC beneficiaries under post-matric scholarship schemes (Article 46).

- **3.** EWS Reservation: The 103rd Amendment (2019) ensures educational and economic interests of the "weaker sections" of the unreserved category. Grassroots Democracy (Article 40)

- **1.** Panchayati Raj: The 73rd and 74th Amendments.

- **2.** Almost all 2.5 lakh Gram Panchayats are connected via BharatNet, enabling local self-government through e-governance. Public Health & Nutrition (Article 47)

- **1.** Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY): The world's largest health insurance scheme. Covers all senior citizens aged 70+ (as of late 2025).

- **2.** National Food Security Act: Provides subsidized food grains to 80 crore people
