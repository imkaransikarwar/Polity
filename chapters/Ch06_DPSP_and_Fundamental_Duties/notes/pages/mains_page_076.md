### Significance of Directive Principles

- **1.** Welfare State: Transitions India from a colonial "police state" to a modern, welfare-oriented democracy for all citizens. Eg. Schemes like Ayushman Bharat uphold Article 47.

- **2.** Socio-Economic Democracy: ensures that political freedom is backed by social and financial security. Eg. MGNREGA fulfills the "Right to Work"(Article 41) and "Living Wage"(Article 43)

- **3.** Judicial Interpretative Tool: Eg. The Right to Digital Access was recognized as essential for fulfilling Article 38's equality mandate in Amar Jain case

- **4.** Policy Continuity: Ensures a common minimum political manifesto that remains consistent even when governments change after elections.

- **5.** Legislative Roadmap: Acts as a constitutional "to-do list" for Parliament to draft transformative laws for the marginalized. Eg. Maternity Benefit Act to fulfill Article 42.

- **6.** Economic Justice and Equity: Mandates the state to prevent the concentration of wealth and ensure fair distribution of resources.

- **7.** International Relations Guide: Eg. India's active role in UN Peacekeeping and climate pacts aligns with the mandate of Article 51.

- **8.** Environmental Stewardship: Eg. The Project Lion and Project Tiger initiatives are practical implementations of the directives in Article 48A.

- **9.** Democratic Accountability: Allows the electorate and the Opposition to evaluate a government's performance based on constitutional benchmarks.

### Criticism of Directive Principles

- **1.** Lack of Legal Force: Being non-justiciable, they are often dismissed as "mere pious wishes" or moral precepts without teeth. Eg. gender pay gap

- **2.** Illogical Arrangement: Critics like Ivor Jennings noted that DPSPs are not logically classified or based on a consistent philosophy.

- **3.** Ambiguity and Vagueness: Eg. "Adequate means of livelihood" (Article 39a) lacks a quantitative definition.

- **4.** Incompatibility with Federalism: The Centre can issue directions to states to implement DPSPs; failure may lead to "President's Rule."

- **5.** Drain on Exchequer: Eg. "Prohibition" (Article 47) as a loss of essential excise revenue.
