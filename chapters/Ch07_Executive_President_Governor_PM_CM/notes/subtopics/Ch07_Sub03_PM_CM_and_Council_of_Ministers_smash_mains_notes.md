### Prime Minister

Lord Morley described the Prime Minister as "primus inter pares" (first among equals) and "key stone of the cabinet arch". Ramsay Muir described the Prime Minister as "the steering wheel of the ship of the state."

### Powers and Functions

### With Respect to the President

- **1.** Link between the President and the Council of Ministers (Article 78).

- **2.** Advice on Appointment:

  - **a.** Advises the President on the appointment of other ministers.

  - **b.** Recommends the allocation and reshuffling of portfolios among ministers.

- **3.** Advisory Role:

  - **a.** Advises the President on summoning and proroguing of Parliament and dissolution of the Lok Sabha.

  - **b.** Advises in appointments of constitutional authorities (Eg. Attorney General, CAG, Election Commissioners, etc.).

### With Respect to the Council of Ministers

- **1.** Head of the Council: The Prime Minister is the head of the Council of Ministers and decides its agenda.

- **2.** Leader and Coordinator:

  - **a.** Guides, directs, and coordinates the activities of all ministers.

  - **b.** Can ask any minister to resign or recommend dismissal to the President.

- **3.** Chairman of Meetings: Presides over meetings of the Cabinet and influences decisions.

### With Respect to the Parliament -

Leader of the House

### Other Powers and Functions

- **1.** Crisis Leadership: Plays a central role in times of emergency or national crisis.

- **2.** Plays a key role in foreign policy decisions and international representation.

### Issues

- **1.** Concentration of Power in PMO - PMO has expanded into a "parallel government" (Subhash Kashyap). Eg. COVID-19 lockdown decisions centralised.

- **2.** Erosion of Cabinet Collective Responsibility - Eg. 1975 emergency declaration bypassed full Cabinet.

- **3.** Presidentialisation of PM - Shift towards "prime ministerial government". Eg. in foreign policy decisions.

- **4.** Marginalisation of Parliament - Eg. Farm Laws (2020) passed with limited debate.

- **5.** Coalition vs. Majority Dilemma - PM's role weakened in coalitions but over-centralised in majority regimes.

- **6.** Opacity in Decision-Making - Eg. PM CARES Fund not under parliamentary oversight.

- **7.** Institutional Weakening - PMO dominance sidelines institutions. Eg. NITI Aayog reduced to advisory role.

### Way Forward

- **1.** Introduce a PM's Question Hour (UK Model) to improve direct accountability.

- **2.** Restoring Collective Cabinet Responsibility - Define clear guidelines to prevent the PMO from bypassing ministers and functioning as a super-executive.

- **3.** Internal Party Democracy: PMs must emerge from transparent intra-party elections.

- **4.** Bring PM CARES under RTI: Ensure financial transparency.

- **5.** Appointment of key constitutional officers (CAG, CEC, etc.) should follow the collegium model involving CJI, Leader of Opposition, and PM.

- **6.** Codification of Conventions - Draw from UK's Cabinet Manual, particularly concerning PM's powers, Cabinet procedures, and PMO functioning.

### PMO

### Origin

- **1.** 1947–1977: Established as the Prime Minister's Secretariat (PMS) to provide secretarial assistance to Jawaharlal Nehru.

- **2.** 1977: Renamed the Prime Minister's Office (PMO) during the tenure of Morarji Desai to project a less "secretive" and more "office-like" image.

- **3.** Evolution: Under Lal Bahadur Shastri, the role of the Principal Secretary was created, shifting the PMO from a mere clerical body to a high-level policy advisory unit.

### Key Functions

- **1.** Secretarial Assistance: Provides the PM with technical and administrative support for managing the Union Cabinet and Parliament.

- **2.** Inter-Ministerial Coordination: resolving inter-departmental disputes and ensuring policy synergy.

- **3.** Policy Monitoring: Tracks the progress of "big-ticket" infrastructure projects (via the Project Monitoring Group) and flagship schemes like Gati Shakti.

- **4.** National Security & Foreign Policy: Houses the National Security Advisor (NSA) and coordinates with intelligence agencies and the Ministry of External Affairs.

- **5.** Public Grievances: Manages the CPGRAMS portal and the Prime Minister's National Relief Fund (PMNRF).

### Issues with Cabinet System

- **1.** Top-Down approach in decision making: overshadows other ministers, limiting diverse inputs in policy-making.

- **2.** Lack of Transparency: Cabinet meetings are confidential, reducing transparency about decision-making processes.

- **3.** Concentration of Power in the Prime Minister - Reduces the Cabinet to a rubber stamp in strong majority governments.

- **4.** Erosion of Collective Responsibility - Cabinet decisions are often shaped by a small inner circle or PMO, not through broad ministerial deliberation.

- **5.** The Prime Minister's Office (PMO) functions as a parallel power center, weakening Cabinet committees and inter-ministerial consultation.

- **6.** Coalition Compulsions and Policy Paralysis due to political bargaining, compromises, and external pressures.

- **7.** Limited Parliamentary Oversight - Eg. Money Bills and Ordinances are often passed without serious discussion, sidestepping parliamentary scrutiny.

- **8.** Administrative Overload: Eg. In 2026, over 40% of Cabinet agenda items were reported to be "procedural" rather than policy-oriented.

- **9.** The "Kitchen Cabinet" Phenomenon: Crucial decisions are often made by a small "inner circle" of 2-3 ministers, sidelining the larger Cabinet.

### Way Forward to Strengthen the System

- **1.** Formalizing Consultation: Adopting the Punchhi Commission's recommendation for mandatory state-level feedback via the Inter-State Council.

- **2.** Empowering Ministry-level Research: Each Cabinet Minister should have a "Policy Unit" of domain experts to reduce dependence on the PMO.

- **3.** Strict Parliamentary Sittings: Increase the number of mandatory Parliament days to 100+ annually to ensure Cabinet accountability.

- **4.** Shadow Cabinet Tradition: Encourage the opposition to form a "Shadow Cabinet" to provide constructive alternatives and rigorous scrutiny. Eg. in UK/Australia

- **5.** Reforming the Rules of Business: Update the 1961 Rules to clearly define the boundaries between PMO coordination and Cabinet authority.

### Constitution (One Hundred and Thirtieth Amendment) Bill, 2025

The primary aim of the Bill is to uphold "constitutional morality" by preventing the PM, CM, and Ministers from continuing in office while in judicial or police custody for an extended period.

### Key Provisions

- **1.** If a Minister is arrested and remains in custody for 30 consecutive days on serious criminal charges (offenses punishable by 5 years or more), they will automatically lose their office.

- **2.** Reinstatement Clause: If the leader is later granted bail or cleared through the legal process, they may resume their position.

- **3.** Universal Application: Unlike previous historical attempts (like the 39th Amendment) which sought to shield high offices, this Bill specifically includes the PM within its purview.

- **4.** Two related bills, the Union Territories (Amendment) Bill, 2025, and the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill, 2025, were introduced simultaneously to apply these rules to UTs like Delhi, Puducherry, and J&K.

### Arguments In Favour

- **1.** Constitutional Morality - Prevents leaders from continuing in power while facing serious criminal charges

- **2.** Good Governance - Removes "governance from jail" anomaly & ensures executive accountability.

- **3.** Public Trust - Strong stance against criminalisation of politics enhances institutional trust.

- **4.** Bridges Legal Gap - RPA, 1951 disqualifies only post-conviction; this Bill addresses interim pre-conviction situations.

- **5.** Aligns with International Best Practices - UK Ministerial Code, South African Constitution (Section 96) - public officials facing serious charges step aside.

- **6.** Addresses Lily Thomas (2013), Manoj Narula (2014) Spirit - SC has long called for stricter standards for Ministers.

- **7.** Anti-Corruption Push - Highlights zero tolerance against corruption in high offices.

- **8.** As per HM Amit Shah, it is an "insult to democracy" for secretaries and officials to take orders from a jailed leader.

### Arguments Against

- **1.** Arrest vs. Conviction: violates the principle of "innocent until proven guilty" by imposing a severe penalty based on mere arrest/detention rather than a court conviction.

- **2.** Legal experts suggest the Bill might be challenged for violating the "Rule of Law" and the "Equality Code" (Article 14).

- **3.** Weakens PM's Constitutional Prerogative: In Manoj Narula v. UoI (2014), SC held selection of Ministers is "constitutional prerogative" of PM/CM. The Bill limits this discretion.

- **4.** Unequal Standards - Creates asymmetry: MPs/MLAs removed only on conviction, but Ministers on detention.

- **5.** Parliamentary Confidence Eroded: Displaces legislative majority principle under Articles 75 & 164 with police/judicial discretion.

- **6.** Governance Destabilised: Cycles of resignation and reinstatement weaken stability and fracture coalitions.

- **7.** Opportunism Encouraged: Enables ruling parties to weaponise detentions to eliminate rivals.

- **8.** Authoritarian Tilt: Empowers law enforcement over Parliament, reflecting abusive constitutionalism.

- **9.** "Revolving Door" Problem: The provision allowing reappointment after release from custody could lead to cycles of resignation and reinstatement

### Way Forward

- **1.** Address Presumption of Innocence - Consider triggering removal at charge-framing stage (Law Commission 244th Report) instead of mere arrest.

- **2.** Strengthen independence of ED, CBI through institutional reforms to remove fear of political weaponisation.

- **3.** Time-Bound Trials - Fast-track courts for politicians to conclude trials within 1 year to avoid prolonged uncertainty.

- **4.** Mandatory judicial review of detention before automatic removal.
