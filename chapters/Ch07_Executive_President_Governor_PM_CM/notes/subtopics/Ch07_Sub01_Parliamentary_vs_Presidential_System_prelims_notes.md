## CHAPTER 03: PARLIAMENTARY VS PRESIDENTIAL

### THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter

#### 1. REPEATS

#### A. The Core Distinction

- Parliamentary government means the executive is responsible to the legislature and removable by it [2020].

- India qualifies because the Council of Ministers answers to the Lok Sabha [2015].

- The stated advantage is continued responsibility, not independence of organs [2017].

- The Cabinet form rests on collective responsibility through parliamentary democracy [2017].

#### B. Britain Compared with India

- The British Parliament is sovereign; Indian law making is limited by the Constitution [2021].

- Amendment validity goes to a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court [2021].

- India has an elected President and a written Constitution; Britain has a hereditary monarch.

- India has no genuine shadow Cabinet and no legal responsibility by countersignature.

#### C. The Presidential Model and Borrowings

- One directly elected office combines Head of State and Head of Government.

- That executive sits outside the legislature, holds fixed tenure and faces no dissolution.

- Removal is only by impeachment; ministers answer to the head, not the legislature.

- India took Fundamental Rights, judicial review and impeachment from the US Constitution.

#### 2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED

- 2015 to 2020: single answer definitions of parliamentary and Cabinet government [2015, 2017, 2020]

- 2021: a two statement comparison of the British and Indian models [2021]

#### 3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW

- No question since 2021; the live angle is comparison, parliamentary sovereignty in Britain against limited legislative power in India [2021]

#### 1. CORE DISTINCTION

The dividing line is the relationship between the executive and the legislature. In the parliamentary system the executive is drawn from and answerable to the legislature; in the presidential system the two are separately elected and mutually independent.

| PARAMETER | PARLIAMENTARY SYSTEM | PRESIDENTIAL SYSTEM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive | - Dual: nominal head plus real head. In India the President is the nominal executive, yet the executive power of the Union is vested in him by Art 53. | - Single: one person is Head of State and Head of Government. |
| Basis of office | - Confidence of the lower house | - Direct or quasi direct election for a fixed term |

#### 2. INDIA VERSUS THE USA

| PARAMETER | INDIA (PARLIAMENTARY) | USA (PRESIDENTIAL) | TRAP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Head of State and Head of Government | — | — | — |
| Head of Government | **1.** The President is only the Head of State, the nominal executive. The Prime Minister is the Head of Government and the real executive.<br>**2.** The President exercises his functions on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister, and that advice is binding (Art 74(1)).<br>**3.** The Prime Minister holds office only so long as he commands the confidence of the Lok Sabha. | **1.** The President is both Head of State and Head of Government. There is no Prime Minister.<br>**2.** The whole executive power is vested in him personally, and the Secretaries are only his advisers.<br>**3.** He is not answerable to Congress and cannot be voted out; only impeachment removes him. | The counterpart of the US President is not the Indian President but the Prime Minister, for everything to do with governing. Matching the two Presidents is the standard distractor. |
| Election of the executive | **1.** Indirect. An electoral college of the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies, including Delhi and Puducherry.<br>**2.** Proportional representation by single transferable vote, by secret ballot (Arts 54 and 55).<br>**3.** Nominated members do not vote. | **1.** Indirect in form, popular in substance. An Electoral College of 538 electors, allotted to the States, chosen on a popular vote; 270 are needed to win.<br>**2.** A candidate can win the Electoral College while losing the national popular vote. | Nominated members are excluded from the Indian presidential electoral college but do vote in the Vice Presidential election. |
| Term and re-election of the head | **1.** Five years, and re-election is unlimited; the Constitution sets no cap.<br>**2.** Eligible for re-election under Art 57. | **1.** Four years, with a hard cap of two terms under the 22nd Amendment, 1951. | India has no two term limit for the President. |
| Removal of the head | **1.** Impeachment under Art 61, on the sole ground of violation of the Constitution.<br>**2.** Either House may initiate; the charge needs two thirds of the total membership of that House, and the other House investigates and must also pass it by two thirds of its total membership.<br>**3.** Nominated members participate, since it is not an election. | **1.** Impeachment for Treason, Bribery, or other high Crimes and Misdemeanors.<br>**2.** The House of Representatives impeaches by simple majority; the Senate tries and convicts by two thirds of members present.<br>**3.** The Chief Justice presides when the President is tried. | India borrowed the impeachment procedure from the USA, but narrowed the ground to violation of the Constitution alone. |
| Ministers and the Cabinet | **1.** Drawn from the legislature; a non member may serve six months only (Art 75(5)).<br>**2.** Collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Art 75(3)).<br>**3.** Capped at fifteen percent of the Lok Sabha by the 91st Amendment, 2003. | **1.** Secretaries, appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate, and answerable only to him.<br>**2.** They cannot be members of Congress; Article I, Section 6 bars holding both offices.<br>**3.** There is no collective responsibility and no cap of this kind. | Kitchen Cabinet exists in both, but the American Cabinet is an advisory body of the President, not a decision making council. |

#### 3. INDIA VERSUS BRITAIN

| PARAMETER | INDIA | BRITAIN |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head of State | Republican. The Head of State is elected | Monarchical. Hereditary position, King or Queen |
| Sovereignty [PRELIMS 2021] | Constitutional supremacy, with judicial review and the Basic Structure doctrine | Sovereignty of Parliament |
| Prime Minister's House | May sit in either House of Parliament | **1.** Sits in the House of Commons by convention, not by law.<br>**2.** The last Prime Minister from the Lords was Alec Douglas Home, 1963, who disclaimed his peerage to enter the Commons |
| Ministers | A non member may be a minister for a maximum of six months (Arts 75(5) and 164(4)) | Must be members of Parliament |
| Legal responsibility | No such system. India did not adopt countersignature | Legal responsibility of ministers, through the requirement of countersignature |
| Shadow Cabinet | No shadow cabinet in India | **1.** Shadow Cabinet is a distinct institution.<br>**2.** The opposition mirrors every cabinet portfolio |
