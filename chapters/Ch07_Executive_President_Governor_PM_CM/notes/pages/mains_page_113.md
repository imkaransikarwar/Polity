### Issues with Cabinet System

- **1.** Top-Down approach in decision making: overshadows other ministers, limiting diverse inputs in policy-making.

- **2.** Lack of Transparency: Cabinet meetings are confidential, reducing transparency about decision-making processes.

- **3.** Concentration of Power in the Prime Minister - Reduces the Cabinet to a rubber stamp in strong majority governments.

- **4.** Erosion of Collective Responsibility - Cabinet decisions are often shaped by a small inner circle or PMO, not through broad ministerial deliberation.

- **5.** The Prime Minister's Office (PMO) functions as a parallel power center, weakening Cabinet committees and inter-ministerial consultation.

- **6.** Coalition Compulsions and Policy Paralysis due to political bargaining, compromises, and external pressures.

- **7.** Limited Parliamentary Oversight - Eg. Money Bills and Ordinances are often passed without serious discussion, sidestepping parliamentary scrutiny.

- **8.** Administrative Overload: Eg. In 2026, over 40% of Cabinet agenda items were reported to be "procedural" rather than policy-oriented.

- **9.** The "Kitchen Cabinet" Phenomenon: Crucial decisions are often made by a small "inner circle" of 2-3 ministers, sidelining the larger Cabinet.

### Way Forward to Strengthen the System

- **1.** Formalizing Consultation: Adopting the Punchhi Commission's recommendation for mandatory state-level feedback via the Inter-State Council.

- **2.** Empowering Ministry-level Research: Each Cabinet Minister should have a "Policy Unit" of domain experts to reduce dependence on the PMO.

- **3.** Strict Parliamentary Sittings: Increase the number of mandatory Parliament days to 100+ annually to ensure Cabinet accountability.

- **4.** Shadow Cabinet Tradition: Encourage the opposition to form a "Shadow Cabinet" to provide constructive alternatives and rigorous scrutiny. Eg. in UK/Australia

- **5.** Reforming the Rules of Business: Update the 1961 Rules to clearly define the boundaries between PMO coordination and Cabinet authority.

### Constitution (One Hundred and Thirtieth Amendment) Bill, 2025

The primary aim of the Bill is to uphold "constitutional morality" by preventing the PM, CM, and Ministers from continuing in office while in judicial or police custody for an extended period.

### Key Provisions

- **1.** If a Minister is arrested and remains in custody for 30 consecutive days on serious criminal charges (offenses punishable by 5 years or more), they will automatically lose their office.

- **2.** Reinstatement Clause: If the leader is later granted bail or cleared through the legal process, they may resume their position.
