### Major Criticisms

- **1.** Over-Centralization of Power: Critics argue that the PMO has become a "Super-Cabinet"

- **2.** The PMO has largely eclipsed the Cabinet Secretariat, which is the traditional coordinating body under the Rules of Business.

- **3.** Parallel Bureaucracy: The office is seen as creating a "shadow government" where unelected advisors override the expertise of senior civil servants.

- **4.** Unitary Tilt in Federalism: Eg. Frequent direct meetings between the PMO and District Collectors have bypassed state Chief Secretaries.

- **5.** Opaqueness in Decision-Making: the internal deliberation process within the PMO remains shielded from RTI and public scrutiny.

- **6.** Politicization of Bureaucracy: High-profile retired bureaucrats are frequently inducted into the PMO, leading to allegations of a "committed bureaucracy."

### Way Forward

- **1.** Restoring Cabinet Supremacy: Re-empower the Cabinet Secretariat to handle routine coordination, allowing the PMO to focus strictly on the PM's personal secretarial needs.

- **2.** Formalizing Consultation: Institutionalize a "Consultation Charter" where the PMO must seek mandatory input from State Governments before intervening in state-list subjects.

- **3.** Parliamentary Oversight: Create a dedicated Standing Committee or a sub-committee under the Public Accounts Committee to specifically audit the functioning of the PMO.

### Should India Adopt a Presidential Form of Government?

Arguments in Favour of Presidential System

- **1.** Separation of Powers - presidentialism ensures an independent legislature, preventing executive dominance

- **2.** Directly Elected Executive - ensuring legitimacy from people's mandate.

- **3.** Stability of Tenure - Fixed tenure not vulnerable to coalition collapses or legislative whims.

- **4.** Cabinet of Talents - Freedom to appoint ministers from outside legislatures, bringing in experts and technocrats.

- **5.** Clear Accountability - Voters can judge an individual leader on performance in governance rather than political manoeuvring.

- **6.** Decisive Leadership - Direct election gives a strong mandate for effective governance. Shashi Tharoor's Criticism of the Parliamentary System in India

- Unqualified Legislators - The system has created a breed of legislators "largely unqualified to legislate," who contest elections only to gain executive power.

- Government Instability - Governments are dependent on fickle legislative majorities, forcing them to prioritise politics over policy or performance.

- Weak Political Parties - Parties have become "shifting alliances of selfish interests," lacking coherent ideologies.

- Coalition Compulsions - Coalition governments are obliged to cater to the lowest common denominator, diluting governance effectiveness.

- Focus on Staying in Power - Instead of governance, governments spend energy on managing alliances and survival.

### Arguments Against Presidential System

- **1.** Ambedkar in Constituent Assembly - Warned presidential system may "degenerate into dictatorship."
