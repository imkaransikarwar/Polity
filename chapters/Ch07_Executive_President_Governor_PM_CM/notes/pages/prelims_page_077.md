| Ranks of Ministers - constitutional status | **1.** The Constitution nowhere classifies Ministers into ranks.<br>**2.** Arts 74, 75 and 77 speak only of 'Ministers' and the 'Council of Ministers';<br>**3.** The ranks are creations of convention, the Warrant of Precedence and the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961.<br>**4.** Only a Cabinet Minister attends Cabinet meetings as of right.<br>**5.** A Minister of State (Independent Charge) heads a Ministry or Department with no Cabinet Minister above him but is not a member of the Cabinet, and attends only when his subject is listed.<br>**6.** A Minister of State is attached to a Cabinet Minister; 7. a Deputy Minister is attached to a Minister and is never given independent charge.<br>**8.** A Deputy Prime Minister, when appointed, is a purely political designation with no constitutional or statutory basis - he takes the ordinary Minister's oath. | **1.** Identical at the State level, with the ranks set by the Rules of Business made by the Governor under Art 166(3) on the Chief Minister's advice.<br>**2.** A Deputy Chief Minister likewise has no constitutional basis - he is sworn in as an ordinary Minister with no extra power or precedence.<br>**3.** The Supreme Court dismissed a PIL against the practice on 12 February 2024, holding that the label does not offend the Constitution. TRAP<br>**1.** The Constitution of India does not classify the ministers into four ranks.<br>**2.** A Parliamentary Secretary is not a Minister. |
| Minister who is not a member of the legislature | **1.** Art 75(5) - a Minister who for any period of six consecutive months is not a member of either House of Parliament ceases to be a Minister at the expiry of that period.<br>**2.** The rule binds the Prime Minister exactly as it binds any other Minister; 3. a non-member PM may be sworn in first and elected afterwards. | **1.** Art 164(4) - identical, for a Minister who is not a member of the State Legislature.<br>**2.** Membership of either the Assembly or the Council satisfies it in a bicameral State. |
| Cabinet Committees | **1.** Cabinet Committees are not mentioned in the Constitution.<br>**2.** They are set up by the Prime Minister under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961, framed under Art 77(3);<br>**3.** He alone decides their number, composition and terms of reference, can reconstitute them at will, and chairs the most important ones.<br>**4.** Ministers who are not members of the Cabinet may be included. | The same device exists at State level as Cabinet Sub-Committees, constituted by the Chief Minister under the Rules of Business made by the Governor under Art 166(3). |
