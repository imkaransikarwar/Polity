### SC judgment on Article 200 and 201

A Division Bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and R. Mahadevan ruled that Tamil Nadu Governor R.N. Ravi's withholding of 10 state bills was "illegal" and "erroneous." The Court clarified that the Governor's powers under Article 200 are limited and must be exercised within constitutional bounds. Invoking Article 142, the Bench held that the pending and reserved bills are to be treated as having received "deemed assent."

### Article 200 (Governor's Powers)

- **1.** Three Exclusive Options: A Governor may (i) grant assent, (ii) withhold assent and return for reconsideration, or (iii) reserve the Bill for the President. No "pocket" or absolute veto exists.

- **2.** Time‑limits Imposed:

  - **a.** Withholding or Reserving (on Council advice): within 1 month.

  - **b.** Withholding against advice: return within 3 months, with reasons.

  - **c.** Re‑passed Bills: assent immediately, within 1 month.

- **3.** Judicial Review: Governor's inaction or misuse (pocket veto) is justiciable; courts can examine arbitrariness or malafide.

### Article 201 (President's Powers)

- **1.** Three‑Month Deadline to decide on Bills reserved by Governors. Delays beyond must be accompanied by written reasons.

- **2.** No Pocket Veto: President cannot indefinitely withhold assent; "deemed assent" if unreasonably delayed.

- **3.** Proviso to Article 201 (return‑and‑repass) must not be used piecemeal to delay legislation. Once re‑passed, the President must grant or withhold assent with clear reasons.

- **4.** Judicial Review: limited scrutiny where Union interest predominates; deeper review where State primacy and Cabinet advice conflict.

### Significance of the Judgment

- **1.** Curtails Arbitrary Use of Article 200

- **2.** Prevents politicization of office of Governor and President

- **3.** Protects separation of powers - Reclaims the primacy of the legislature in lawmaking by limiting executive interference.

- **4.** Establishes Time Limits for Decision on Bills - ends the practice of "pocket veto"

- **5.** Strengthens Legislative Supremacy in States

- **6.** Concept of 'Deemed Assent' Introduced - Sets a powerful precedent for ensuring legislative continuity and constitutional efficiency.

- **7.** Promotes Constitutional Morality - Reinforces the expectation that constitutional authorities must act in good faith, and not stall democratic processes.

### Major Issues in the Judgment

- **1.** Judicial Overreach -Eg. legal doctrine of deemed assent not explicitly found in Constitution

- **2.** Ambiguity - Unclear how "deemed assent" will be operationalized or enforced

- **3.** The judgment reaffirms limited discretion for Governors, but doesn't fully define the scope of "constitutional discretion" under Article 200.

- **4.** One-Size-Fits-All Federal Application - does not account for varying political, administrative, or legal complexities across different regions.
