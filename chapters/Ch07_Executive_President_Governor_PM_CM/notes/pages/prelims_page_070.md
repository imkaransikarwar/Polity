| Personal Immunities [PRELIMS 2025,2018] | **1.** Art 361(1) - the President is not answerable to any court for the exercise and performance of the powers and duties of his office, or for any act done in the exercise of those powers.<br>**2.** Proviso: his conduct may be reviewed by a court/tribunal appointed to investigate a charge in impeachment proceedings.<br>**3.** Art 361(2) - no criminal proceedings whatsoever shall be instituted or continued against him during his term.<br>**4.** Art 361(3) - no process for his arrest or imprisonment shall be issued during his term.<br>**5.** Art 361(4) - civil proceedings for personal acts allowed during the term only after 2 months' prior written notice.<br>**6.** Immunity is personal 7. official acts of the Government remain judicially reviewable.<br>**8.** Emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during his term. | **1.** Identical protection - Art 361 covers the Governor (and Rajpramukh) in the same terms: not answerable to any court, no criminal proceedings instituted or continued during the term of office, no arrest/imprisonment process, civil suits only after 2 months' notice.<br>**2.** Emoluments and allowances not to be diminished during his term.<br>**3.** Distinguish the separate legislative immunity of members of Parliament and of the State Legislatures: no member is liable to any court proceedings in respect of anything said or any vote given in the House or its committees. TRAP<br>**1.** Immunity protects the person of the office holder, not the office's decisions. The Supreme Court has repeatedly reviewed gubernatorial actions (e.g. Bommai, Nabam Rebia) even though the Governor himself cannot be summoned. |
| Governor as Administrator of a Union Territory [PRELIMS 2009] | **1.** Every Union Territory is administered by the President acting, to such extent as he thinks fit, through an administrator appointed by him with such designation as he may specify (Lieutenant Governor, Chief Commissioner or Administrator).<br>**2.** The administrator is an agent of the President, not a constitutional head like a Governor. | **1.** The President may appoint the Governor of a State as the administrator of an adjoining Union Territory;<br>**2.** When so appointed, the Governor exercises those functions independently of his State Council of Ministers.<br>**3.** Example: Chandigarh, administered by the Governor of Punjab holding additional charge.<br>**4.** Lakshadweep does not follow this pattern - it has its own separately appointed Administrator, not the Governor of Kerala. |
