| Ministers[PRELIMS 2022] | Council of Minister:<br>**1.** Constitutional body, dealt in detail by the Articles 74 and 75 of the Constitution.<br>**2.** 3 categories - cabinet ministers, ministers of state, and deputy ministers.<br>**3.** Does not meet as a single body, to transact everyday government business.<br>**4.** Implements the decisions taken by the cabinet. Cabinet:<br>**1.** Inserted through 44th CAA, 1978 in Article 352, which only defines the cabinet and does not describe its powers and functions.<br>**2.** Includes the cabinet ministers only.<br>**3.** Meets, as a body, frequently and take decisions regarding the transaction of government business.<br>**4.** Supervises the implementation of its decisions by the council of ministers. Kitchen Cabinet:<br>**1.** Informal body consists of the PM/CM and 2-4 influential colleagues in whom he has faith called the ‘Inner Cabinet’ or ‘Kitchen Cabinet’ has become the real centre of power.<br>**2.** Composed of not only cabinet ministers but also outsiders like friends and family.<br>**3.** Phenomenon of ‘kitchen cabinet’ is not unique to India it also exists in USA and Britain. | — |

| Creation of Ministries / Allocation of Business [PRELIMS 2021,2009] | **1.** Ministries and Departments of the Union are not listed in the Constitution.<br>**2.** Ministries are created, merged or abolished by amending the First Schedule of the Allocation of Business Rules, 1961. The President makes these rules under Art 77(3) on the PM's advice, not on the Cabinet Secretary's.<br>**3.** Each Ministry is then assigned to a Minister by the President on the PM's advice.<br>**4.** The number is elastic - about 18 ministries at Independence (15 August 1947), several times that number today.<br>**5.** The Department of Personnel was set up in June 1970 within the Cabinet Secretariat, i.e. under the Prime Minister's charge, following the first Administrative Reforms Commission (1966);<br>**6.** It later moved out and is today the DoPT under the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions - a portfolio conventionally retained by the PM, along with Atomic Energy and Space. | **1.** State mirror: the Governor makes Rules of Business under Art 166(3) on the advice of the Chief Minister, creating departments and allocating them among ministers.<br>**2.** The General Administration Department (and usually Home) is conventionally kept by the Chief Minister. TRAP Art 77/166 rules are rules of business, not law making. Breaking the Allocation of Business Rules does not by itself invalidate government action, because Art 77(2)/166(2) bars any inquiry into whether an order authenticated in the President's or Governor's name was actually made by him. |
