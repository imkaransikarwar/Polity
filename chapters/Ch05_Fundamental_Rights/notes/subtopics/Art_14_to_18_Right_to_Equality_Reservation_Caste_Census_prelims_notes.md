PART III - FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS: GENERAL FEATURES Nature and Scope

### CORE PROVISIONS

- **1.** Contained in Part III of the Constitution; described as the Magna Carta of the Indian Constitution.

- **2.** Place limits on the tyranny of the Executive and on arbitrary laws of the Legislature.

- **3.** Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights.

---

### [Prelims Source Page 49](../../pages/prelims/prelims_page_049.pdf)

- **4.** Not absolute; subject to reasonable restrictions.

- **5.** Important for the overall development of citizens. EXCEPTIONS / LAWS / JUDICIAL INTERPRETATION

- **1.** While Articles 17, 20, and 24 act as strict limitations that Parliament cannot override (though it must legislate to enforce them), Articles 15 and 18 are not absolute because the Constitution explicitly permits the legislature to create exceptions like reservations, military titles, and academic honors.

- **2.** Available to Indian citizens only: Art 15, 16, 19, 29, 30.

- **3.** Available to all residents of India, both citizens and foreigners: Art 14, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28.

- **4.** Negatively worded as prohibitions on the State: Art 14, 15(1), 16(2), 18(1), 20, 22(1), 28(1).

- **5.** Rights outside Part III: (a) Art 265: no tax except by authority of law. (b) Art 300A: property, a constitutional and legal right after the 44th Amendment Act, 1978. (c) Art 301: freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse. DEFINITION AND SCOPE (ART 12-13)

### Article 12 - Definition of 'State'

### CORE PROVISIONS

- **1.** Defines State for the purposes of Part III, covering four categories.

- **2.** Government and Parliament of India, i.e. the executive and legislature of the Union.

- **3.** Government and legislature of each State.

- **4.** All local authorities within the territory of India: municipalities, panchayats, district boards, improvement trusts.

- **5.** All other authorities within the territory of India or under the control of the Government of India: bodies such as LIC, ONGC, SAIL. EXCEPTIONS / LAWS / JUDICIAL INTERPRETATION

- **1.** As per the Supreme Court, a private body or agency working as an instrument of the State falls within the meaning of State under Article 12.

### Article 13 - Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights [PRELIMS 2026]

### CORE PROVISIONS

- **1.** All laws that are inconsistent with, or in derogation of, any of the Fundamental Rights shall be void.

- **2.** Article 13 does not include a Constitutional Amendment within the meaning of law. EXCEPTIONS / LAWS / JUDICIAL INTERPRETATION 'Law' under Article 13 includes:

- **1.** Permanent and temporary laws made by Parliament, and Ordinances.

- **2.** Delegated legislation: order, bye-law, rule, regulation or notification.

- **3.** Non-legislative sources of law, that is custom or usage having the force of law. RIGHT TO EQUALITY (ART 14-18)

### Article 14 - Equality before law [PRELIMS 2021]

### CORE PROVISIONS

- **1.** The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.

- **2.** Available to all persons, citizens as well as foreigners, and to legal persons. EXCEPTIONS / LAWS / JUDICIAL INTERPRETATION Five exceptions:

- **1.** Article 31C: laws made by the State for implementing the Directive Principles in Art 39(b) or 39(c) cannot be challenged on the ground that they violate Article 14. 'Where Article 31-C comes in, Article 14 goes out', the Supreme Court ruled.

---

- **2.** Article 361: protection of the President, Governors and Rajpramukhs - 361(1) not answerable to any court for performance of their duties; 361(2) no criminal proceeding; 361(3) no arrest; 361(4) no civil proceeding.

- **3.** Article 361A: no person liable for civil or criminal proceedings in respect of publication in a newspaper of a substantially true report of proceedings of Parliament or a State legislature.

- **4.** Article 105: parliamentary privileges of Members of Parliament.

- **5.** Article 194: privileges of members of State legislatures.

### Article 15 - Prohibition of discrimination

### CORE PROVISIONS

- **1.** The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on five grounds: (1) religion, (2) race, (3) caste, (4) sex, (5) place of birth.

- **2.** The provision prohibits discrimination both by the State and by private individuals.

- **3.** Articles 15(3) and 15(4) are the foundational bricks of reservation in the country. EXCEPTIONS / LAWS / JUDICIAL INTERPRETATION Laws passed:

- **1.** Reservation for OBCs in educational institutions: Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006.

- **2.** It provides 27% reservation for OBCs in all central higher educational institutions, including the IITs and IIMs.

- **3.** Reservation for EWS in educational institutions: the 103rd Amendment Act, 2019 inserted Article 15(6), and the Central Government issued an order in 2019 providing 10% reservation to EWS in admission to educational institutions.

### Article 16 - Equality of opportunity in public employment [PRELIMS 2023]

### CORE PROVISIONS

- **1.** Equality of opportunity for all citizens in matters of employment or appointment to any office under the State.

- **2.** No discrimination on seven grounds: (1) religion, (2) race, (3) caste, (4) sex, (5) descent, (6) place of birth, (7) residence. EXCEPTIONS / LAWS / JUDICIAL INTERPRETATION Four exceptions:

- **1.** Article 16(3): residence within a State may be prescribed as a qualification for appointment to a government post.

- **2.** Article 16(4): reservation of posts in government jobs in favour of any backward class.

- **3.** Article 16(5) : creates an exception to the rule of equal opportunity by allowing the state to make a law that the head or governing members of a religious or denominational institution must belong to that specific religion or denomination.

- **4.** Article 16(6): the State may reserve up to 10% of appointments in favour of any economically weaker section of citizens.

### Article 17 - Abolition of untouchability [PRELIMS 2020]

### CORE PROVISIONS

- **1.** Abolishes “untouchability” and forbids its practice in any form.

- **2.** Enforcing any disability arising out of untouchability is an offence punishable by law.

- **3.** It is an absolute right with no exceptions, and it is available against private individuals as well. The State has a constitutional obligation to take necessary action so the right is not violated (PUDR v. Union of India, 1982).

- **4.** It does not cover the social boycott of a few individuals or their exclusion from religious services, unless that exclusion rests on caste or untouchability.

- **5.** Neither the Constitution nor the Act defines “untouchability”.

- **6.** Under Art 35, only Parliament (not State legislatures) can make laws prescribing punishment for untouchability. EXCEPTIONS / LAWS / JUDICIAL INTERPRETATION Laws passed:

- **1.** Parliament enacted the Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955.

- **2.** In 1976 it was made more stringent and renamed the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955. Judicial interpretation:

- **3.** Mysore High Court: the subject matter of Article 17 is not untouchability in the literal or grammatical sense, but the practice as it developed historically in the country, involving social disabilities imposed on certain classes.

### Article 18 - Abolition of titles

### CORE PROVISIONS

- **1.** Prohibits the State from conferring any title, except a military or academic distinction, on anybody, whether a citizen or a foreigner.

- **2.** Prohibits a citizen of India from accepting any title from any foreign state. EXCEPTIONS / LAWS / JUDICIAL INTERPRETATION Supreme Court judgment:

- **1.** Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India, 1996: the Supreme Court upheld the validity of the National Awards - Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan and Padma Shri.

- **2.** The theory of equality does not mandate that merit should not be recognised.

- **3.** These awards do not amount to titles within the meaning of Article 18, which prohibits only hereditary titles of nobility.

- **4.** They should not be used as suffixes or prefixes to the names of awardees. RIGHT TO FREEDOM (ART 19-22)

### Article 19 - Right to Freedom

### CORE PROVISIONS

- **1.** Guarantees six freedoms to citizens; originally seven.

- **2.** Article 19(1)(f), the right to acquire, hold and dispose of property, was omitted by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978.

- **3.** Available to citizens only, not to foreigners or legal persons such as companies. EXCEPTIONS / LAWS / JUDICIAL INTERPRETATION

- **1.** Each freedom is subject to reasonable restrictions specified in Articles 19(2) to 19(6).

- **2.** See the freedom-wise breakdown below.
