### Limitations and Criticism

- **1.** Not Absolute - subject to reasonable restrictions for public order, morality, and security. Eg. Internet shutdowns in Kashmir.

- **2.** Limited Nature - Do not include social and economic rights necessary for a free and quality life. Eg. Right to employment, right to social security.

- **3.** Suspension during Emergency under Art. 359. (except Articles 20 & 21).

- **4.** Amendable Nature - Eg. 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978 removed Right to Property.

- **5.** Accessing justice under Art. 32 or 226 is a challenge for poor or illiterate citizens.

- **6.** The scope of FRs depends heavily on judicial interpretation. Eg. Expansion of Art. 21 (from "procedure established by law" in A.K. Gopalan, 1950 to "just, fair, reasonable" in Maneka Gandhi, 1978).

- **7.** Conflict with Directive Principles - Eg. Champakam Dorairajan v. State of Madras (1951) struck down caste-based reservations as violating Art. 15, later addressed by the First Amendment (1951).

- **8.** Misuse of Restrictions - Vague grounds like "public order" or "decency" allow overreach.

### Role of Judiciary

Guardian of Fundamental Rights - Judiciary acts as the protector and guarantor of FRs under Art. 32 & 226. Eg. Ambedkar called Art. 32 the "heart and soul" of the Constitution. Judicial Review - Ensures that laws and executive actions do not violate FRs. Eg. Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) - Parliament cannot amend FRs to destroy the Basic Structure. Expansive Interpretation

- **1.** Eg. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978) - Due process made part of personal liberty.

- **2.** Eg. K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017) - Recognised Right to Privacy. Creation of New Rights - Judicial activism has expanded the scope of FRs.

- **2.** Eg. Right to Clean Environment (Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar, 1991).

- **2.** Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018) - Decriminalised homosexuality (Sec. 377 IPC). ### Critical analysis of the role of judiciary

- **1.** Judicial Overreach - Courts intrude into policy-making, raising separation of powers concerns. Eg. SC ban on firecrackers (2017), SC's regulation of BS-VI fuel deadlines (2018).

- **2.** Inherent Judicial Subjectivity - Eg. Doctrine of Basic Structure - Varied interpretations affecting predictability.

- **3.** Accessibility Barriers - Eg. NALSA reports (2023) show over 70% rural poor lack effective legal aid.
