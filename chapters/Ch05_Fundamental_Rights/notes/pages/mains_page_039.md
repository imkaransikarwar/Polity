### Challenges

- **1.** Politicization of caste and castecization of politics undermines the original intent of reservation policies. Eg. reservation demands by dominant communities like Patels, Marathas, etc.

- **2.** Strengthening Caste Identity: The redistributive justice based on caste reinforces caste identities and poses challenges to establishing a casteless egalitarian society.

- **3.** Stigmatization and Stereotyping: Individuals from reserved categories face discrimination or be perceived as beneficiaries of preferential treatment.

- **4.** Creamy Layer: The absence of a creamy layer for SC and ST and the higher creamy layer limit for OBCs can lead to a 'class within caste'. Eg. As per Justice G. Rohini Commission's report, 97% of central OBC quota benefits go to just under 25% of its castes.

- **5.** Reservation in Promotions: lead to inefficiency and compromise the merit-based selection process, affecting overall productivity and performance in government institutions.

- **6.** Dependency on Reservations: Over-reliance on reservation policies discourage investment in holistic measures for socio-economic development such as health and education.

### SC Judgments

| Case | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Indra Sawhney v. UoI (1992) | Upheld reservations for OBCs but capped them at 50%. |
| M. Nagaraj & Others v. Union of India (2006) | Upheld reservations in promotions for SC/STs under specific conditions: backwardness, inadequate representation, and administrative efficiency. |
| Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta (2018) | States are not required to provide quantifiable data to justify reservations in promotions, reversing M. Nagaraj. |
| Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil v. Chief Minister (2021) | Struck down Maratha Reservation law for exceeding the 50% cap; upheld Indra Sawhney and the 102nd Constitutional Amendment. |

### Reservation in promotions

- **1.** As per 16 (4A), the state can make provision for reservation in matters of promotion, with consequential seniority, in favor of Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes which in the opinion of the State are not adequately represented in the services under the State.

- **2.** Among the 275 joint secretaries, 13 (4.73 per cent) are SCs, nine (3.27 per cent) are STs and 19 belong to the OBC category. Of 89 secretaries, there are just 3 STs, 1 Dalit and no OBCs.

### Benefits

- **1.** Addressing Structural Discrimination and systemic barriers that hinder the upward mobility of marginalized groups in the workforce. Eg. In Group-A, the highest tier among these, the representation of SCs is just 12.86%, of STs 5.64% and of OBCs 16.88%. Reservation for these communities is 15%, 7.5% and 27% respectively.

- **2.** Role Model Effect within marginalized communities.

- **3.** Encouraging Equal Opportunity and Building a Representative Workforce
