### Right to Property in Other Countries

- **1.** USA: Protected by the 5th Amendment, which states that private property shall not be taken for public use without "just compensation."

- **2.** Australia: The government uses "Conclusive Titling" (Torrens Title), meaning the State guarantees ownership, effectively eliminating title-related litigation.

### Article 32, 226

"It is the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it." Dr Ambedkar

- **1.** Article 32 and 226: Guarantees the right to move the SC and HC respectively for the enforcement of FR. Empowers the SC to issue directions, orders & writs

- **2.** 32(3): Allows Parliament to empower any other court to exercise these powers within their local limits.

- **3.** 32(4): States that the right to move the SC shall not be suspended except during a National Emergency.

### WRITS

- **1.** Habeas Corpus (To have the body): Used to release a person who has been detained or imprisoned unlawfully. Eg. ADM Jabalpur Case

- **2.** Mandamus (We command): Issued to a public official, corporation, or lower court to perform a mandatory duty they have failed or refused to do. Eg. In Gujarat State Financial Corp v. Lotus Hotels (1983), SC issued a writ of Mandamus to provide loan to a hotel,

- **3.** Prohibition (To forbid): Issued by a higher court to a lower court or quasi-judicial body to stop them from continuing a case that exceeds their jurisdiction.

- **4.** Certiorari (To be certified): Issued to quash or overturn an order already passed by a lower court or authority because it lacked jurisdiction or contained a legal error. Eg. in A.K. Kraipak case, SC quashed a selection list for the Forest Service as one of the members of the selection board was also a candidate.

- **5.** Quo Warranto (By what authority): Issued to prevent a person from illegally holding a public office and to inquire into the legality of their claim to that office.

### Comparison between SC & HC Powers

| Feature | Supreme Court (Article 32) | High Court (Article 226) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature of Right | The SC cannot refuse to exercise this power. | Discretionary Power. The HC may refuse to grant relief if an alternative remedy exists. |
| Scope of Power | Narrower: Can only be invoked for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights (Part III). | Wider: Can be invoked for Fundamental Rights plus ordinary legal rights. |
