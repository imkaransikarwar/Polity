### Key Provisions in State Laws

- **1.** Mandatory prior permission or intimation to district magistrate for religious conversion.

- **2.** Ban on conversion through force, fraud, inducement, or allurement.

- **3.** Prescribed penalties (1-10 years imprisonment, fines up to ₹50,000 or more).

- **4.** Special protection for women, minors, and SC/ST individuals.

- **5.** Burden of proof is often placed on the person converting or the religious leader.

### Judicial Pronouncements

- **1.** Rev. Stainislaus v. State of Madhya Pradesh (1977) - SC upheld state laws, ruling that the right to "propagate" religion does not include the right to "convert" another.

- **2.** Hadiya Case (Shafin Jahan v. Asokan K.M., 2018) - SC upheld adult woman's right to choose religion and marriage partner.

### Significance

- **1.** Reflects the balance between individual liberty and state interest in public order.

- **2.** Prevents forced, fraudulent, or induced conversions, ensuring genuine choice. Eg. The 1977 Stainislaus judgment noted that "propagate" does not include the right to convert others forcibly.

- **3.** Safeguards Vulnerable Groups - Protects women, minors, and SC/STs from exploitation.

- **4.** Regulates Conversion by Marriage - Prevents concealment or coercion in interfaith marriages.

- **5.** Protecting Public Order: Coercive conversions can trigger social unrest, communal tensions, and localized violence between different religious groups.

- **6.** Cultural Preservation: Protects the indigenous cultural and religious identity of Adivasi/tribal populations from being erased by external missionary activities.

- **7.** Curbing "Love Jihad": Prevents marriages conducted solely for the purpose of religious conversion through deception or misrepresentation.

- **8.** National Security: Concerns that mass conversions funded by foreign NGOs could be used to destabilize border regions or influence politics.

- **9.** Countering Allurement: Prevents "material inducements" like free education or healthcare from being used as bait for switching religions.

### Criticism of Anti-Conversion Laws

- **1.** Infringement of Privacy: Eg. Mandatory notification to a District Magistrate violates the "Right to Privacy" established in the Puttaswamy judgment.

- **2.** Vague Terms - Words like "allurement" and "inducement" are loosely defined, enabling misuse.

- **3.** Violation of Fundamental Rights - Curtails Article 25 (religious freedom) and Article 21 (personal liberty & privacy).

- **4.** Targeted Enforcement - Disproportionately affects minorities and interfaith couples.

- **5.** Politicisation - Often linked to identity politics and communal polarisation.

- **6.** Incentivizing Vigilantism: Laws embolden fringe groups to disrupt interfaith weddings or prayer meetings under the guise of "stopping crime."

- **7.** Burden of Proof: Reversing the burden onto the accused (the convert/priest) is inconsistent with the fundamental legal principle of "innocence until proven guilty."

### Way Forward

- **1.** Uniform Principles - Clear national guidelines to prevent misuse while safeguarding genuine freedom of choice.

- **2.** Judicial Safeguards - Ensure laws do not curtail rights under Articles 21 and 25.
