### Rights of Minorities

The Indian constitution doesn't define the term Minority. However, Article 29(the word "minorities", generally means a minority or a group within a majority community), Article 30 (speaks of religious and linguistic minorities), and Articles- 350A and 350B relate to minorities. Special Provisions for Linguistic Minorities: Article 350B provides for a Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities to investigate complaints of neglect and safeguard their linguistic rights. At present, Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Parsis, and Jains are notified as minority communities at the national level. On a global level, Parts 3 & 4 of the first article of the UN Charter and Articles 55 & Article 65 recognizes FRs and freedoms for minority groups without any discrimination based on race, religion, language, etc.

### Significance of Minority Rights

- **1.** Protects Diversity - Safeguards India's multi-religious, multi-lingual, and multi-cultural fabric by ensuring inclusion of all communities.

- **2.** Prevents Majoritarianism - Acts as a constitutional shield against domination by majority groups, preserving democratic balance.

- **3.** Educational Empowerment - Minority-run institutions under Article 30 promote literacy, higher education, and cultural continuity. Eg. St. Stephen's College, Jamia Millia Islamia.

- **4.** Cultural Preservation - Secures the language, script, and culture of minority groups under Article 29, fostering cultural pluralism.

- **5.** Promotes Social Justice - Enables disadvantaged minorities to access affirmative action and welfare schemes, reducing inequalities.

- **6.** Strengthens Democracy - Encourages political participation of minorities, ensuring their voices in representative institutions.

- **7.** Supports National Integration - Builds trust and belongingness, reducing alienation and fostering unity in diversity.

- **8.** Upholds Constitutional Morality - Reinforces justice, equality, fraternity, and the basic structure doctrine as emphasized in S.R. Bommai (1994).

### Issues with Minority Rights

| Issue | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Absence of a universally agreed-upon definition of 'minority' | Presently, linguistic minorities are identified at the state level, decided by respective state governments, while religious minorities are determined by the Central Government. |
| Discrimination and Social Exclusion | In a survey by Lokniti-CSDS, 60% of Muslims reported facing discrimination in housing. |
| Political Underrepresentation | In the 2024 Lok Sabha elections, only 24 out of 543 members (4.5%) were Muslim, compared to their 14% share of the population. |
| Educational Inequality | According to the Ministry of Minority Affairs, only 4.8% of government funds for education are allocated to minority institutions. |
