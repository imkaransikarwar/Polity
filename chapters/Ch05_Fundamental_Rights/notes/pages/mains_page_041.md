- **1.** Justice Rohini commission on OBC sub-categorization was set up by the President of India, mandated under Article 340 to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes and propose steps for their improvement. It submitted its report in October 2023.

  - **a.** 97% of the reserved jobs and seats have gone to 25% of OBC sub-castes, and only 10 OBC communities have taken the 25% of reserved central jobs and institutional seats.

  - **b.** As many as 983, or 37%t, of the 2,600 communities under the OBC had zero representation in jobs and institutes, and only 2.68% reservations have been used by 994 castes. Sub-categorisation of OBCs in states:

- 1972: Karnataka CM Devraj Urs appointed Havanur Commission, its report formed the basis for the sub-categorisation of OBCs.

- 1977: Bihar CM Karpoori Thakur implemented the Mungeri Lal Commission report to divide OBCs into two categories: extremely backward classes (EBCs), and backward classes.

- 2002: UP CM Rajnath Singh appointed Hukum Singh committee to earmark 5% OBC quota for Yadavs and 14% for Most Backward Classes

### Need for Sub-Categorization of OBCs

- **1.** Unequal Distribution of Quota Benefits - 97% of the reserved jobs and seats have gone to 25% of OBC sub-castes

- **2.** Horizontal Equity - Sub-categorization ensures fair share for most backward classes (MBCs) within OBCs, aligning with Articles 14, 15, and 16 guaranteeing equality.

- **3.** Better Targeting of Affirmative Action - Helps identify truly deprived groups for focused support in education, jobs, and welfare.

- **4.** Political Representation - Sub-categorization could improve political inclusion for marginalised OBC groups who are otherwise overshadowed by dominant castes.

- **5.** Ensuring Fair Distribution Structure - Justice Rohini Commission's recommendation to sub-categorize OBCs into three benefit bands: communities receiving no benefits, those with partial benefits, and those receiving maximum benefits.

- **6.** Scientific Resource Allocation - Sub-categorization allows the state to direct specialized welfare schemes to specific clusters of castes.

- **7.** Fulfills the mandate of Article 340, requiring the State to investigate and improve backward class conditions.

- **8.** Harmonizing State-Central Lists - Eg. Bihar and Tamil Nadu have successfully used sub-categorization for decades to balance social groups.

- **9.** Moves from "formal equality" (treating unequals as equals) to "substantive equality" (graded assistance).

### Challenges

- **1.** Absence of Current Population Data Without a completed 2027 Census, determining the exact population weight for sub-quotas remains speculative.

- **2.** Fear of political backlash from dominant OBCs leading to delay in implementing Justice Rohini report

- **3.** Identical castes use different surnames or spellings across states, leading to classification errors.

- **4.** Triggering "Competitive Backwardness" Further division might encourage even more groups to demand specialized "micro-quotas" within categories.

- **5.** Erosion of Collective Bargaining - Eg. Sociologists argue that sub-categorization is a "divide and rule" strategy to weaken the Mandal movement.
