### Criticism of Censorship in India

- **1.** Chilling Effect: Over-regulation leads to self-censorship among creators who fear legal harassment or arrest.

- **2.** Vague Terminology: Use of broad terms like "anti-national" allows for arbitrary interpretation by state agencies.

- **3.** Lack of Transparency: Many content blocking orders are not made public, preventing judicial or public review.

- **4.** Executive Overreach: The 2026 IT Rules shift the Inter-Departmental Committee's role from grievance-handling to an instrument of censorship.

- **5.** Targeting Dissent: Censorship is often perceived as a tool to silence political opponents and activists.

- **6.** Selective Enforcement: Critics argue that hate speech from pro-establishment groups is often overlooked while critical voices are suppressed. Eg. action against Kunal Kamara

- **7.** Undermining Art: Artistic freedom is often sacrificed to placate extremist groups or religious sentiments. Eg. Padmavat movie controversy or Samay Raina controversy

### Major SC Judgments on Censorship

- **1.** Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015): Struck down Section 66A of the IT Act, ruling that speech cannot be restricted merely for being "offensive."

- **2.** The Supreme Court in Ranjit D. Udeshi v. State of Maharashtra (1965) defined obscenity as a reasonable restriction to free speech (Article 19).

### Way Forward

- **1.** Judicial Oversight: Mandate that all blocking orders undergo a summary judicial review within 24 hours of implementation.

- **2.** Independent Regulator: Replace ministerial committees with a multi-stakeholder body (judiciary, civil society, and industry) for digital regulation.

- **3.** Narrow Definitions: Clearly define "national security" and "public order" to prevent administrative misuse.

- **4.** Transparency Reports: Require the government to publish bi-annual reports on all content takedown requests and their legal basis.

- **5.** Safeguarding Freelancers: Provide legal aid and exemptions for independent creators from burdensome broadcast-level compliance.

- **6.** Ethical Self-Regulation: Encourage media houses to adopt robust, voluntary fact-checking standards to reduce the "need" for state intervention.

### Global Best Practices

- **1.** The Santa Clara Principles: Global standards for transparency and accountability in content moderation by digital platforms.

- **2.** Manila Principles on Intermediary Liability: Framework to protect intermediaries from being forced to censor content without a court order.

- **3.** EU Digital Services Act (DSA): Focuses on systemic risk and transparency while providing users with clear avenues to challenge content removal.

### Way forward

- **1.** Revise censorship laws to provide clearer definitions of terms like "public order" and "morality" to minimize subjective interpretations and misuse.
