### OTT Regulation in India

OTT stands for "Over-the-Top." An OTT platform is an audio and video hosting and streaming service that delivers streaming media content directly to viewers over the internet, bypassing traditional cable, satellite, and broadcast television. Eg. Netflix, Amazon, etc. As per Ormax Media report, India has over 500 million OTT users and 150 million paid subscriptions. It also shows a growth rate of 20% showing an increasing market for OTT platforms.

### Regulatory Mechanism in India

- **1.** IT Act, 2000

  - **a.** Section 67 & 67A - deals with publication and transmission of obscene content

  - **b.** Section 69A - empowers central government to block public access

- **2.** IT rules, 2021

  - **a.** Self-Classification: OTT platforms (Netflix, Hotstar, etc.) must classify content into age categories: U, U/A 7+, U/A 13+, U/A 16+, and A.

  - **b.** Parental Locks: Platforms must provide parental locks for content rated U/A 13+ or higher.

- **3.** Section 294 of BNS - defines and criminalises obscene content

- **4.** POSCO Act - criminalises child pornography

### Need for OTT regulation

- **1.** Protection from Obscenity & Harmful Content - The SC in Ranjit Udeshi case (1965), upheld obscenity as a reasonable restriction under Article 19. Eg. India's Got Latent controversy.

- **2.** Level Playing Field - Traditional media (TV, cinema) faces strict censorship (CBFC, Cable TV Act), but OTT enjoys regulatory vacuum, leading to unfair competition.

- **3.** Consumer Rights - Need for clear mechanisms to address complaints on content classification, parental control, and offensive material.

- **4.** Protecting dignity of women - Eg. Bulli Bai app incident (auctioning of images of women)

- **5.** Child Safety & Age-Appropriate Content - Lack of effective age-rating, parental filters, and access controls exposes minors to inappropriate content.

- **6.** Data Privacy & Algorithmic Concerns - OTT platforms collect vast user data, raising issues of profiling, consent, and surveillance capitalism.

- **7.** Cultural Sensitivity - Content may offend religious, caste, or regional sentiments, sparking protests. Eg. Controversies over Tandav (2021) series.

- **8.** Preventing Monopolistic Practices - Need to regulate pricing models, market dominance, and exclusivity contracts by global giants like Netflix, Amazon Prime.
