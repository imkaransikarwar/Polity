### Essential Religious Practices

- **1.** Recently, SC constituted a 9-judge bench to review the 2018 Sabarimala verdict focusing on the scope of religious freedom (Article 25) versus denominational rights (Article 26).

- **2.** Thiruparankundram Deepathoon Dispute: Madras HC warned officials of contempt for obstructing the Karthigai Deepam ritual atop the hill. It ruled that the state cannot use "law and order" concerns as a pretext to block long-standing religious rituals.

- **3.** Thenkalai Sect Rights: Reaffirming a century-old legal position, the Court ruled that only the Thenkalai sect has the exclusive right to recite specific hymns (Mantrams/Prabandhams) at the Kancheepuram Varadaraja Perumal Temple. It held that an individual's right to worship (Article 25) does not override the established denominational and ritual office rights of a sect protected under Article 26.

### Major Judgments of Judiciary Involvement

- **1.** Shirur Mutt Case(1954): Established the Essential Religious Practices (ERP) test, protecting essential or core rituals while allowing State regulation of secular temple administration.

- **2.** Durgah Committee Case (1961): Ruled that "superstitious beliefs" or "unessential accretions" are not protected under Article 26.

- **3.** Sastri Yagnapurushadji (1966): Defined the scope of "Hinduism" to include diverse sects, preventing them from using "denominational" status to exclude others from temples.

- **4.** Seshammal Case (1972): Held that while the performance of rituals is religious, the appointment of priests is a secular function that the State can regulate.

- **5.** Ananda Marga Case (2004): Clarified that a practice is "essential" only if its absence fundamentally alters the religion itself.

- **6.** Acharya Jagadishwarananda Avadhuta case(2004): Introduced the "essentiality test". A practice is essential only if its absence fundamentally alters the religion.

- **7.** Haji Ali Dargah Case(2016): Struck down the ban on women entering the sanctum

- **8.** Shayara Bano Case(2017)

- **9.** Sabarimala Case(2018)

### Major Doctrines

- **1.** Doctrine of Essentiality: Only those practices that are "integral" or "fundamental" to a religion receive protection under Article 25.

- **2.** Constitutional Morality: The idea that the Constitution's core values (justice, liberty, equality) must prevail over traditional or religious norms if they conflict.

- **3.** Doctrine of Principled Distance: The state does not strictly separate from religion but intervenes only when religious practices infringe upon fundamental rights or social welfare.

- **4.** Doctrine of Pith and Substance: Used to determine if a law truly regulates a "secular" aspect of religion or is an unconstitutional strike at the "religious" core.

- **5.** Definitional Filter: The court first assesses if a claim is "religious" in nature (theistic vs. non-theistic) before applying other tests.

### Justification of Judicial Oversight

- **1.** Protection of Fundamental Rights: Courts intervene when religious customs violate Article 14 (Equality), ensuring traditions do not override rights.
