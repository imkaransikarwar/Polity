### Scheduled Caste Reservation

Recently, SC in Chinthada Anand v. State of Andhra Pradesh (2026) ruled that a person professing any religion other than Hinduism, Buddhism, or Sikhism cannot be recognised as a member of a SC community and conversion to any other religion (Islam or Christianity) results in the loss of SC status. (there is no religion-based restriction on ST status) SC held that as other religions do not theologically recognize the caste system, a person cannot publicly practice them while simultaneously claiming SC status

### SC Reservation criteria

- **1.** Under Article 341 of the Constitution, the President can specify the castes that are officially deemed Scheduled Castes.

- **2.** Once the initial Presidential list was published, only the Parliament of India has the authority to make additions or removal.

- **3.** SC eligibility is strictly governed by Clause 3 of the Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950.

- **4.** SC status is State/Union Territory-specific. A community recognized as a SC in one state may be categorized as general category or an OBC or in another state

### Caste census

- **1.** Caste-based census refers to the collection of demographic data that includes information about the caste or social group of individuals within a population.

- **2.** M. N. Srinivas (Indian sociologist) defines caste as a hereditary, endogamous, and usually localized group, linked to a specific occupation, and occupying a certain position in the social hierarchy.

- **3.** The Bihar government released the caste census and a few other state governments like Karnataka announced the same.

- **4.** After a gap of 16 years, the Government of India officially launched Phase 1 (House Listing and Housing Census) of the "Census 2027" on April 1, 2026.

- **5.** The Union Government confirmed that caste and sub-caste data will be collected nationwide for the first time since 1931.

- **6.** Every Census in independent India from 1951 to 2011 has published data on Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, but not on other castes. Before that, every Census until 1931 had data on caste.

- **7.** The Socio-Economic Caste Census (SECC) conducted in 2011 aimed to gather comprehensive data on socio-economic indicators, including caste information. However, issues regarding data quality and classification challenges have hindered the effective release and utilization of the raw caste data collected in the SECC.

### Need of caste census

- **1.** Evidence-Based Policymaking: A caste census can help design targeted welfare programs for the most marginalized within OBCs, SCs, and other underserved communities.

- **2.** Update Outdated Baselines - Current OBC quotas rely on 1931 colonial data extrapolated by the 1980 Mandal Commission, now nearly a century old

- **3.** Enable Sub-categorization - Granular data allows the state to divide broad categories like OBCs to ensure benefits reach "Extremely Backward Classes" (EBCs)

- **4.** Rationalize Reservation Quotas - Updated population figures may justify increasing the 50% reservation cap if demographics show significantly higher marginalized populations.
