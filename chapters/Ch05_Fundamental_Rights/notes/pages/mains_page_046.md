- **1.** M.P. Sharma v. Satish Chandra (1954) - SC initially held privacy is not a fundamental right.

- **2.** Kharak Singh v. State of U.P. (1962) - SC struck down domiciliary visits by police, linking privacy to personal liberty.

- **3.** Govind v. State of M.P. (1975) - SC hinted privacy is protected under Article 21, but not absolute.

- **4.** Justice K.S. Puttaswamy case: Privacy is a fundamental right intrinsic to dignity, liberty, autonomy.

### Scope of Right to Privacy (post-Puttaswamy)

- **1.** Informational Privacy - Protection of personal data, Aadhaar details, medical records, online footprints.

- **2.** Decisional Autonomy - Freedom to make intimate personal choices such as marriage, procreation, reproductive rights, and sexual orientation. Eg. Navtej Singh Johar (2018)

- **3.** Bodily Integrity - Protection from intrusive medical procedures, biometric misuse, surveillance, and forced sterilisation.

- **4.** Family, Marriage & Relationships - Choice of partner and right to marry a person of choice. Eg. Shafin Jahan v. Asokan (2018, Hadiya case) upheld interfaith marriage choice.

- **5.** Reproductive Rights - Autonomy over pregnancy decisions. Eg. X v. Principal Secretary, Health (2022) allowed abortion for unmarried women under MTP Act.

- **6.** Digital Privacy - Protection against surveillance, right to internet access, and data protection in cyberspace.

### Significance of Right to Privacy in India

Political Significance

- **1.** Protection from Arbitrary State Action - Limits police/state surveillance, checked through proportionality test.

- **2.** Strengthening Democracy - Enables dissent, protest, and freedom of expression without fear. Economic Significance

- **1.** Consumer & Corporate Regulation - Protects citizens from misuse of data in e-commerce, fintech, and big tech.

- **2.** Informational Privacy in the Digital Era - Prevents misuse of Aadhaar, biometrics, financial and medical data. Social Significance

- **1.** Dignity & Bodily Integrity - Safeguards reproductive rights and autonomy from forced medical procedures.

- **2.** Gender Justice & Equality - Ensures women's rights in marriage, contraception, and abortion.

- **3.** Protection of Minority Rights - Ensures religious practices are not arbitrarily interfered with. Technological Significance

- **1.** Safe Digital Space & Cyber Rights - Expands to right to internet, digital freedoms.

- **2.** Regulation of AI & Big Data - Prevents excessive state or corporate surveillance in technology-driven governance. Legal Significance

- **1.** Strengthening Rule of Law - Ensures restrictions are legal, necessary, proportionate (Puttaswamy proportionality test).

- **2.** Judicial Enforcement - Privacy protected via writs under Articles 32 & 226, making it enforceable against state abuse. Global / External Significance

- **1.** Global Human Rights Alignment - Consistent with Article 12 UDHR and Article 17 ICCPR.

- **2.** Enhances India's reputation as a constitutional democracy respecting civil liberties.
