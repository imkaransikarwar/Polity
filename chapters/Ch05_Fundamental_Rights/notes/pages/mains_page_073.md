| Feature | Supreme Court (Article 32) | High Court (Article 226) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Territorial Jurisdiction | Bigger: Can issue writs against any person or government throughout the entire territory of India. | Smaller: Limited to its own state or where the "cause of action" arises within its territory. |

### Significance

- **1.** Article 32 is a Fundamental Right. Ambedkar called it the "Heart and Soul" because rights are meaningless without a direct enforcement mechanism.

- **2.** Public Interest Litigation allows any citizen to approach courts for the protection of marginalized groups' rights. Eg. M C Mehta cases on environment

- **3.** Democratic Stability: By providing a legal vent for grievances, they prevent citizens from resorting to extra-constitutional means for justice.

- **4.** They provide a direct shield against the abuse of power, ensuring that fundamental rights are not just promises on paper but actionable guarantees.

- **5.** They act as a constitutional check and balance, forcing the executive and legislature to operate strictly within the boundaries of the law.

- **6.** They foster a culture of accountability and justice by allowing the marginalized to challenge systemic inequalities through Public Interest Litigation (PIL).

### Major Challenges

- **1.** Exponential Misuse: Eg. Justice Nagarathna flagged the "gross misuse" of Article 32 for seeking simple court adjournments.

- **2.** Massive Case Pendency: Eg. over 6 million cases pending in HCs, with writs forming a significant chunk.

- **3.** Executive Non-compliance: Eg. A 2026 report noted a 15% rise in contempt cases against state officials for ignoring Mandamus orders.

- **4.** Geographical Barriers: While Article 32 is Pan-India, traveling to Delhi is prohibitively expensive for citizens from remote southern or eastern regions.

- **5.** Cost of Litigation: High legal fees for senior advocates in writ matters make these remedies inaccessible to the poor.

- **6.** Vigilante Intimidation: Eg. attacks on RTI/PIL activists who filed writs against illegal mining.

- **7.** Over-reach Concerns: Eg. The Law Minister in 2025 suggested that writ jurisdiction should not interfere in "complex economic policy decisions."

### Major SC judgments

- **1.** L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997) - power of judicial review under Articles 32 and 226 is part of the "Basic Structure" of the Constitution.

- **2.** Kishore Samrite v. State of Uttar Pradesh (2013) - The SC established strict guidelines to prevent the abuse of the process of court through frivolous Article 32 petitions.

- **3.** S.S. Ahluwalia v. Union of India - SC can award compensation under Article 32 for the violation of fundamental rights by the State. Validity of Narco Tests The SC recently clarified the legal boundaries of narco-tests in Amlesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2025).
