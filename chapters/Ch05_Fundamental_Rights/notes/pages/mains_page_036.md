### Right to Equality

### Uniform Civil Code

It is a single set of laws governing personal matters like marriage, divorce, inheritance, adoption, and maintenance. Article 44 of DPSP of the Constitution states that the 'State shall endeavor to secure a Uniform Civil Code for the citizens throughout the territory of India.' In his Independence Day speech, the Prime Minister called for a Uniform Civil Code, advocating a secular civil framework to replace the existing religion-based personal laws.

### Present Status of Civil Laws in India

- **1.** Hindu Personal Laws - Codified under four Acts: Hindu Marriage Act (1955), Hindu Succession Act (1956), Hindu Minority & Guardianship Act (1956), and Hindu Adoptions & Maintenance Act (1956). Applies also to Sikhs, Jains, Buddhists.

- **2.** Muslim Personal Laws - Uncodified, based on religious texts; partly recognised under the Shariat Act (1937) and Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act (1939).

- **3.** Christian Personal Laws - Governed by the Indian Christian Marriage Act (1872).

- **4.** Secular Laws - Special Marriage Act (1954) allows inter-religious marriages; Guardians and Wards Act (1890) provides a secular guardianship framework.

- **5.** Regional Exceptions - Art. 371 grants exemptions to states like Nagaland, Mizoram, Assam for customary laws.

- **6.** Uniform Codes - Goa and Uttarakhand have adopted a UCC; states like Assam and Gujarat are exploring feasibility.

### Need for UCC

- **1.** Constitutional Duty - Art. 44 directs the state to secure UCC.

- **2.** Addressing Social Divisions - Eg. Polygamy under Shariat Act (1937) still legal for Muslims, but discriminatory against women.

- **3.** Equality under Law - Ensures equal rights for all citizens irrespective of religion, in line with Art. 14. Eg. Uttarakhand UCC (2024) bans polygamy and standardises marriage age at 21.

- **4.** Empowering Women - Removes patriarchal and discriminatory practices like triple talaq, unequal inheritance, child marriage.

- **5.** Simplifying Legal System - Replaces multiple personal laws with one uniform law, reducing judicial backlog and making rights clearer.

- **6.** National Integration - Strengthens common citizenship over religious identity; promotes constitutional patriotism (Habermas).

- **7.** Modernisation & Social Reform - Updates outdated practices and aligns laws with contemporary values. Eg. Navtej Johar (2018) decriminalised same-sex relations.

- **8.** International Alignment - Brings India closer to global human rights standards.
