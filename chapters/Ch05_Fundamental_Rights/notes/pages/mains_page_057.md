### Censorship in India

Censorship refers to the suppression, prohibition, or restriction of information, ideas, speech, or other forms of expression by governmental authorities, institutions, or other controlling bodies.

> "Censorship is the tool of those who need to hide actualities from themselves and others." - Isaac Asimov

> "The first condition of progress is the removal of censorship." - George Bernard Shaw

### Constitutional Basis

- **1.** Article 19(2) - Allows reasonable restrictions on grounds: sovereignty, security, public order, decency, morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to offence.

### Forms of Censorship

- **1.** Press & Print Media - Indirect through sedition, defamation, Press Council of India guidelines.

- **2.** Cinema - Regulated under Cinematograph Act, 1952; certification by Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC). Eg. Cuts in Padmavat (2018) citing public order.

- **3.** Television & Radio - Governed by Cable TV Networks (Regulation) Act, 1995 and programme codes.

- **4.** Digital Media & OTT - Regulated by IT Rules, 2021, giving government oversight over online content.

- **5.** Books & Literature - Bans under Customs Act and IPC for sedition/hate speech. Eg. Ban on Salman Rushdie's Satanic Verses (1988).

- **6.** Internet & Social Media - Section 69A of IT Act allows blocking of online content. Eg. Twitter handles blocked during farmers' protests (2021).

- **7.** Sahyog Platform under Section 79 of the IT Act - allows even district-level officers to demand social media firms pull down posts. Key Provisions of IT Rules, 2021

- For Social Media Intermediaries (SMI)

- Due Diligence: Platforms must inform users not to host/upload illegal content (defamatory, obscene, harmful to minors, etc.).

- Significant Social Media Intermediaries (SSMI): Platforms with over 5 million users face extra obligations: ◦ Resident Officers: Must appoint a Chief Compliance Officer, a Nodal Contact Person, and a Resident Grievance Officer (all must live in India). ◦ Traceability: Messaging apps (like WhatsApp) must be able to identify the "first originator" of a message if ordered by a court/government for serious crimes. ◦ Monthly Compliance Reports: Platforms must publish the number of complaints received and actions taken.

- Safe Harbour Protection: If a platform follows these rules, it is not legally liable for content posted by its users (Section 79 of the IT Act).

- For Digital Media & OTT Platforms (Ethics Code)

- Self-Classification: OTT platforms (Netflix, Hotstar, etc.) must classify content into age categories: U, U/A 7+, U/A 13+, U/A 16+, and A.

- Parental Locks: Platforms must provide parental locks for content rated U/A 13+ or higher.

- Three-Tier Grievance Redressal: ◦ Level I: Self-regulation by the publisher. ◦ Level II: Self-regulation by an independent body of publishers.
