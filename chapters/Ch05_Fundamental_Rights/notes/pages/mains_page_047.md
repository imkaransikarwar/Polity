### Challenges to Right to Privacy in India

Political

- **1.** Mass Surveillance by State - Eg. controversy over Pegasus spyware (2021).

- **2.** Lack of independent oversight on agencies like IB, RAW, NIA.

- **3.** Balancing Privacy with National Security - Frequent internet shutdowns in Jammu & Kashmir. Institutional

- **1.** Though the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 was enacted, critics argue it gives excessive exemptions to the government.

- **2.** Agencies continue to misuse outdated colonial laws. Eg. Telegraph Act for phone tapping

- **3.** Privacy often clashes with Right to Information, transparency in governance, and freedom of press, leading to judicial dilemmas. Technological

- **1.** Corporate Surveillance (Surveillance Capitalism) - Big tech firms harvest personal data for targeted ads without informed consent, threatening informational privacy.

- **2.** Health Data & Bio-Surveillance - COVID-19 contact tracing apps (like Aarogya Setu) sparked concerns over storage, retention, and misuse of sensitive health data. Social

- **1.** Lack of Awareness & Digital Literacy - Citizens often unaware of their privacy rights in digital and financial transactions, making them vulnerable to exploitation.

### Reasonable Restrictions

- **1.** Test of Proportionality - Puttaswamy (2017) - To justify restrictions on the Right to Privacy, three conditions must be satisfied:

  - **a.** Legality - Any interference with privacy must be sanctioned by law, with a just, fair, and reasonable procedure, free from arbitrariness.

  - **b.** Legitimate Aim - The law must serve a legitimate state interest (Eg. national security, public health), preventing unnecessary state intrusion.

  - **c.** Proportionality - The nature, method, and extent of interference must be proportionate to the purpose sought, ensuring minimal restriction of rights.

  - **d.** National Security - Eg. PUCL v. Union of India (1997) upheld phone tapping under strict safeguards.

  - **e.** Public Order & Crime Prevention - Eg. UAPA surveillance cases justified under "public order".

  - **f.** Protection of Rights of Others - Privacy cannot be invoked to harm others' rights (Eg. child safety, protection from domestic violence, misuse of digital content). g. Public Health & Safety - Eg. COVID-19 contact tracing and Aarogya Setu App justified under health emergency.

### Way Forward for Protection of Right to Privacy

- **1.** Judicial Oversight on Surveillance - Set up mechanisms like warrant-based approvals, review committees, and parliamentary scrutiny to prevent arbitrary phone tapping and spyware use.

- **2.** Strengthen application of legality, legitimacy, and proportionality test (Puttaswamy 2017) in all privacy-related state actions.

- **3.** Establish an independent Privacy Commission to monitor data use by government and corporates.

- **4.** Build secure data storage infrastructure in India and improve cybersecurity protocols to prevent leaks and hacks.

- **5.** Digital Literacy Campaigns to educate citizens on data protection, online safety, and privacy rights.
