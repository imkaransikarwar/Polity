◦ Level III: Oversight by an Inter-Departmental Committee (headed by the Ministry of Information & Broadcasting). April 2023 Amendment

- Fact Check Unit (FCU): Permitted the government to designate an agency to identify "fake or misleading" information regarding government business. Note: This was struck down by the Bombay High Court in 2024; the Supreme Court is currently examining the Centre's plea as of March 2026.

- Online Gaming: require them to be registered with a self-regulatory body and prohibit games involving wagering/betting. October 2025 Amendment

- Rule 3(1)(d) of the IT Rules, 2021 requires internet intermediaries to remove or disable access to unlawful content upon receiving a government order or notification.

- An officer of at least Joint Secretary rank or Inspector General of Police (DIG) rank can issue order Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026

- For the first time, Indian law provides a statutory definition for SGI. It includes any audio, visual, or audio-visual information created or modified using algorithms that appear "real, authentic, or true."

- Mandatory Labeling and Provenance - Intermediaries must ensure that all SGI is prominently and clearly labeled so that users can easily identify it as AI-generated.

- The window for removing unlawful content has been slashed from 36 hours to just 3 hours upon receiving a court order or government notification.

- For cases involving non-consensual nudity, deepfake pornography, or impersonation, the removal window is just 2 hours after a complaint is received.

- If a platform (like X, YouTube, or Meta) fails to label AI content or misses the 3-hour takedown deadline, it loses its Safe Harbour protection under Section 79 of the IT Act.

- Platforms must now acknowledge a user grievance within 24 hours and provide a final resolution within 7 days (down from the previous 15-day limit). MeiTY has also proposed to include "users who are not publishers" who share content related to "news and current affairs" within a "code of ethics" it currently applies to registered news publishers.

### Need for Censorship in India

- **1.** Communal Harmony: Prevents the spread of hate speech that could incite religious or ethnic violence in a diverse society.

- **2.** National Security: Eg. blocking of 59 Chinese applications including TikTok in 2020.

- **3.** Public Order: Restricts the viral spread of rumors that could lead to mob lynchings or mass panic. Eg. Internet shutdowns

- **4.** Sovereignty and Integrity: Eg. Authorities regularly seize publications or block websites displaying "incorrect" Indian borders.

- **5.** Protecting Minors: Regulates access to obscene material and pornographic content to safeguard child psychology. Eg. blocking of over 800 pornographic websites.

- **6.** Defamation and Contempt: Restricts expression that undermines judicial authority or causes unlawful harm to an individual's reputation. Eg. removal of NCERT content criticising judiciary

- **7.** Countering Deepfakes: Eg. The 2026 IT Rules requiring mandatory labeling of synthetically generated audio-visual content.

- **8.** Moral Standards: Ensures that broadcast content adheres to "community standards" of decency and morality. Eg. CBFC (Censor Board) cuts in films containing extreme violence or excessive profanity.
