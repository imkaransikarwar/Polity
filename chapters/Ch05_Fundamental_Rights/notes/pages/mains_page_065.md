### Way Forward

- **1.** Professionalisation of Management - Implement Vaidyanathan Committee recommendation for trained, full-time CEOs in cooperatives.

- **2.** Autonomy and Accountability - Ensure political neutrality and audit transparency by enforcing Model Bye-Laws and MSCS Amendment Act, 2023.

- **3.** Diversification Beyond Agriculture

  - **a.** Promote cooperatives in logistics, green energy, healthcare, and urban services, as suggested by NITI Aayog (2021).

  - **b.** Encourage multi-sector PACS through Margdarshika Plan.

- **4.** Integrated Policy Framework

  - **a.** Finalise the National Cooperative Policy with stakeholder input.

  - **b.** Harmonise laws to resolve dual control of multi-state cooperatives (as noted by the HLC on Cooperatives, 2013).

- **5.** Access to Capital and Credit

  - **a.** Encourage cooperative banks to meet Basel norms and expand refinancing from NCDC, NABARD, and SIDBI.

  - **b.** Operationalise umbrella body NUCFDC for urban cooperative banks.

- **6.** Grassroots Awareness and Inclusion

  - **a.** Expand cooperative education through Sahkar Bharti-type campaigns.

  - **b.** Ensure inclusion of women, SC/STs, and youth in leadership roles. To truly realise the vision of "Sahkar se Samriddhi", India must transform cooperatives into modern, autonomous, tech-driven institutions rooted in democratic governance. X-Factor Pointers on Cooperatives

- India hosts 27% of the world's cooperatives, with 20% of its population engaged, compared to the global average of 12%.

### Anti-Conversion Laws

Anti-conversion laws are the legislations that restrict or prohibit individuals from converting from one religion to another. In India, more than 10 states have anti-conversion laws. Article 25 guarantees freedom of conscience and right to profess, practice, and propagate religion. But this right is not absolute, subject to public order, morality, health, and other fundamental rights.

### Evolution of Anti-Conversion Laws

- **1.** Pre-Independence: Several princely states (Eg. Raigarh, Kota, Patna, Bikaner) enacted anti-conversion laws in the 1930s-40s.

- **2.** Post-Independence State Laws: Since Parliament never passed a national law, states legislated under Entry 1 (public order) and Entry 2 (criminal law) of State List.
