### Sedition Law

- **1.** In India, Section 124(A) of IPC defines sedition as an offense committed when any person by words or otherwise brings or attempts to bring into hatred or contempt, or excites or attempts to excite disaffection towards the government established by law in India by words, either spoken or written, signs, visible representation, or otherwise.

- **2.** The new Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita has removed section 124A of IPSC but has introduced similar provisions under Section 152.

- **3.** It penalizes anyone who, purposely or knowingly, by words, signs, electronic communication, or financial means, excites or attempts to excite secession, armed rebellion, or subversive activities.

### Statistics about Sedition Cases

- **1.** The 2023 NCRB report (released in 2025) noted a 26% drop in "Offences against the State," down from 7,128 in 2019 to 5,272 in 2023.

- **2.** Between 2015 and 2020, out of 548 persons arrested under Section 124A, only 12 faced conviction (approx. 2%).

- **3.** Pendency rate exceeding 90% for sedition-related trials, leading to "punishment by process.

### Need for Sedition Law

- **1.** Combating Anti-National Elements - Eg. Use against groups advocating for Khalistan or separatist movements in Jammu & Kashmir.

- **2.** Maintaining Public Order Prevents the incitement of violence that could lead to large-scale civil unrest or communal riots.

- **3.** Ensures that a government established by law is not subverted by unconstitutional, violent, or illegal means.

- **4.** Reasonable Restriction on Freedom of speech (Article 19). Upheld by SC in Kedar Nath (1962) case

- **5.** Curbing Fake News & Digital Warfare - The new BNS includes "electronic means" to tackle foreign-funded digital campaigns aimed at destabilizing India.

- **6.** Arguments by Law Commission,

  - **a.** The existence of laws such as the UAPA and the NSA does not cover all elements of the offense envisaged under Section 124A of the IPC.

  - **b.** The mere colonial origin of a legal provision does not automatically justify its repeal.

  - **c.** The mere occurrence of misuse doesn't necessitate the scrapping of a law; instead, establishing procedural safeguards can effectively curb any alleged misuse.

### Arguments Against

- **1.** Vagueness of "Subversive Activities" - The term is not defined in the BNS, allowing police to interpret legitimate protest as subversion.

- **2.** Chilling Effect on Dissent - The fear of life imprisonment silences journalists, activists, and citizens from criticizing government failures.

- **3.** Low Conviction Rate - only ~3% of sedition cases result in actual convictions. (NCRB Data)

- **4.** Colonial Baggage - Critics argue Section 152 is just "Old Wine in a New Bottle," retaining colonial-era suppression tactics.

- **5.** Weaponization for Political Vendetta - Eg. State governments using sedition against protesters in environmental or land-rights movements.

- **6.** Contradicts the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR), which India has ratified.

- **7.** Subjective "Tendency Test" allows arrests for poems or slogans even if no riot actually occurred.
