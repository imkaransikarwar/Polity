Recently, the case of phone tapping of political leaders in Telangana, NSE Phone Tapping Case has brought this issue into highlight.

### In India, phone tapping is governed by The Indian Telegraph Act, 1885.

- **1.** Section 5(2): At the center, 10 agencies (IB, CBI, etc.) and at states, police have powers to tap the phones.

- **2.** Authority: Under Rule 419A of the Indian Telegraph (Amendment) Rules, 2007, at the central level, the secretary in MoHA and at the state level, the secretary in the Home Department has the authority to issue orders for phone tapping. They must record reasons for tapping in writing.

- **3.** Grounds for phone tapping: If the center or states satisfy that it is necessary in case of public emergency in the interest of "public safety", "sovereignty and integrity of India, the security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States or public order or for preventing incitement to the commission of an offense".

### Safeguards available against phone tapping

- **1.** Last option: It must be ordered only if there is no other way of getting the information.

- **2.** Time period: If not revoked earlier, the order remains in force for 60 days. It can be renewed but not for more than 180 days in total.

- **3.** Review committee: At the center, it is headed by the Cabinet secretary, and at the state level, it is headed by the Chief Secretary. It reviews orders by competent authorities, copies of which are sent to it within 7 working days.

### Issues related to phone tapping

| Issue | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Violation of Right to Privacy | Infringes upon the right to privacy (Article 21) as sensitive information about personal life and business is tapped without permission. |
| Chilling Effect on Freedom of Speech | Fear of being monitored can lead to self-censorship and discourage open communication about sensitive topics. (PUCL vs. Union of India). |
| Abuse of Power | Governments might use phone tapping for political purposes, targeting dissidents or critics to suppress dissent. Example: Pegasus issue. |
| Divergence with International Obligation | Article 17 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights states that no one should face arbitrary interference with their privacy. India is a signatory. |
