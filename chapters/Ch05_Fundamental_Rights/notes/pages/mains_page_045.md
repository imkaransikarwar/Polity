### Way forward

- **1.** The law commission has recommended aligning the law with Kedar Nath judgment which stipulated that there must be a 'pernicious tendency to incite violence' to invoke the sedition law.

- **2.** Sensitization of law enforcement agencies to prevent the problem of misuse.

- **3.** Alternatives like strengthening existing mechanisms related to hate speech, and violence so that there would be no need to use it or use it sparingly.

### SC Judgments

- **1.** Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962): Upheld the law but limited its use only to acts involving incitement to violence or public disorder.

- **2.** P. Alavi vs. State of Kerala, 1982: SC held that sloganeering, criticizing Parliament, or Judicial setup did not amount to sedition.

- **3.** Vinod Dua (2021): The court quashed a sedition case against journalist Vinod Dua. It reiterated that criticism of the government isn't sedition and called for the "disinvocation" of the law in most cases.

- **4.** Javed Ahmad Hazam v. State of Maharashtra (2024): Ruled that dissent must be judged by the "reasonable person" standard, not by overly sensitive state actors.

### Global Best Practices

- **1.** United Kingdom: Formally abolished sedition in 2009

- **2.** United States: The "Clear and Present Danger" test

### Right to Privacy

Right to Privacy is recognised as part of Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty) and extended to Articles 14 and 19 (triple test of liberty).

> "Privacy is the constitutional core of human dignity." - Justice D.Y. Chandrachud

> "In an age of information, privacy is not merely a civil right but the very foundation of freedom." - UN Special Rapporteur on Privacy (2016) Evolution - Constituent Assembly on Right to Privacy

- Proposals by Sub-Committee on Fundamental Rights - Suggested: ◦ Inviolability of home against unreasonable searches & seizures. ◦ Secrecy of correspondence.

- Kazi Syed Karimuddin proposed explicit protection against state interference, searches, and seizures, modeled on American and Irish Constitutions.

- Members like B.N. Rau, K.M. Pannikar, dissented, arguing it would hinder law enforcement

- Ambedkar's Response - Noted that Criminal Procedure Code already provided safeguards; hence no need for constitutional mention.
