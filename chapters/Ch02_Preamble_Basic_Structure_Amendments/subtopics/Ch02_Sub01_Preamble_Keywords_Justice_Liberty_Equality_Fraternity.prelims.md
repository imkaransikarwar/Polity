- Eighth Schedule holds 22 languages [2024, 2008]; classical language status is separate [2015, 2014].

#### C. Ninth and Tenth Schedules
- The Ninth Schedule came with the First Amendment, 1951; entries after 1973 face Basic Structure review [2019,
2018].
- The Tenth Schedule carries anti defection [2014]; the presiding officer decides with no time frame [2022].
- Political party appears in the Constitution only through the Tenth Schedule [2025].

#### D. Basic Features and Core Concepts
- A Constitution defines and limits government power [2023, 2021, 2020, 2014].
- Rule of law: limitation of powers, equality before law, liberty and civil rights [2018].
- Separation of powers best safeguards liberty [2021]; due process means natural justice [2023].
- Concept staples: liberty [2019, 2018], rights against the State, equality as absence of privileges [2017].

### [Prelims Source Page 19](../pages/prelims/prelims_page_019.pdf)

Constitutional Supremacy
1. Proper synthesis between the British principle of Parliamentary sovereignty(Parliament has the sole authority over law
making on anything) and the
2. American principle of judicial supremacy(judiciary the ultimate authority to interpret the Constitution and laws of a
country.) In India we have Constitutional supremacy where the constitution is supreme not the organs.
Integrated + independent judiciary
1. SC at top of hierarchy then HC for one or more states below SC then below it subordinate courts.
2. Separation of powers under art 50 to keep judiciary independent.
Fundamental Rights
1. Six Fundamental Rights, in Part III (Arts 12 to 35).
2. Promote the ideal of political democracy.
3. Act as a limitation on the tyranny of the executive and on arbitrary laws of the legislature.
4. Justiciable and enforceable through Arts 32 and 226.
DPSPs
1. Contained in Part IV (Arts 36 to 51).
2. Promote the ideal of social and economic democracy.
3. Non justiciable under Art 37, yet declared fundamental in the governance of the country and a duty of the State to apply
in making laws.
Fundamental Duties
1. Ten Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh
Committee.
2. The eleventh, on providing education to a child between six and fourteen, was added by the 86th Amendment, 2002, so
there are eleven in all.
3. They sit in Part IVA, in the single Article 51A.
4. Not directly enforceable by any court, but given effect through ordinary statutes.
Secular State
Indian secularism means the State has no religion of its own and gives equal respect to all faiths, rather than the Western
wall of separation between religion and State.
It ensures
1. that one religious community does not dominate another;
2. that some members do not dominate other members of the same religious community;
3. that the State neither enforces any particular religion nor takes away the religious freedom of individuals.
The word secular was added to the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, though the concept was always implicit in Arts
14 to 16 and 25 to 28.

PREAMBLE
Introduction [PRELIMS 2021,2017]
1. Origin: The US Constitution was the first to begin with a Preamble.
2. Philosophy: Contains the philosophy and basic values of the Constitution; called its “identity card” (N.A. Palkhivala),
“soul” (Thakurdas Bhargava) and “horoscope” (K.M. Munshi).
3. Source: Based on Nehru’s Objectives Resolution, moved on 13 December 1946 and adopted on 22 January 1947.
4. Amendment: “Socialist,” “Secular” and “Integrity” added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, the only amendment so
far; their insertion was upheld by the SC in Balram Singh v Union of India (2024).
5. Source of authority: Declares the people of India as the source of authority of the Constitution; the Preamble itself
is neither a source of power to the legislature nor a limit on it.
6. Non-justiciable: Not enforceable in courts on its own; its ideals work through other provisions.
7. Interpretation: States the ideals and objectives, and helps interpret provisions where the text is ambiguous.
8. Aspirations: Reflects the aspirations of the people to be achieved.
Preamble: Keywords and Objectives [PRELIMS 2017]
Order of words: “Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic”. The original Preamble (adopted 26 November 1949)
read “Sovereign Democratic Republic”; Socialist, Secular and Integrity were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
1. Sovereign
- India is neither a dependency nor a dominion; internally supreme and externally free from foreign control.
Commonwealth or UN membership does not dilute it.
- Can acquire foreign territory; but ceding Indian territory needs a constitutional amendment (Art 368), not an
ordinary law under Art 3 (In re Berubari Union, 1960, advisory opinion). Eg. 9th Amendment, 1960 (Berubari); 100th
Amendment, 2015 (Land Boundary Agreement with Bangladesh).
2. Socialist (42nd Amendment, 1976)
- Democratic socialism: a mixed economy where public and private sectors coexist; not communistic socialism or
state ownership of everything. Aims at a welfare State through DPSPs, without abolishing private property.
- Dr Balram Singh v Union of India (25 November 2024): SC upheld the insertion of “socialist” and “secular”;
“socialist” means a welfare State and does not restrict economic policy.
3. Secular (42nd Amendment, 1976)
- The idea already existed in Arts 14 to 16 and 25 to 28.
- No State religion; the State neither favours nor penalises any faith.
- Positive secularism: equal respect for all religions (sarva dharma sambhava), not the Western “wall of separation”.
- Part of the Basic Structure (S.R. Bommai, 1994).
4. Democratic
- Popular sovereignty: “We, the People of India” are the source of authority.
- Indirect (representative) parliamentary democracy, not direct democracy; referendum, initiative and recall are not
part of the constitutional scheme.
- Pillars:
- Universal adult franchise (Art 326; voting age lowered from 21 to 18 by the 61st Amendment, 1988)
- Periodic free and fair elections
- One person, one vote
- An independent judiciary and rule of law
- Aims at political, social and economic democracy, echoing Ambedkar’s warning about a “life of contradictions”.
5. Republic
- Head of State is elected, not hereditary: the President is indirectly elected for a fixed 5 year term (Arts 52, 54, 55),
unlike the British Crown.
- Political sovereignty rests with the people; no privileged class; all public offices open to every citizen without
discrimination.

### [Prelims Source Page 21](../pages/prelims/prelims_page_021.pdf)

Objectives: Justice is taken from the Russian Revolution (1917); Liberty, Equality and Fraternity from the French Revolution
(1789).
6. Justice: Social, Economic, Political
- Social justice: no discrimination on grounds of caste, religion, sex etc. (Arts 15, 16, 17, 46).
- Economic justice: removal of gross inequalities in wealth and income (Arts 38, 39).
- Political justice: equal political rights and equal access to public office.
- Secured through a mix of Fundamental Rights and DPSPs.
7. Liberty: of Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith and Worship
- The Preamble secures only these five liberties; not “action” or “conscience”.
- Means absence of arbitrary restraint plus conditions for full development of personality.
- Not absolute: Art 19 rights are subject to reasonable restrictions.
8. Equality: of Status and Opportunity
- Core idea: absence of special privileges.
- Civic: Arts 14 to 18. Political: Arts 325, 326. Economic: Art 39(d) (equal pay for equal work).
9. Fraternity
- A sense of common brotherhood, promoted through single citizenship.
- Assures both (i) dignity of the individual and (ii) unity and integrity of the Nation; “Integrity” added in 1976.
- Reinforced by Fundamental Duty Art 51A(e).
Traps
- Word order: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic.
- Justice from the Russian Revolution; Liberty, Equality, Fraternity from the French Revolution.
- Fraternity covers both dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the Nation.
- Liberty lists five items only; action and conscience are not among them.
Is the Preamble amendable? The 42nd Amendment, 1976
1. Can it be amended? Yes. Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held the Preamble is part of the Constitution, so it can be
amended under Art 368, but not in a way that destroys its basic features.
2. Amended only once, by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, which:
- added “Socialist” and “Secular”, and
- changed “unity of the Nation” to “unity and integrity of the Nation”.
- So on 26 January 1950, India was a “Sovereign Democratic Republic”.
3. Not a source of power for the legislature, nor a limit on its powers.
4. Non-justiciable, but a “key to open the mind of the makers” (Berubari, 1960); used to resolve ambiguity in the text.
5. LIC v Consumer Education and Research Centre (1995) reaffirmed it as an integral part of the Constitution.
6. Insertion of “socialist” and “secular” upheld in Dr Balram Singh v Union of India (2024)
