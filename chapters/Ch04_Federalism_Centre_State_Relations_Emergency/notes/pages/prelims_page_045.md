| **Effects on FR** *(Contd.)* | war/external aggression, not armed rebellion (Art 358, 44th AA); 2. only laws carrying a recital linking them to the Emergency are protected.<br>**3.** Art 359 - President may by order suspend the right to move courts to enforce any FR except Arts 20 & 21, for whole or part of the Emergency. | — | — |
| Executive Control | CoM is not dismissed but they take executive directions from the Center on all matters. | The President assumes all or any of the functions of the Government of the State, of the Governor or any body or authority in the State (Art 356(1)(a)). The takeover is optional and can be partial. The CM and State Council of Ministers are dismissed. In practice, the Governor runs the State as the President's agent with the Chief Secretary heading the administration. The President cannot assume the powers of a High Court (Art 356(1)). | **1.** States are bound to follow financial guidelines of the center.<br>**2.** Reduce pay of judges of HC and functionaries even. |
| Legislative Control [PRELIMS 2018,2017] | **1.** The State Legislature is not dismissed but the system becomes Unitary.<br>**2.** Parliament can legislate on State subjects (Art 250); 3. such laws cease 6 months after the Emergency ends. | The Proclamation may transfer the State Legislature's powers to Parliament (Art 356(1)(b)). The President cannot take these powers himself. They reach him only through delegation by Parliament (Art 357(1)(a)). The Proclamation may keep the Assembly in suspended animation. The Proclamation cannot dissolve the Assembly. Dissolution cannot happen before both Houses approve the Proclamation (S.R. Bommai, 1994). This is a judicial rule, not part of the text of Art 356. After both Houses approve the Proclamation, the President may dissolve the Assembly. He uses the Governor's power to do so. | — |
