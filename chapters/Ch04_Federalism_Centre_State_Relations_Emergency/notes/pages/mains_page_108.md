### Presidential Reference

Recently, The President invoked Article 143(1) of the Constitution to seek the Supreme Court's opinion on the scope of powers of Governors and the President under Articles 200 and 201. The reference follows a recent Supreme Court ruling that set clear timelines for Governors and the President to act on bills passed by state legislatures. The verdict also introduced the idea of 'deemed consent' to address delays caused by gubernatorial inaction. It is a power that the President has invoked on at least 15 occasions since 1950. In 1951, India's first President Rajendra Prasad made the first reference to the Supreme Court in relation to provisions of Delhi Laws Act, 1912, the Ajmer-Merwara (Extension of Laws) Act, 1947 and Part C of the States (Laws) Act of 1950.

### Article 143 (Advisory jurisdiction of SC)

Article 143(1) - Discretionary Reference

- **1.** The President may refer to the Supreme Court a question of law or fact that is of public importance.

- **2.** The Supreme Court may choose to answer the reference or not.

- **3.** The Court's opinion is advisory, not binding, but carries significant constitutional weight. Article 143(2) - Mandatory Reference

- **1.** If any question arises relating to pre-constitutional treaties or agreements, the President must refer it to the Court.

- **2.** The Court is obligated to give its opinion in this case. Article 145(3) requires any such reference to be heard by five judges, after which the SC returns the reference to the President with the majority opinion. Important Judgments

- In re Kerala Education Bill (1958) - First use of Article 143(1). Set precedent for judicial restraint and clarified that advisory opinion is not binding.
