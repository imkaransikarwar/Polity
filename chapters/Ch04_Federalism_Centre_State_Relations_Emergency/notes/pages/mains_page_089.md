### Competitive Federalism

Competitive federalism refers to the healthy competition among different levels of government within a federal system to enhance efficiency, governance, and resource attraction.

### Steps Taken Towards Competitive Federalism

- **1.** Monthly rankings of 112 Aspirational Districts create "fame and shame" pressure on district collectors to improve social indicators.

- **2.** Ease of Doing Business (EoDB) Rankings - DPIIT ranks states based on their implementation of the Business Reform Action Plan (BRAP).

- **3.** Fiscal incentives - 16th FC has linked a portion of central grants to state performance in areas like fiscal discipline, forest conservation, and implementation of power sector reforms.

- **4.** Sectoral Performance Indices

  - **a.** Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) India Index: Ranks states on 16 goals, pushing them to achieve universal targets like poverty reduction and clean energy.

  - **b.** India Innovation Index: Measures the innovation ecosystem across states, encouraging a "knowledge economy."

  - **c.** Export Preparedness Index (EPI): Identifies the export potential and performance of states, pushing landlocked and coastal states to build better logistics.

  - **d.** State Health Index: Compares health outcomes, driving states like Uttar Pradesh and Bihar to bridge gaps with leaders like Kerala.

  - **e.** Composite Water Management Index - Measuring progress on water resource management.

- **5.** Reforms in Labor Laws and Land Acquisition - States have reformed labor laws and land acquisition processes to attract investment

- **6.** PM Gati Shakti: States compete to align their industrial corridors and logistic parks with the national master plan to receive central funding and attract multinational manufacturing hubs

- **7.** Competitive Social Welfare Models - Eg. "Delhi Education Model" & "Odisha 5T Transformation" has forced other states to rethink school infrastructure and digital governance to stay politically and economically competitive.

### Significance of Competitive Federalism

- **1.** Investment Attraction: Eg. labor reforms and land subsidies to attract subsidies

- **2.** Efficiency in Service Delivery: Public rankings create reputational pressure on governments to improve basic services. Eg. Jal Jeevan Mission dashboard.

- **3.** Innovation in Public Policy: States act as "policy laboratories," testing unique welfare models that are later scaled nationally. Eg. Odisha's 5T school transformation model (2025)
