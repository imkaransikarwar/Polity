### PRELIMS PREVIOUS YEAR QUESTIONS

#### Q1 (2021)

**Which one of the following in Indian polity is an essential feature that indicates that it is federal in character?**

- **(a)** The independence of the judiciary is safeguarded.
- **(b)** The Union Legislature has elected representatives from constituent units.
- **(c)** The Union Cabinet can have elected representatives from regional parties.
- **(d)** The Fundamental Rights are enforceable by Courts of Law.

> **Answer:** `(a)` · [Prelims Source Page 36](../../pages/prelims/prelims_page_036.pdf)

#### Q2 (2017)

**Which one of the following is not a feature of Indian federalism ?**

- **(a)** There is an independent judiciary in India.
- **(b)** Powers have been clearly divided between the Centre and the States.
- **(c)** The federating units have been given unequal representation in the Rajya Sabha.
- **(d)** It is the result of an agreement among the federating units.

> **Answer:** `(d)` · [Prelims Source Page 36](../../pages/prelims/prelims_page_036.pdf)

#### FEDERAL AND UNITARY FEATURES

| PARAMETER | FEDERAL FEATURES | UNITARY FEATURES | TRAP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nature of the polity [PRELIMS 2021,2017] | Dual polity: a Union government and State governments, each drawing authority directly from the Constitution. | Art 1 describes India as a Union of States. In Dr Ambedkar's explanation the federation is not the result of an agreement among the units, and no State has a right to secede. | Ambedkar: an indestructible union of destructible States, the mirror of the American formula. |
| Distribution of powers | Three lists in the Seventh Schedule: Union, State and Concurrent, so the division is constitutionally guaranteed. | Residuary powers rest with the Union. Parliament may legislate on the State List under Arts 249, 250, 252, 253 and during Art 356. | In the USA and Australia the residuary power lies with the States; in Canada, as in India, with the Centre. |
| The Constitution itself | Written and largely rigid, and supreme: any law inconsistent with it is void under Art 13. | Most of it can be amended by Parliament alone; only the entries in the proviso to Art 368(2) need ratification by half the States. | A blend of rigidity and flexibility, unlike the uniformly rigid American Constitution. |
| Judiciary | An independent judiciary headed by the Supreme Court umpires Centre and State disputes under Art 131, the essential federal indicator. | A single integrated judiciary enforcing both Union and State laws, with judges appointed by the Centre. | The USA has a dual judiciary, federal and State. India deliberately did not. |
| Legislature | Bicameralism, with the Rajya Sabha as the chamber representing the States. | Representation is unequal, in proportion to population under the Fourth Schedule, and members are elected by State legislators, not by the people. | The US Senate gives two seats to every State regardless of size. Equal representation of units is the classic federal test India fails. |
| Citizenship | None. A federal marker India chose not to adopt. | Single citizenship for the whole country. | The USA, Switzerland and Australia have dual citizenship, federal and State. |
| Executive of the unit | The State government is elected and responsible to its own legislature. | The Governor is appointed by the Centre and holds office during the pleasure of the President (Art 155, 156). | The Governor doubles as the agent of the Centre, which is why Art 356 works through him. |
| Public services | State Public Service Commissions and State services. | All India Services (IAS, IPS, IFoS) are common to the Centre and the States but controlled by the Centre (Art 312). | Officers serve the States but the Centre holds disciplinary and cadre control. |
