### President's Rule

President's Rule ensures federal stability during crises and is governed by Articles 355 to 357 (Part XVIII) and Article 365 (Part XIX) of the Indian Constitution.

- **1.** Article 355 - Mandates the Union to protect states from external aggression and internal disturbances.

- **2.** Article 356 - Allows the President to assume state government functions if the constitutional machinery fails.

- **3.** Article 357 - Grants the President power to legislate for a state via ordinance when President's Rule is in effect.

- **4.** Article 365 - Specifies the consequences for a state failing to comply with Union directives, potentially leading to President's Rule.

### Impact of President's Rule

- **1.** State Executive:

- **2.** Assumption of State Government Functions

- **3.** Dismissal of Council of Ministers

- **4.** The President can sanction expenditure from the state consolidated fund when the Lok Sabha is not in session, pending Parliament's approval.

- **5.** Ordinance Promulgation by the President.

- **6.** State Legislature:

- **7.** The President can declare that the powers of the state legislature are to be exercised by the Parliament.

- **8.** Suspension or Dissolution of State Legislative Assembly

- **9.** Parliament's Authority Over State Matters: passing state bills and budgets.

- **10.** Laws enacted by Parliament continue to remain effective even after the imposition of President's Rule.

- **11.** Judiciary: The Constitutional position, status, powers, and functions of the State High Court remain unchanged

- **12.** Fundamental Rights of Citizens: The fundamental Rights of citizens are not impacted upon use of Article 356.

### Issues pertaining to Article 356

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar hoped that the drastic power under Article 356 would remain a Dead Letter and only be a last resort.

- **1.** Arbitrary Dismissal of Governments: Eg. invoked over 100 times since independence

- **2.** Political Misuse - Often invoked to remove opposition-led governments. Eg. President rule in Kerala in 1959.

- **3.** Prolonged Use of President's Rule: Eg. over 4 years in Jammu and Kashmir

- **4.** The term "failure of constitutional machinery" lacks a precise legal definition, allowing for subjective central interpretation.

- **5.** Partisan Role of Governors: Eg. Sarkaria Commission noted that 90% of Article 356 cases were based on purely political gubernatorial recommendations.
