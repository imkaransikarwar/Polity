### Financial Emergency (Article 360)

It is a constitutional provision in India that allows the Union government to take control of the financial affairs of the State and the country if the economic stability or credit of India is threatened.

### Proclamation

- **1.** The President can declare a Financial Emergency if he is satisfied that a situation has arisen whereby the financial stability or credit of India (or any part of its territory) is threatened.

- **2.** The proclamation must be approved by both Houses of Parliament within two months by a simple majority. Once approved, it continues indefinitely until revoked by the President. There is no maximum period prescribed, and it does not require periodic approval like a National Emergency.

### Key Effects

- **1.** Executive Control: The Union can give directions to any State to observe specific canons of "financial propriety."

- **2.** The President can issue orders to reduce the salaries and allowances of any class of persons serving the State or the Union, Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.

- **3.** The President can require that all Money Bills or other financial bills passed by a State Legislature be reserved for his consideration.
