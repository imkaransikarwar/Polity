### Federalism

"Indian Union is a unitary state with subsidiary federal features rather than a federal state with subsidiary unitary features." - K.C. Wheare. "India is a Union of States, not a federation of states... the Union is indestructible, though the states are not." - B.R. Ambedkar

> "Centre and States are co-equal in their respective fields, bound together by a spirit of cooperation." - SC in Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006)

> "Indian federalism is highly centralized, designed more to maintain unity than to promote autonomy." - Paul Brass Federalism is a system of governance where power is distributed between a central authority and regional entities like states or provinces. Scholarly Views on Indian Federalism

- K.C. Wheare - Described Indian federalism as "quasi-federal", meaning a federation with a strong centralizing tendency.

- Granville Austin - a "cooperative federalism" model, where Centre and States are bound in a "seamless web" to achieve socio-economic revolution.

- Ivor Jennings - Stated that India is "a federation with a strong centralising tendency", highlighting the dominant role of the Union.

- D.D. Basu - Termed Indian federalism as a "sui generis model" (unique in itself), combining both federal and unitary features.

- Rajeev Bhargava - Saw Indian federalism as "multi-layered federalism", evolving towards cooperative and competitive models under economic liberalisation.

- Arend Lijphart - Argued that Indian federalism is an example of "consociational democracy", managing pluralism through power-sharing.

- R. Kothari - Viewed Indian federalism as "centripetal federalism", ensuring stability through a strong Centre while granting States participatory space.

### Key Features of Federalism

- **1.** Division of Powers - Powers divided through the Union List, State List, and Concurrent List (Seventh Schedule).

- **2.** Dual Polity - Division of governance between the Union and States, each with its own government.

- **3.** Written Constitution - Provides clarity and certainty in the division of powers.

- **4.** Supremacy of the Constitution - Both Centre and States are bound by the Constitution as the supreme law.

- **5.** Rigid Constitution - Certain provisions can only be amended by special procedure, requiring consent of States.

- **6.** Independent Judiciary - The Supreme Court acts as guardian and interpreter of the Constitution to resolve Centre-State disputes.

- **7.** Bicameralism at Centre - Rajya Sabha represents States in the Union legislature.

- **8.** Financial Autonomy - Distribution of financial resources and functions through Finance Commission and constitutional provisions.
