Social Significance

- **1.** Inclusive Governance - Enhances representation of diverse communities at local, state, and national levels.

- **2.** Social Justice - Enables States to frame affirmative action policies based on local needs. Eg. Tamil Nadu's 69% reservation model.

- **3.** Cultural and Linguistic Preservation: Eg. Tamil Nadu enforcing 2 language formula

- **4.** National Integration - Balances regional identity with national citizenship, strengthening unity.

### Asymmetric Federalism

Asymmetric federalism refers to an unequal distribution of powers and relationships in the political, administrative, and fiscal spheres among federal units within a federation. According to Riker (1975), federalism is an outcome of rational bargain among various constituents.

### Key Aspects

- **1.** Dual Asymmetry - It exists in both vertical (Centre vs. States) and horizontal (among States/UTs) dimensions.

- **2.** Self-Rule & Shared Rule - India follows a "salad bowl" approach, recognizing distinct cultural identities and permitting self-governance within a unified structure.

- **3.** Founding Vision allows for regional autonomy while ensuring a strong central authority when needed.

### Vertical Asymmetry (Between Centre and States)

- **1.** Article 3 -Parliament can alter state boundaries and names, without state consent

- **2.** Single Citizenship - Unlike US, India has only one citizenship for uniform national identity

- **3.** Articles 352 & 356 empower the Centre to take direct control of states during Emergency.

- **4.** Article 248 - Residuary legislative powers rest solely with the Parliament.

- **5.** Governor Appointment - appointed by the President & serves as the Centre's representative

- **6.** 7th Schedule - On subjects in the Concurrent List, Parliament laws override state laws.

- **7.** All India Services: ultimate control lies with the centre.

### Horizontal Asymmetry (Among States/UTs)

- **1.** Articles 371A-371J - Provide special provisions and autonomy to certain states.

- **2.** Schedule 5 & 6 - self-governance in tribal regions.

- **3.** Article 370 (Revoked in 2019) - special status to J&K with substantial autonomy

- **4.** Unequal representation in the Rajya Sabha: The allocation of seats based on population.

### Fiscal Asymmetry

- **1.** Centre controls buoyant tax sources (income tax, corporate tax, GST share). States bear higher expenditure responsibilities (health, education, welfare, agriculture).

- **2.** Horizontal Devolution - criteria of income disparity, demographic trends, and ecological performance.

- **3.** Article 293: States' borrowing is subject to Centre's consent.

- **4.** CSS - Central share of 90% for Special Category States (60% for others)

- **5.** Special Category Status - Granted by the National Development Council (NDC) to states with hilly terrain, low population density, or economic backwardness.
