## CHAPTER 04
UNITARY VS FEDERAL
THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter
1. REPEATS

#### A. The Essential Federal Marker
- The one essential federal feature tested is a safeguarded independent judiciary, umpiring Centre and State disputes
under Art 131 [2021].
- Indian federalism is not the result of an agreement among federating units; Ambedkar called it an indestructible
Union of destructible States [2017].
- Unequal State representation in the Rajya Sabha is Indian, unlike the equal American Senate; equal representation is
the classic federal test India fails [2017].

#### B. What Makes the Polity Federal
- Dual polity: Union and State governments, each drawing authority directly from the Constitution.
- Three lists in the Seventh Schedule; a written, largely rigid and supreme Constitution, with inconsistent law void under
Art 13.
- Bicameralism, with the Rajya Sabha as the chamber representing the States.

#### C. The Unitary Tilt
- Residuary powers rest with the Union; Parliament reaches the State List under Arts 249, 250, 252, 253 and during Art
356.
- Single citizenship, a single integrated judiciary, one Election Commission and one CAG for the Union and the States.
- All India Services under Art 312; the Governor appointed by the Centre and holding office during the President's
pleasure.
- Art 3: Parliament alters the area, boundaries or name of a State by simple majority; no State has guaranteed territory.

#### D. Labels and Comparisons
- K.C. Wheare: quasi federal; Granville Austin: cooperative federalism; the Constitution never uses the word federation.
- Under Arts 352, 356 and 360 the Constitution turns unitary, with the Centre directing the States.
- Residuary power lies with the States in the USA and Australia, with the Centre in Canada and India; the USA has a dual
judiciary and dual citizenship.
2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED
- 2017: a single answer "not a feature" stem on Indian federalism [2017]
- 2021: a single answer on the one essential feature that makes the polity federal [2021]
3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW
- Federal character tested through the one essential feature, an independent judiciary [2021]
- Sorting features as federal or unitary: Seventh Schedule, Rajya Sabha composition, single citizenship, All India
Services and Art 3

## CHAPTER 06
EMERGENCY PROVISIONS
THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter
1. REPEATS

#### A. What President's Rule Actually Does
- Parliament exercises the State's law making power; the President does not legislate [2018].
- The Assembly may be dissolved or suspended, so dissolution is not automatic [2017, 2018].
- The Council of Ministers always goes; local bodies continue [2017].
- The High Court is untouched, a standing distractor.

#### B. The Three Emergencies Compared
- Article 352 on war or armed rebellion; Article 356 on failure of constitutional machinery; Article 360 on financial
stability.
- Special majority for Article 352, simple majority for Articles 356 and 360.
- Six months at a time with a three year ceiling for Article 356; no ceiling for Article 352.

#### C. Effects on Fundamental Rights
- Article 358 suspends Article 19 only under National Emergency.
- Article 359 suspends enforcement, but Articles 20 and 21 stay protected.
- No right is suspended under Article 356, the trap planted in [2018].

#### D. Safeguards and Case Law
- The Forty Fourth Amendment Act, 1978 replaced internal disturbance with armed rebellion.
- S.R. Bommai made the proclamation justiciable and barred dissolution before approval [2017].
- The Sarkaria and Punchhi Commissions on sparing use.
2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED
- 2017: a code based "not necessarily" question on President's Rule [2017]
- 2018: a single answer stem on Art 356 with Art 19 as the distractor [2018]
3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW
- No question since 2018; when it returns, expect statement sets on what President's Rule and each emergency
actually change
- Hooks: the Assembly can be kept in suspended animation; Art 19 is suspended only in a war or external aggression
emergency

## CHAPTER 25
FEDERAL BODIES
THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter
1. REPEATS

#### A. Tricky Statements and Subtle Distinctions
- Zonal Councils are statutory creations of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956, not constitutional bodies.
- The Inter State Council has a constitutional base in Art 263 yet was set up by a Presidential order, on the Sarkaria
Commission's recommendation.
- The GST Council is the only constitutional body of the three, created by Art 279A.
- The North Eastern Council is statutory, under the North Eastern Council Act, 1971, and is not one of the five Zonal
Councils [2024].

#### B. Type and Legal Basis at a Glance
- Art 279A created the GST Council through the 101st Amendment Act, 2016.
- The Union Home Minister chairs all five Zonal Councils, with a Chief Minister as rotating Vice Chairman.
- The North Eastern Council is a separate statutory body and is not one of the five Zonal Councils.
- NITI Aayog rests only on a Cabinet resolution of 1 January 2015, as the Planning Commission rested on a 1950
resolution [2015].

#### C. Decision Making and Voting Arithmetic
- A GST Council decision needs three fourths of the weighted votes of members present and voting.
- The Centre holds one third of that weight and all States together two thirds.
- The Inter State Council and Zonal Councils only advise and take no binding vote.
- NITI Aayog and the NEC are both advisory - neither allocates funds nor issues binding orders.

#### D. Who Chairs and Who Sits
- Prime Minister for the Inter State Council, Union Home Minister for the Zonal Councils, Union Finance Minister for
the GST Council.
- The GST Council includes the Union Minister of State for Finance and a minister nominated by each State.
- Art 263 lets the President define the Council's duties, so its mandate rests on the order, not on the Article alone.
- The NEC comprises the Governor and Chief Minister of each of the eight States plus three Members nominated by
the President; the Home Minister chairs it by a 2018 Cabinet decision, not by the 2002 amendment [2024].

#### E. Bodies Not Mentioned in the Constitution
- Zonal Councils, the Planning Commission and the National Development Council all find no mention in the
Constitution [2013].
- Only the Inter-State Council among the coordination bodies has a constitutional source, Art 263 [2025].
- The NDC comprised the Prime Minister, Union Cabinet Ministers, Chief Ministers and members of the Planning
Commission, never the Chairman of the Finance Commission [2013].
- Also tested: NDC status, planning in the Concurrent List, Panchayat plans [2013]
2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED
- 2013: three code sets on the NDC and bodies outside the Constitution [2013]
- 2015: a single answer stem on NITI Aayog [2015]
- 2024 to 2025: a composition set and a "how many" status count [2024, 2025]
3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW
- Legal status: constitutional (Inter State Council, Art 263) versus executive or statutory [2025]
- Membership: the NEC after 2002 [2024]

Who Chairs and Who Sits • Prime Minister for the Inter State Council, Union Home Minister for the Zonal Councils, Union Finance Minister for the GST Council. • The GST Council includes the Union Minister of State for Finance and a minister nominated by each State. • Art 263 lets the President define the Council's duties, so its mandate rests on the order, not on the Article alone. • The NEC comprises the Governor and Chief Minister of each of the eight States plus three Members nominated by the President; the Home Minister chairs it by a 2018 Cabinet decision, not by the 2002 amendment [2024]. E. Bodies Not Mentioned in the Constitution • Zonal Councils, the Planning Commission and the National Development Council all find no mention in the Constitution [2013]. • Only the Inter-State Council among the coordination bodies has a constitutional source, Art 263 [2025]. • The NDC comprised the Prime Minister, Union Cabinet Ministers, Chief Ministers and members of the Planning Commission, never the Chairman of the Finance Commission [2013]. • Also tested: NDC status, planning in the Concurrent List, Panchayat plans [2013] 2.
