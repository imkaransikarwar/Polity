Need and Significance
1. Political Significance
a. Rekha Saxena describes Indian Asymmetric Federalism as 'Self-rule within shared rule'
b. National Integration - tailored autonomy for preventing alienation and separatism. Eg. Article 371F
for Sikkim's accession (1975).
c. Conflict Resolution - Eg. Mizoram Accord (1986) under Sixth Schedule.
2. Institutional Significance
a. Decentralised Governance Eg. Autonomous District Councils under Sixth Schedule
b. Democratic Representation - Eg. Bodoland Territorial Council (BTC, 2020) for STs.
3. Economic Significance
a. Bridging Regional Imbalances - Eg. N-E states get 90:10 funding ratio under CSS.
b. Equity in resource distribution - Hill states like Himachal, UK get higher devolution.
4. Social Significance
a. Cultural Autonomy - Eg. Nagaland's customary laws protected under Article 371A
b. Inclusivity & Harmony - Prevents majority domination and fosters trust.
c. Unity in Diversity - Builds confidence in communities within a national framework. Eg. Goa
retained Portuguese Civil Code post-integration.
Issues
1. Political
a. Asymmetric provisions have weakened due to legislative manoeuvres and judicial
reinterpretations, causing a centralising drift. Eg. Abrogation of Article 370
b. Politicisation of Asymmetry -Eg. Demand for special category status by Bihar
c. Threat to National Unity - Excessive asymmetry encourages identity politics and secessionist
tendencies. Eg. Demands for greater autonomy in Manipur hills
d. Representation Imbalances - Delimitation reducing clout of southern states
2. Institutional
a. Complexity in Governance - Eg. Erstwhile J&K had separate civil and criminal procedure, no
reservations for SC/STs
b. Administrative Complexity causes bureaucratic delays and weak coordination. Eg. Delays in fund
utilisation in tribal councils
c. Ambiguities in Provisions - Lack of clarity in constitutional provisions leads to legal disputes. Eg.
Article 370 case in Supreme Court
3. Economic
a. Uneven Development - Fiscal and institutional asymmetries deepen regional inequalities. Eg. NE
states dependent on central grants
4. Social
a. Ethnic Tensions - Autonomous regions face internal conflicts between different ethnic groups.
b. Social Integration Challenges - Special statuses may foster regionalism instead of integration. Eg.
Gorkhaland demand in West Bengal
Way Forward
1. As recommended by the Sarkaria Commission (1983) and NCRWC (2000), the ISC should be made an
independent body under Article 263 to resolve Centre-state disputes.
2. Establishing clear guidelines for Special Category Status (SCS).

Cooperative Federalism
Cooperative federalism refers to a governance model where national, state, and local governments work
collectively to address common challenges through collaboration and shared responsibilities.
The SC, in State of Rajasthan v. Union of India (1977), affirmed that India's federalism is predominantly
cooperative, requiring mutual interaction between Centre and States.
