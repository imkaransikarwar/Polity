## CHAPTER 06
EMERGENCY PROVISIONS
THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter
1. REPEATS

#### A. What President's Rule Actually Does
- Parliament exercises the State's law making power; the President does not legislate [2018].
- The Assembly may be dissolved or suspended, so dissolution is not automatic [2017, 2018].
- The Council of Ministers always goes; local bodies continue [2017].
- The High Court is untouched, a standing distractor.

#### B. The Three Emergencies Compared
- Article 352 on war or armed rebellion; Article 356 on failure of constitutional machinery; Article 360 on financial
stability.
- Special majority for Article 352, simple majority for Articles 356 and 360.
- Six months at a time with a three year ceiling for Article 356; no ceiling for Article 352.

#### C. Effects on Fundamental Rights
- Article 358 suspends Article 19 only under National Emergency.
- Article 359 suspends enforcement, but Articles 20 and 21 stay protected.
- No right is suspended under Article 356, the trap planted in [2018].

#### D. Safeguards and Case Law
- The Forty Fourth Amendment Act, 1978 replaced internal disturbance with armed rebellion.
- S.R. Bommai made the proclamation justiciable and barred dissolution before approval [2017].
- The Sarkaria and Punchhi Commissions on sparing use.
2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED
- 2017: a code based "not necessarily" question on President's Rule [2017]
- 2018: a single answer stem on Art 356 with Art 19 as the distractor [2018]
3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW
- No question since 2018; when it returns, expect statement sets on what President's Rule and each emergency
actually change
- Hooks: the Assembly can be kept in suspended animation; Art 19 is suspended only in a war or external aggression
emergency
