| EVENT AND YEAR | KEY PROVISIONS / SIGNIFICANCE | TRAP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Montagu Declaration (August Declaration), 20 Aug 1917 | Secretary of State E.S. Montagu declared British policy to be the 'increasing association of Indians in every branch of the administration and the gradual development of self-governing institutions, with a view to the progressive realisation of responsible government in India as an integral part of the British Empire'. | **1.** The first official commitment to responsible government - and the goal was 'responsible government', never 'Dominion status'.<br>**2.** The 1917 declaration is the preamble/promise behind the 1919 Act. |
| Road to the 1935 Act, 1927 to 1935 [PRELIMS 2010,2009] | From Simon to the 1935 Act (1927 to 1935)<br>**(a)** Simon Commission: appointed 1927, reported 1930.<br>**(b)** Nehru Report (1928, Motilal Nehru): the first Indian draft constitution, with a list of fundamental rights and Dominion status.<br>**(c)** Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929).<br>**(d)** Three Round Table Conferences (1930 to 1932).<br>**(e)** Communal Award (1932, Ramsay MacDonald), then the Poona Pact (September 1932).<br>**(f)** White Paper (1933), then the Joint Select Committee (Lord Linlithgow), then the GoI Act 1935. | **1.** The Communal Award gave separate electorates to the Depressed Classes;<br>**2.** The Poona Pact replaced them with reserved seats in joint electorates.<br>**3.** Gandhi attended only the Second RTC;<br>**4.** Ambedkar attended all three.<br>**5.** Fundamental rights enter Indian constitutional thinking with the Nehru Report (1928), not the 1935 Act, which has none. |
| Government of India Act, 1935 [PRELIMS 2018,2012,2010] | **1.** Longest British statute till then (321 sections, 10 schedules).<br>**2.** Provincial autonomy replacing dyarchy - provinces as autonomous units with responsible ministries, in force from 1 April 1937; 3. three lists - Federal (59), Provincial (54), Concurrent (36);<br>**4.** Bicameralism in 6 of the 11 provinces (Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, UP);<br>**5.** Abolished the Council of India (Secretary of State given advisers); provided for a Federal Court (set up 1937) and for Federal, Provincial and Joint Public Service Commissions. | [Prelims 2018] Residuary powers went to the Governor General, not to the Federal Legislature, the Provincial Legislatures or the Governors. Burma was separated from India (1937) by a separate Act; Sind and Orissa became new provinces. The Act is the single largest source of our Constitution: the federal scheme, Governors, judiciary, PSCs, emergency powers and administrative detail. |
| 1935 Act - Federation & dyarchy at the Centre, 1935 [PRELIMS 2024] | **1.** Provided an All-India Federation of the British Indian provinces (compulsory) plus the princely states (voluntary accession) - it needed rulers entitled to not less than half the 104 princely-state seats in the Council of State to accede.<br>**2.** Introduced dyarchy at the Centre: defence, external affairs, ecclesiastical affairs and tribal areas reserved to the Governor-General acting with counsellors. | [Prelims 2024] The All India Federation was provided for but never came into being, because the princely states did not accede. So dyarchy at the Centre existed only on paper. Only provincial autonomy (the provincial part of the Act) was implemented. Defence and Foreign Affairs were not federal subjects; they were the Governor General's reserved subjects. In short: the Act abolished dyarchy in the provinces and provided for it at the Centre. |
| 1935 Act - franchise & communal representation, 1935 | **1.** Extended communal/separate representation to the depressed classes, women, labour and commerce/industry; provided reserved seats for women in the provincial legislatures and for Scheduled Castes (post-Poona Pact). | [Prelims 2021] The 1935 Act gave women reserved seats in the legislature. The 1935 Act had no Preamble of its own; it relied on the Preamble to the 1919 Act, which carried the promise of responsible government. |
