| Provisional Parliament, 26 Jan 1950 to Apr 1952 | **1.** After commencement, the Constituent Assembly continued as the Provisional Parliament of India until the first general elections (Oct 1951 - Feb 1952);<br>**2.** The new Parliament met in May 1952.<br>**3.** G.V. Mavalankar became the first Speaker of the Lok Sabha. | **1.** The First Constitutional Amendment, 1951 (Ninth Schedule, Arts 15(4), 31A, 31B) was passed by the Provisional Parliament - i.e. by the very body that framed the Constitution, not by an elected Lok Sabha.<br>**2.** Upheld in Shankari Prasad (1951). |
| Dr B.R. Ambedkar's role, 1947 to 1950 | **1.** Chairman of the Drafting Committee and independent India's first Law Minister.<br>**2.** Elected to the Assembly first from Bengal (Jessore-Khulna), lost the seat to partition, and re-entered from Bombay.<br>**3.** Known as the 'Chief Architect of the Constitution' and 'a modern Manu'. | **1.** He was not President of the Assembly (that was Rajendra Prasad) and did not write the first draft (that was Sir B.N. Rau, Constitutional Adviser).<br>**2.** He also chaired no other Assembly committee, and famously defended Art 32 as the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution. |
| Composition & diversity of the Assembly, 1946 to 1949 | **1.** 15 women members. The names most likely in a question:<br>**(a)** Sarojini Naidu, Hansa Mehta, Rajkumari Amrit Kaur, Durgabai Deshmukh.<br>**(b)** Dakshayani Velayudhan: the only Dalit woman member.<br>**(c)** Others: Sucheta Kripalani, Renuka Ray, Begum Aizaz Rasul, Ammu Swaminathan, Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit, Annie Mascarene.<br>**2.** Members drawn from every major religion;<br>**3.** 28 members from the Scheduled Castes. | **1.** Dakshayani Velayudhan was the only Dalit woman member;<br>**2.** Begum Aizaz Rasul the only Muslim woman member.<br>**3.** Hansa Mehta, not Nehru, is credited with pressing 'all human beings' into the UDHR - a common personality-mapping question. |
| Criticisms of the Constituent Assembly, 1946 to 1949 | **1.** Not a representative body - indirectly elected on a restricted franchise (~28% of adults), not adult suffrage; 2. not a sovereign body - created under a British plan and initially sat with British permission; 3. time-consuming - critics called it 'elephantine';<br>**4.** Congress-dominated - Granville Austin: 'a one-party country and a one-party Assembly' 5. lawyer-politician dominated, at the cost of plain language;<br>**6.** Hindu-dominated after the League's exit - Winston Churchill called it a body of 'men of straw' representing 'only one major community'. | **1.** Counters worth quoting: it became fully sovereign after 15 Aug 1947 and never needed British assent; 2. the US Convention (1787) took 4 months but produced a 7-article text against India's 395;<br>**3.** Congress was itself an umbrella and co-opted opponents on merit - Ambedkar, Syama Prasad Mookerjee, Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar.<br>**4.** Austin also called it 'India in microcosm'. |
