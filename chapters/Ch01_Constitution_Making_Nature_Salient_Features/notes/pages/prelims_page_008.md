| Wavell Plan & Simla Conference, 14 Jun to 14 Jul 1945 [PRELIMS 2008] | **1.** Announcement: 14 June 1945, in a broadcast by Viceroy Lord Wavell, and simultaneously by Secretary of State L.S. Amery in the House of Commons.<br>**2.** Simla Conference: Talks with Indian leaders from 25 June to 14 July 1945.<br>**3.** Core offer: Reconstitution of the Viceroy’s Executive Council with all Indian members, except the Viceroy and the Commander-in-Chief (who would remain War Member).<br>**4.** Parity: Equal numbers of Muslims and Caste Hindus, with representation for Scheduled Castes, Sikhs and other interests.<br>**5.** External Affairs: To pass to an Indian member (except tribal and frontier matters linked to defence); India to have fully accredited representatives abroad.<br>**6.** Nature: An interim arrangement under the GoI Act, 1935, until Indians framed a new constitution; the Viceroy’s veto was retained.<br>**7.** Failure: Jinnah insisted that the Muslim League alone could nominate all Muslim members, rejecting any Congress-nominated Muslim (Congress was led by Maulana Azad); Wavell announced the breakdown on 14 July 1945.<br>**8.** Next step: The Cabinet Mission (1946). | **1.** [Prelims 2008 answer] The proposal to reconstitute the Viceroy's Executive Council with Indians holding the portfolios came from the Simla Conference (Wavell Plan) - not the Simon Commission, the Cripps Proposals or the Cabinet Mission.<br>**2.** Keep the two apart: Wavell Plan 1945 = an interim executive inside the 1935 Act;<br>**3.** Cabinet Mission 1946 = the constitution-making machinery and the Constituent Assembly.<br>**4.** The Commander-in-Chief stayed on as War Member - the Council was Indianised, defence was not; and the Viceroy kept his veto.<br>**5.** Simla 1945 (Wavell Plan) is not the Simla Deputation of 1906 (Muslim demand for separate electorates) and not the Simla Agreement of 1972.<br>**6.** Wavell, not Mountbatten, was Viceroy in 1945-47 up to March 1947. |
| Cabinet Mission Plan, 16 May 1946 [PRELIMS 2015] | **1.** Mission of three British Cabinet ministers - Pethick-Lawrence (Secretary of State), Stafford Cripps and A.V. Alexander.<br>**2.** Rejected the demand for a full-fledged Pakistan; 3. proposed a loose Union with a three-tier structure and grouping of provinces (Groups A, B, C); 4. laid down the scheme and composition of the Constituent Assembly and an interim government. | **1.** The Mission rejected Pakistan - a frequent reversal in MCQs.<br>**2.** Lord Wavell was the Viceroy, NOT a member of the Mission. The Muslim League first accepted, then withdrew and called Direct Action Day (16 Aug 1946). |
| Constituent Assembly - composition, 1946 [PRELIMS 2013] | **1.** Total strength 389 = 296 British India + 93 princely states.<br>**(a)** The 296 were 292 from the 11 Governors' provinces and 4 from the Chief Commissioners' provinces (Delhi, Ajmer Merwara, Coorg, British Baluchistan).<br>**2.** Seats allotted in proportion to population - roughly 1 seat per million;<br>**3.** Each province's seats are divided among Muslims, Sikhs and General in proportion to their population. | [Prelims 2013] Provincial members were elected by the members of the Provincial Legislative Assemblies by proportional representation with a single transferable vote - indirect election, NOT direct election by the people and NOT nomination by the Congress/League. The 93 princely-state seats were to be filled by nomination by the rulers. |
| Elections and post-partition strength, Jul to Aug 1946 → 1947 | **1.** Elections held July-August 1946 for the 296 British India seats: Congress 208, Muslim League 73, others<br>**2.** After partition the Assembly's strength fell to 299 - 229 from the provinces + 70 from the princely states. | **1.** 299 is the working strength after 15 Aug 1947. Muslim League members from areas going to Pakistan withdrew, which is why the residual Assembly was so heavily Congress.<br>**2.** Princely states joined gradually - the last only by April 1948. |
