EVENT AND YEAR KEY PROVISIONS / SIGNIFICANCE TRAP
First sitting & Dr
Sachchidananda Sinha,
9 Dec 1946
[PRELIMS 2024]
1. First meeting in the Constitution Hall (now the
Central Hall of Parliament House), New Delhi,
attended by around 205-210 members
including 9 women.
2. Dr Sachchidananda Sinha, the oldest member,
was made temporary / provisional President
following French practice.
3. The Muslim League boycotted and pressed for a
separate Pakistan.
[Prelims 2024] The provisional President
was Dr Sachchidananda Sinha - not
Rajendra Prasad, Ambedkar,
Rajagopalachari or T.T. Krishnamachari.
Watch the name: Sachchidananda Sinha
(1946) ≠ Satyendra Prasad Sinha (1909
Law Member).
Dr Rajendra Prasad
elected President, 11
Dec 1946
1. Elected permanent President of the
Constituent Assembly;
2. H.C. Mukherjee elected Vice-President (later
also V.T. Krishnamachari). Sir B.N. Rau appointed
Constitutional Adviser to the Assembly - he
prepared the original draft (Oct 1947) that the
Drafting Committee scrutinised.
1. Rajendra Prasad presided over the
Assembly and later became the first
President of India (elected 24 Jan
1950, sworn in 26 Jan 1950).
2. B.N. Rau was an adviser, not a
member of the Assembly's Drafting
Committee - yet the first draft was
his, not Ambedkar's.
Objectives Resolution,
Moved 13 Dec 1946 ·
Adopted 22 Jan 1947
1. Moved by Jawaharlal Nehru.
2. Declared India an Independent Sovereign
Republic;
3. a Union of the British Indian territories, the
princely states and other willing areas, with
residuary powers in the autonomous units;
4. all power and authority derived from the
people;
5. guaranteed justice - social, economic and
political, equality of status and opportunity
before the law, and freedom of thought,
expression, belief, faith, worship, vocation,
association and action subject to law and public
morality;
6. safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal
areas and depressed/other backward classes;
7. integrity of the territory and sovereign rights on
land, sea and air; and India's contribution to world
peace and human welfare.
1. It is the basis of the Preamble but
NOT a verbatim copy.
2. It said 'Republic' in Dec 1946, when
India was NOT even a Dominion.
3. It contains no mention of 'Socialist' or
'Secular' - those came with the 42nd
Amendment, 1976.
4. It also promised the units residuary
powers - which the final Constitution
reversed (residuary powers to the
Union, Art 248).
Indian Independence
Act, 1947
1. Based on the Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947);
2. Royal assent 18 July 1947.
3. From 15 August 1947 two independent
Dominions - India and Pakistan - each free to
secede from the Commonwealth.
4. Crown paramountcy over ~560 princely states
lapsed, leaving them free to join either Dominion
or remain independent.
5. Ended British rule; abolished the office of
Secretary of State for India;
6. dropped 'Emperor of India' from the royal title.
1. The Act gave Dominion status, NOT a
republic - India became a republic only
on 26 Jan 1950.
2. Governors-General became
constitutional heads bound by
ministers' advice.
3. Each Dominion got its own
Governor-General - Mountbatten
(India), Jinnah (Pakistan) - there was
no common GG.
4. C. Rajagopalachari (1948) was the
first Indian and last GG.
National Flag, 22 July
1947
1. The Constituent Assembly adopted the National
Flag on 22 July 1947, on a motion by Nehru.
2. The Ad hoc Committee on the National Flag
was chaired by Dr Rajendra Prasad.
1. Keep the symbol dates apart: Flag 22
July 1947 · Anthem and Song 24 Jan
1950 · State Emblem (Sarnath Lion
Capital) 26 Jan 1950.
2. The Ashoka Chakra replaced the
charkha of the Congress flag.

EVENT AND YEAR KEY PROVISIONS / SIGNIFICANCE TRAP
Constituent Assembly
as sovereign body &
legislature, 15 Aug 1947
1. From 15 Aug 1947 the Assembly became a fully
sovereign body free to frame any constitution
and to repeal or alter any Act of the British
Parliament, including the Indian Independence
Act itself.
2. It also assumed the role of the Dominion
Legislature - 'Constituent Assembly
(Legislative)' - first meeting 17 Nov 1947,
Speaker G.V. Mavalankar.
1. Two hats, two chairs: as
constitution-maker Dr Rajendra
Prasad presided;
2. as legislature G.V. Mavalankar
presided.
3. Same members, separate sittings - so
the criticism 'not a sovereign body' is
only true of 1946-47.
Drafting Committee, 29
Aug 1947
[PRELIMS 2023]
1. Set up by resolution on 29 August 1947 to
scrutinise B.N. Rau's draft.
2. Seven members:
(a) Dr B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman), Alladi
Krishnaswami Ayyar, N. Gopalaswami
Ayyangar, K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad
Saadulla.
(b) B.L. Mitter: resigned on health grounds,
replaced by N. Madhava Rau.
(c) D.P. Khaitan: died in 1948, replaced by T.T.
Krishnamachari.
[Prelims 2023] The Drafting Committee
was set up on 29 Aug 1947, not on 26
Nov 1949, which is Constitution Day.
The resolution named no chairman; the
Committee elected Ambedkar at its first
meeting on 30 Aug 1947.
Membership was always seven, though
the individuals changed.
Major committees -
Nehru's three, 1947
1. Union Powers Committee - Jawaharlal Nehru;
2. Union Constitution Committee - Jawaharlal
Nehru;
3. States Committee (Committee for Negotiating
with the States) - Jawaharlal Nehru.
1. Nehru chaired three of the eight
major committees - MCQs habitually
shift the Union Constitution
Committee to Patel or Ambedkar.
2. The Union Powers Committee
recommended a strong Centre,
reversing the Objectives Resolution's
promise of residuary powers to the
units.
Major committees -
Patel's two, 1947
1. Provincial Constitution Committee - Sardar
Vallabhbhai Patel;
2. Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights,
Minorities and Tribal & Excluded Areas - Sardar
Vallabhbhai Patel.
1. Patel's Advisory Committee worked
through sub-committees:
Fundamental Rights - J.B. Kripalani;
2. Minorities - H.C. Mukherjee;
3. North-East Frontier Tribal Areas &
Assam Excluded Areas - Gopinath
Bardoloi;
4. Excluded & Partially Excluded Areas
other than Assam - A.V. Thakkar.
5. Kripalani, not Ambedkar, chaired the
Fundamental Rights sub-committee.
Major committees -
Rajendra Prasad's, 1946
to 1949
1. Committee on Rules of Procedure - Dr Rajendra
Prasad;
2. Steering Committee - Dr Rajendra Prasad.
3. He also chaired the Finance and Staff
Committee and the Ad hoc Committee on the
National Flag.
1. Prasad chaired the procedural and
housekeeping committees;
2. Ambedkar chaired only the Drafting
Committee and was not a member of
the Union Constitution or Union
Powers Committees.
3. The 'eight major committees' = Union
Powers, Union Constitution, Provincial
Constitution, Drafting, Advisory, Rules
of Procedure, States, Steering.
Minor / expert
committees, 1947 to
1949
1. Committee on Chief Commissioners' Provinces
- B. Pattabhi Sitaramayya;
2. House Committee - B. Pattabhi Sitaramayya;
1. The Committee on Chief
Commissioners' Provinces was
chaired by B. Pattabhi Sitaramayya -

#### N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar was only a
member of it. S. Varadachariar

Adoption of the
Constitution, 26 Nov
1949
1. The Assembly 'adopted, enacted and gave to
itself' the Constitution - Preamble + 395
Articles + 22 Parts + 8 Schedules.
2. It has been celebrated as Constitution Day /
Samvidhan Divas since 2015 (earlier observed
as National Law Day).
On this date the Constitution was
adopted, not commenced. But 15
Articles came into force at once under
Art 394, so that elections could be held
and institutions set up before 26 Jan
1950:
(a) Citizenship: Arts 5 to 9.
(b) Oath of the President: Art 60.
(c) Elections: Art 324.
(d) Interpretation: Arts 366, 367.
(e) Transitional provisions: Arts 379,
380, 388, 391, 392, 393.
Manuscript, calligraphy
& artwork, 1949 to
1950
1. The Constitution was handwritten, not printed -
flowing italic calligraphy by Prem Behari Narain
Raizada, who charged no fee;
2. decorated by artists of Santiniketan under
Acharya Nandalal Bose, with the Preamble page
illuminated by Beohar Rammanohar Sinha;
3. 22 miniature-style illustrations.
4. Originals preserved in helium-filled cases in the
Parliament Library.
1. Two handwritten originals - Hindi and
English - both signed on 24 Jan 1950.
2. Raizada's only condition was that his
name appear on every page and his
grandfather's on the last page.
3. The 22 illustrations correspond to
the 22 Parts, tracing Indian civilisation
from Mohenjo-daro to the freedom
movement.
Signing & final session,
24 Jan 1950
1. Last session of the Constituent Assembly.
2. 284 members signed the Constitution (the Hindi
and English handwritten copies) in Constitution
Hall;
3. 'Jana Gana Mana' adopted as the National
Anthem and 'Vande Mataram' given equal
status as the National Song;
4. Dr Rajendra Prasad was elected the first
President of India.
1. Three dates routinely swapped:
adopted 26 Nov 1949 · signed 24 Jan
1950 · commenced 26 Jan 1950.
2. The Anthem and Song were adopted
on 24 Jan 1950, not on 26 Nov 1949
and not on Republic Day.
3. It rained that day, which Prasad noted
as an omen.
Commencement, 26 Jan
1950
1. The Constitution came into force;
2. India became a sovereign democratic republic.
3. Art 395 repealed the Indian Independence Act
1947 and the Government of India Act 1935
(with all amendments and supplementary Acts).
4. The date was chosen to honour the Purna
Swaraj declaration of 26 Jan 1930, resolved at
the Lahore session of Dec 1929.
1. Art 395 repealed the 1935 and 1947
Acts but expressly NOT the Abolition
of Privy Council Jurisdiction Act,
1949.
2. 26 Jan = Republic Day
(commencement);
3. 26 Nov = Constitution Day (adoption);

Act). 'Sovereign Democratic Republic'

Provisional Parliament,
26 Jan 1950 to Apr
1952
1. After commencement, the Constituent
Assembly continued as the Provisional
Parliament of India until the first general
elections (Oct 1951 - Feb 1952);
2. The new Parliament met in May 1952.
3. G.V. Mavalankar became the first Speaker of
the Lok Sabha.
1. The First Constitutional Amendment,
1951 (Ninth Schedule, Arts 15(4), 31A,
31B) was passed by the Provisional
Parliament - i.e. by the very body that
framed the Constitution, not by an
elected Lok Sabha.
2. Upheld in Shankari Prasad (1951).
Dr B.R. Ambedkar's role,
1947 to 1950
1. Chairman of the Drafting Committee and
independent India's first Law Minister.
2. Elected to the Assembly first from Bengal
(Jessore-Khulna), lost the seat to partition, and
re-entered from Bombay.
3. Known as the 'Chief Architect of the
Constitution' and 'a modern Manu'.
1. He was not President of the Assembly
(that was Rajendra Prasad) and did
not write the first draft (that was Sir
B.N. Rau, Constitutional Adviser).
2. He also chaired no other Assembly
committee, and famously defended
Art 32 as the 'heart and soul' of the
Constitution.
Composition & diversity
of the Assembly, 1946
to 1949
1. 15 women members. The names most likely in a
question:
(a) Sarojini Naidu, Hansa Mehta, Rajkumari
Amrit Kaur, Durgabai Deshmukh.
(b) Dakshayani Velayudhan: the only Dalit
woman member.
(c) Others: Sucheta Kripalani, Renuka Ray,
Begum Aizaz Rasul, Ammu Swaminathan,
Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit, Annie Mascarene.
2. Members drawn from every major religion;
3. 28 members from the Scheduled Castes.
1. Dakshayani Velayudhan was the only
Dalit woman member;
2. Begum Aizaz Rasul the only Muslim
woman member.
3. Hansa Mehta, not Nehru, is credited
with pressing 'all human beings' into
the UDHR - a common
personality-mapping question.
Criticisms of the
Constituent Assembly,
1946 to 1949
1. Not a representative body - indirectly elected on
a restricted franchise (~28% of adults), not
adult suffrage;
2. not a sovereign body - created under a British
plan and initially sat with British permission;
3. time-consuming - critics called it 'elephantine';
4. Congress-dominated - Granville Austin: 'a
one-party country and a one-party Assembly'
5. lawyer-politician dominated, at the cost of plain
language;
6. Hindu-dominated after the League's exit -
Winston Churchill called it a body of 'men of
straw' representing 'only one major community'.
1. Counters worth quoting: it became
fully sovereign after 15 Aug 1947 and
never needed British assent;
2. the US Convention (1787) took 4
months but produced a 7-article text
against India's 395;
3. Congress was itself an umbrella and
co-opted opponents on merit -
Ambedkar, Syama Prasad Mookerjee,
Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar.
4. Austin also called it 'India in
microcosm'.
