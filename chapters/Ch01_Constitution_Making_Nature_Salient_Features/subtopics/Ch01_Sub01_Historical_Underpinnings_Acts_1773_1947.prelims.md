## CHAPTER 01
MAKING OF THE INDIAN CONSTITUTION

### THEME ANALYSIS
1. REPEATS

#### A. Legacy of the Colonial Acts
- Government of India Act, 1935: residuary powers with the Governor General [2018].
- It’s Centre State scheme borrowed into our Constitution [2012].
- Instrument of Instructions becoming the Directive Principles of State Policy [2010].
- The All India Federation that never came into being [2009, 2024].
- Simon Commission seeking responsible government in place of dyarchy [2010].

#### B. Missions and Plans
- Cripps Mission: Dominion Status, not full independence [2016, 2009].
- Its Constitution making body [2009] and provincial opt out clause [2022].
- Sent by Churchill [2009]; Nehru and Azad negotiated [2010]; rejection led to Quit India [2013].
- Simla Conference: Indians in every portfolio, including War Member [2008].
- Cabinet Mission recommended a federal government only [2015].

#### C. The Constituent Assembly
- Members indirectly elected by Provincial Legislative Assemblies [2013].
- Dr Sachchidananda Sinha as Provisional President before Dr Rajendra Prasad [2024].
- Drafting Committee under Ambedkar, set up 29 August 1947 [2023].

#### D. The Format Itself
- Half true statements on federation and composition [2009, 2015, 2022, 2024].
- Statement I and Statement II pairing of date with explanation [2023].
- Absolutes such as full independence mark wrong options.
2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED
- 2008 to 2016: one line recall, mostly single answer stems on missions and the 1935 Act [2008, 2009, 2010, 2013,
2016]
- 2022 and 2024: two statement sets on precise clauses, the Cripps opt out [2022] and the unrealised 1935 federation

- **[2024]** 
- 2023: the Statement I and Statement II format arrives, pairing a date with its explanation [2023]
3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW
- Clauses that failed or never operated: the 1935 federation, and Defence and Foreign Affairs as reserved subjects

- **[2024]** 
- Exact dates and offices: the Drafting Committee date [2023], the Provisional President [2024]
- Expect one true and one false statement on a single Act or mission

EVENT AND YEAR KEY PROVISIONS / SIGNIFICANCE TRAP
Montagu Declaration
(August Declaration), 20
Aug 1917
Secretary of State E.S. Montagu declared British
policy to be the 'increasing association of Indians in
every branch of the administration and the gradual
development of self-governing institutions, with a
view to the progressive realisation of responsible
government in India as an integral part of the
British Empire'.
1. The first official commitment to
responsible government - and the
goal was 'responsible government',
never 'Dominion status'.
2. The 1917 declaration is the
preamble/promise behind the 1919
Act.
Road to the 1935 Act,
1927 to 1935
[PRELIMS 2010,2009]
From Simon to the 1935 Act (1927 to 1935)
(a) Simon Commission: appointed 1927, reported
1930.
(b) Nehru Report (1928, Motilal Nehru): the first
Indian draft constitution, with a list of
fundamental rights and Dominion status.
(c) Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929).
(d) Three Round Table Conferences (1930 to
1932).
(e) Communal Award (1932, Ramsay MacDonald),
then the Poona Pact (September 1932).
(f) White Paper (1933), then the Joint Select
Committee (Lord Linlithgow), then the GoI Act
1935.
1. The Communal Award gave separate
electorates to the Depressed
Classes;
2. The Poona Pact replaced them with
reserved seats in joint electorates.
3. Gandhi attended only the Second
RTC;
4. Ambedkar attended all three.
5. Fundamental rights enter Indian
constitutional thinking with the Nehru
Report (1928), not the 1935 Act,
which has none.
Government of India
Act, 1935
[PRELIMS
2018,2012,2010]
1. Longest British statute till then (321 sections, 10
schedules).
2. Provincial autonomy replacing dyarchy -
provinces as autonomous units with responsible
ministries, in force from 1 April 1937;
3. three lists - Federal (59), Provincial (54),
Concurrent (36);
4. Bicameralism in 6 of the 11 provinces (Bengal,
Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, UP);
5. Abolished the Council of India (Secretary of
State given advisers); provided for a Federal
Court (set up 1937) and for Federal, Provincial
and Joint Public Service Commissions.
[Prelims 2018] Residuary powers went to
the Governor General, not to the Federal
Legislature, the Provincial Legislatures or
the Governors.
Burma was separated from India (1937)
by a separate Act; Sind and Orissa
became new provinces.
The Act is the single largest source of our
Constitution: the federal scheme,
Governors, judiciary, PSCs, emergency
powers and administrative detail.
1935 Act - Federation &
dyarchy at the Centre,
1935
[PRELIMS 2024]
1. Provided an All-India Federation of the British
Indian provinces (compulsory) plus the princely
states (voluntary accession) - it needed rulers
entitled to not less than half the 104
princely-state seats in the Council of State to
accede.
2. Introduced dyarchy at the Centre: defence,
external affairs, ecclesiastical affairs and tribal
areas reserved to the Governor-General acting
with counsellors.
[Prelims 2024] The All India Federation
was provided for but never came into
being, because the princely states did not
accede.
So dyarchy at the Centre existed only on
paper. Only provincial autonomy (the
provincial part of the Act) was
implemented.
Defence and Foreign Affairs were not
federal subjects; they were the Governor
General's reserved subjects.
In short: the Act abolished dyarchy in the
provinces and provided for it at the
Centre.
1935 Act - franchise &
communal
representation, 1935
1. Extended communal/separate representation
to the depressed classes, women, labour and
commerce/industry; provided reserved seats
for women in the provincial legislatures and for
Scheduled Castes (post-Poona Pact).
[Prelims 2021] The 1935 Act gave women
reserved seats in the legislature.
The 1935 Act had no Preamble of its
own; it relied on the Preamble to the 1919
Act, which carried the promise of
responsible government.

Idea and demand for a
Constituent Assembly,
1934 to 1942
1. Idea first put forward by M.N. Roy (1934), a
pioneer of the communist movement in India;
2. officially demanded by the Indian National
Congress in 1935;
3. Nehru (1938) declared it must be elected on

2. The Assembly finally came under the
Cabinet Mission Plan 1946, not the
Cripps proposals - and, contrary to
Nehru's 1938 demand, it was never
elected on adult franchise.
Cripps Mission, Mar to
Apr 1942
[PRELIMS
2022,2016,2013,2010,
2009]
1. Context: Sent by Winston Churchill’s War
Cabinet as Japan advanced to India’s eastern
borders after the fall of Rangoon (March 1942).
2. Leader: Sir Stafford Cripps (Lord Privy Seal and
Leader of the House of Commons) arrived in
Delhi in late March 1942; the Draft Declaration
was published at the end of March 1942.
3. Main offer: Dominion status after the war for a
new Indian Union, equal to other Dominions, with
the right to secede from the Commonwealth;
not immediate independence.
4. Constitution-making body (after the war):
- Elected by the Lower Houses of Provincial
Legislatures as a single electoral college,
through proportional representation.
- Princely States to nominate members in
proportion to their population.
5. Right of non-accession: Any province unwilling
to accept the new constitution could stay out
and sign a separate agreement with Britain for
equal status; seen as the seed of Pakistan.
6. During the war: Britain to retain control of
defence.
7. Rejection:
- Congress (negotiators Pt. Nehru and
Maulana Azad, then Congress President)
rejected it over defence and the lack of a real
National Government; Gandhi called it a
“post-dated cheque”.
- Muslim League (no clear acceptance of
Pakistan), Depressed Classes and Sikhs also
rejected it.
1. [Prelims 2022] Constituent Assembly
members were to be elected by the
provincial lower houses - only the
princely states' representatives were
nominated; so 'nominated by the
Provincial Assemblies' is FALSE, while
the province's right to stay out and
sign a separate agreement with
Britain is TRUE.
2. [Prelims 2009 / 2016] Cripps
promised a constitution-making body
and Dominion status, never full
independence.
3. [Prelims 2010] The Congress's official
negotiators were Nehru and Azad, not
Gandhi-Patel, not
Kripalani-Rajagopalachari.
4. [Prelims 2009] The Mission was sent
by Winston Churchill - not MacDonald
(1931 RTC era), Baldwin or
Chamberlain.
5. [Prelims 2013] The failure of the
Cripps Proposals triggered the Quit
India Movement (Bombay, 8 August
1942) - not the Cabinet Mission, Simon
Report or Wavell Plan.
6. Note also that the Constituent
Assembly that actually met came
under the Cabinet Mission Plan 1946,
not Cripps.

Wavell Plan & Simla
Conference, 14 Jun to
14 Jul 1945
[PRELIMS 2008]
1. Announcement: 14 June 1945, in a broadcast by
Viceroy Lord Wavell, and simultaneously by
Secretary of State L.S. Amery in the House of
Commons.
2. Simla Conference: Talks with Indian leaders
from 25 June to 14 July 1945.
3. Core offer: Reconstitution of the Viceroy’s
Executive Council with all Indian members,
except the Viceroy and the
Commander-in-Chief (who would remain War
Member).
4. Parity: Equal numbers of Muslims and Caste
Hindus, with representation for Scheduled
Castes, Sikhs and other interests.
5. External Affairs: To pass to an Indian member
(except tribal and frontier matters linked to
defence); India to have fully accredited
representatives abroad.
6. Nature: An interim arrangement under the GoI
Act, 1935, until Indians framed a new
constitution; the Viceroy’s veto was retained.
7. Failure: Jinnah insisted that the Muslim League
alone could nominate all Muslim members,
rejecting any Congress-nominated Muslim
(Congress was led by Maulana Azad); Wavell
announced the breakdown on 14 July 1945.
8. Next step: The Cabinet Mission (1946).
1. [Prelims 2008 answer] The proposal
to reconstitute the Viceroy's Executive
Council with Indians holding the
portfolios came from the Simla
Conference (Wavell Plan) - not the
Simon Commission, the Cripps
Proposals or the Cabinet Mission.
2. Keep the two apart: Wavell Plan
1945 = an interim executive inside
the 1935 Act;
3. Cabinet Mission 1946 = the
constitution-making machinery and
the Constituent Assembly.
4. The Commander-in-Chief stayed on
as War Member - the Council was
Indianised, defence was not; and the
Viceroy kept his veto.
5. Simla 1945 (Wavell Plan) is not the
Simla Deputation of 1906 (Muslim
demand for separate electorates) and
not the Simla Agreement of 1972.
6. Wavell, not Mountbatten, was Viceroy
in 1945-47 up to March 1947.
Cabinet Mission Plan, 16
May 1946
[PRELIMS 2015]
1. Mission of three British Cabinet ministers -
Pethick-Lawrence (Secretary of State),
Stafford Cripps and A.V. Alexander.
2. Rejected the demand for a full-fledged
Pakistan;
3. proposed a loose Union with a three-tier
structure and grouping of provinces (Groups A,
B, C);
4. laid down the scheme and composition of the
Constituent Assembly and an interim
government.
1. The Mission rejected Pakistan - a
frequent reversal in MCQs.
2. Lord Wavell was the Viceroy, NOT a
member of the Mission. The Muslim
League first accepted, then withdrew
and called Direct Action Day (16 Aug
1946).
Constituent Assembly -
composition, 1946
[PRELIMS 2013]
1. Total strength 389 = 296 British India + 93
princely states.
(a) The 296 were 292 from the 11 Governors'
provinces and 4 from the Chief
Commissioners' provinces (Delhi, Ajmer

2. Seats allotted in proportion to population -

3. Each province's seats are divided among

the rulers.

2. After partition the Assembly's strength fell to

princely states.
1. 299 is the working strength after 15
Aug 1947. Muslim League members
from areas going to Pakistan
withdrew, which is why the residual

### [Prelims Source Page 9](../pages/prelims/prelims_page_009.pdf)

EVENT AND YEAR KEY PROVISIONS / SIGNIFICANCE TRAP
First sitting & Dr
Sachchidananda Sinha,
9 Dec 1946
[PRELIMS 2024]
1. First meeting in the Constitution Hall (now the
Central Hall of Parliament House), New Delhi,
attended by around 205-210 members
including 9 women.
2. Dr Sachchidananda Sinha, the oldest member,
was made temporary / provisional President
following French practice.
3. The Muslim League boycotted and pressed for a
separate Pakistan.
[Prelims 2024] The provisional President
was Dr Sachchidananda Sinha - not
Rajendra Prasad, Ambedkar,
Rajagopalachari or T.T. Krishnamachari.
Watch the name: Sachchidananda Sinha
(1946) ≠ Satyendra Prasad Sinha (1909
Law Member).
Dr Rajendra Prasad
elected President, 11
Dec 1946
1. Elected permanent President of the
Constituent Assembly;
2. H.C. Mukherjee elected Vice-President (later
also V.T. Krishnamachari). Sir B.N. Rau appointed
Constitutional Adviser to the Assembly - he
prepared the original draft (Oct 1947) that the
Drafting Committee scrutinised.
1. Rajendra Prasad presided over the
Assembly and later became the first
President of India (elected 24 Jan
1950, sworn in 26 Jan 1950).
2. B.N. Rau was an adviser, not a
member of the Assembly's Drafting
Committee - yet the first draft was
his, not Ambedkar's.
Objectives Resolution,
Moved 13 Dec 1946 ·
Adopted 22 Jan 1947
1. Moved by Jawaharlal Nehru.
2. Declared India an Independent Sovereign
Republic;
3. a Union of the British Indian territories, the
princely states and other willing areas, with
residuary powers in the autonomous units;
4. all power and authority derived from the
people;
5. guaranteed justice - social, economic and
political, equality of status and opportunity
before the law, and freedom of thought,
expression, belief, faith, worship, vocation,
association and action subject to law and public
morality;
6. safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal
areas and depressed/other backward classes;
7. integrity of the territory and sovereign rights on
land, sea and air; and India's contribution to world
peace and human welfare.
1. It is the basis of the Preamble but
NOT a verbatim copy.
2. It said 'Republic' in Dec 1946, when
India was NOT even a Dominion.
3. It contains no mention of 'Socialist' or
'Secular' - those came with the 42nd
Amendment, 1976.
4. It also promised the units residuary
powers - which the final Constitution
reversed (residuary powers to the
Union, Art 248).
Indian Independence
Act, 1947
1. Based on the Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947);
2. Royal assent 18 July 1947.
3. From 15 August 1947 two independent
Dominions - India and Pakistan - each free to
secede from the Commonwealth.
4. Crown paramountcy over ~560 princely states
lapsed, leaving them free to join either Dominion
or remain independent.
5. Ended British rule; abolished the office of
Secretary of State for India;
6. dropped 'Emperor of India' from the royal title.
1. The Act gave Dominion status, NOT a
republic - India became a republic only
on 26 Jan 1950.
2. Governors-General became
constitutional heads bound by
ministers' advice.
3. Each Dominion got its own
Governor-General - Mountbatten
(India), Jinnah (Pakistan) - there was
no common GG.
4. C. Rajagopalachari (1948) was the
first Indian and last GG.
National Flag, 22 July
1947
1. The Constituent Assembly adopted the National
Flag on 22 July 1947, on a motion by Nehru.
2. The Ad hoc Committee on the National Flag
was chaired by Dr Rajendra Prasad.
1. Keep the symbol dates apart: Flag 22
July 1947 · Anthem and Song 24 Jan
1950 · State Emblem (Sarnath Lion
Capital) 26 Jan 1950.
2. The Ashoka Chakra replaced the
charkha of the Congress flag.
