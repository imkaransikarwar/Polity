- **1.** ED Director: appointed by the Central Government on a committee's recommendation (CVC Act, 2003). (a) Chaired by the Central Vigilance Commissioner. (b) Members: the Vigilance Commissioners and the Secretaries of Home, Personnel and Revenue.

- **2.** The ED holds office for a minimum of 2 years, extendable year by year up to a total of 5 years after the 2021 amendments

- **3.** The CVC chairs the committee recommending appointments of CBI officers of the rank of SP and above (DSPE Act, 1946)

- **4.** Exercises superintendence over the vigilance administration of all Central Ministries, Departments and PSUs, and every Chief Vigilance Officer functions as its extended arm

- **5.** Has the powers of a civil court, and its proceedings are deemed judicial proceedings (CVC Act, 2003) CBI

### STATE CONSENT

- **1.** General consent of the State is needed to act in its area (DSPE Act, 1946)

- **2.** Withdrawn by about 9 States - WB, Kerala, Punjab, Jharkhand, Meghalaya , Mizoram, TN, Telangana, Karnataka (2024)

- **3.** Maharashtra restored consent in 2022

- **4.** The SC/HC can still order a CBI probe without State consent

### HOW THE LOKPAL AND THE CVC REACH THE CBI

- **1.** The CBI's dependence runs both ways - the Lokpal may direct the CBI to conduct a preliminary inquiry or investigation

- **2.** An officer so assigned cannot be transferred without the Lokpal's approval - the same protection the CVC's superintendence gives in PC Act cases

### STRUCTURE

- **1.** Headquarters New Delhi; training at the CBI Academy, Ghaziabad

- **2.** Main investigative divisions - Anti-Corruption, Economic Offences and Special Crimes - supported by Policy, Administration, Prosecution and a Central Forensic Science Laboratory

- **3.** India's National Central Bureau for INTERPOL, the single official channel for police cooperation with foreign countries

### THE STANDING LEGAL QUESTION

- **1.** Because it rests only on the DSPE Act, 1946 and a 1963 MHA resolution and has no statute of its own, the Gauhati High Court in Navendra Kumar v. Union of India (Nov 2013) held the constituting resolution unconstitutional

- **2.** The Supreme Court stayed that ruling within days and the matter remains pending - the standing argument for a dedicated CBI statute rather than reliance on an executive resolution ED

### NO STATE CONSENT NEEDED

- **1.** The ED does not need the general consent of a State

- **2.** Unlike the CBI, whose police powers rest on the DSPE Act, 1946, the ED enforces central laws with all-India jurisdiction, which is why withdrawal of consent by States hits the CBI alone

### TENURE LITIGATION

- **1.** Common Cause, 2021 - the Supreme Court allowed one last extension to the then Director and issued a mandamus barring any further extension

- **2.** Dr Jaya Thakur v. Union of India (26 July 2023) - the Court upheld the CVC (Amendment) Act, 2021 and the DSPE (Amendment) Act, 2021, which allow up to three one-year extensions, a 5-year ceiling for the ED and the CBI Director alike

- **3.** But the specific extensions of November 2021 and November 2022 were held illegal, since a writ of mandamus cannot be annulled by legislation, and a fresh appointment was directed

### PMLA JURISPRUDENCE

Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India (2022) upheld: (a) the ED's powers of search, seizure, arrest and attachment; (b) the twin conditions for bail; (c) that an ECIR is not an FIR and need not be supplied to the accused.
