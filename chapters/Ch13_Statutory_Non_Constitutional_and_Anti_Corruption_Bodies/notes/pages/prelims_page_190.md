### DETAILED NOTES

Additional points NHRC: HUMAN RIGHTS COURTS AND UNION TERRITORY COVERAGE

- **1.** For the speedy trial of offences arising out of violation of human rights, the State Government, with the concurrence of the Chief Justice of the High Court, may by notification specify for each district a Court of Session to be a Human Rights Court (Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993)

- **2.** It is a designated existing court, not a new one, and cannot be so specified if a Special Court already tries those offences

- **3.** A Special Public Prosecutor - an advocate of at least 7 years' practice - is provided for each such court (Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993)

- **4.** Human rights functions for Delhi are discharged by the NHRC itself, not by any State Commission SHRC: HUMAN RIGHTS COURTS AND UNION TERRITORY COVERAGE

- **1.** The Central Government may, by notification, confer on the SHRC of a State the functions of a State Commission in relation to a Union Territory - Delhi excepted (Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993)

- **2.** A person may be Chairperson or Member of more than one State Commission at the same time with his consent - the fix for small States that cannot sustain a full commission (PHRA (Amendment) Act, 2019)

- **3.** Setting up an SHRC is optional - a State Government 'may' constitute one, and several States still have none or leave it vacant (Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993) CIC: APPEALS AND PENALTY POWERS UNDER THE RTI ACT

- **1.** First appeal lies within 30 days to an officer senior in rank to the CPIO in the same public authority (RTI Act, 2005)

- **2.** Second appeal lies to the CIC within 90 days (RTI Act, 2005)

- **3.** The burden of proving that a refusal was justified rests on the CPIO (RTI Act, 2005)

- **4.** Penalty of Rs 250 per day of delay, subject to a maximum of Rs 25,000, imposed personally on the CPIO after giving him a hearing (RTI Act, 2005)

- **5.** May recommend disciplinary action for persistent default (RTI Act, 2005)

- **6.** Crucially, the CIC's decision is binding - unlike the merely recommendatory output of the NHRC or the CVC - subject only to writ jurisdiction (RTI Act, 2005) SIC: APPEALS AND PENALTY POWERS UNDER THE RTI ACT

- **1.** Identical scheme at the State level. First appeal to the senior officer; second appeal to the SIC within 90 days (RTI Act, 2005)

- **3.** The same Rs 250 per day up to Rs 25,000 penalty on the SPIO, and the same power to recommend disciplinary action (RTI Act, 2005). The SIC's decision is likewise binding (RTI Act, 2005)
