## CHAPTER 24: ANTI CORRUPTION BODIES

### THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter

#### 1. REPEATS

#### A. Legal Basis and Status

- None of the four is a constitutional body - the Lokpal and the CVC are statutory, the Lokayukta rests on a State Act, and the CBI has no statute of its own.

- The CVC turned statutory through the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003 after the Vineet Narain judgement.

- The CBI rests on an executive resolution plus the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946, under the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions [2026].

- The ED is not statutory either - it is an executive agency under the Department of Revenue that draws its powers from the PMLA, 2002, FEMA, 1999 and the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018.

#### B. Who Reaches the CBI

- The Lokpal may direct the CBI to conduct a preliminary inquiry or investigation, and an officer so assigned cannot be transferred without the Lokpal's approval.

- The CVC superintends the CBI only in Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 cases; otherwise the CBI answers to the DoPT.

- The CVC chairs the committee recommending CBI officers of the rank of SP and above, and the committee recommending the Director of Enforcement.

- The CVC chairs the committee that recommends the Director of Enforcement, but the ED is not under the CVC's superintendence.

#### C. Headship and Selection Committees

- The Lokpal Chairperson need not be the sitting Chief Justice of India; a former CJI, a former Supreme Court judge or an eminent person qualifies [2025].

- The CVC has no judicial requirement at all and is in practice almost always a retired civil servant.

- The CBI Director is chosen by the Prime Minister, the Leader of the Opposition and the CJI or his nominee, the same trio that removes the Lokpal's own members through a Supreme Court reference.

#### D. Jurisdiction Limits and Traps

- The Lokpal covers Indian public servants posted outside India as well, and the sitting Prime Minister is within its jurisdiction, subject to excluded subjects and a Full Bench two-thirds safeguard [2025].

- The Lokpal has no suo motu power and cannot look at a complaint more than seven years old.

- The CBI needs the general consent of a State to act in its area, which about ten States have withdrawn, though the SC or a High Court can still order a probe.

- The CBI needs a State's general consent; the ED does not, because it enforces central laws with all-India jurisdiction.

#### E. Tenure of the Two Directors

- CBI and ED Directors both get a 2-year minimum (Vineet Narain, 1997), extendable 1 year at a time to a ceiling of 5 years.

- Dr Jaya Thakur, 2023, upheld the CVC (Amendment) Act, 2021 and the DSPE (Amendment) Act, 2021 but struck down the specific extensions given to the then ED Director.

- Also tested: ED, DRI and DGSDM with their parent departments [2025]
