### ADDITIONAL POINTS

### NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR MINORITIES

WHO IS A 'MINORITY' - AND AT WHAT LEVEL

- **1.** The term minority is not defined in the Constitution; Arts 29 and 30 speak of minorities based on religion or language without listing them

- **2.** Notification is purely executive - the Central Government may notify communities as minorities, which is how the current six were added: Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists and Parsis in 1993, and Jains on 27 January 2014 (NCM Act, 1992)

- **3.** The unit of determination matters: minority status is decided State by State. T.M.A. Pai Foundation v. State of Karnataka (2002), eleven judges: for Art 30, the State is the unit. So a community may be a minority in one State and a majority in another.

- **4.** Linguistic minorities are not covered by the NCM at all - their constitutional guardian is the Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities under Art 350B

### NC FOR MINORITY EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS

### STATE-LEVEL AND LOWER-TIER COUNTERPARTS

- **1.** No State counterpart exists - the NCMEI is a Central body only

- **2.** An institution seeking recognition as a minority educational institution applies to the competent authority of the State

- **3.** Against a refusal, cancellation or failure to decide, an appeal lies to the NCMEI, and its decision on that appeal binds the competent authority, subject to writ jurisdiction (NCMEI Act, 2004)

### NCMEI - ITS THREE DISTINCT ROLES

The Commission has three separable roles (NCMEI Act, 2004)

- **1.** Adjudicatory - decides all questions relating to the status of an institution as a Minority Educational Institution, and inquires into complaints of deprivation or violation of the Art 30(1) right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions; for this it has the powers of a civil court and can pass binding orders

- **2.** Advisory - advises the Central or any State Government on any question relating to the education of minorities referred to it

- **3.** Recommendatory - recommends measures and schemes for the effective implementation of programmes for minority educational institutions and for preserving their minority character

- **4.** May also intervene in any court proceeding involving a minority educational institution, and may cancel a minority status certificate obtained by fraud or misrepresentation (NCMEI Act, 2004)

- **5.** Its original mandate included granting direct affiliation of minority professional institutions to a Central university - the reason it was created separately from the NCM

### NC FOR WOMEN

### STATE-LEVEL AND LOWER-TIER COUNTERPARTS

- **1.** State Commissions for Women exist under separate State enactments, not under the NCW Act, 1990

- **2.** The NCW has no appellate, supervisory or coordinating authority over them - a frequently assumed but non-existent hierarchy

### NC FOR PROTECTION OF CHILD RIGHTS

### STATE-LEVEL AND LOWER-TIER COUNTERPARTS

- **1.** Every State must constitute a State Commission for Protection of Child Rights (SCPCR) (CPCR Act, 2005)

- **2.** The State Government, with the concurrence of the Chief Justice of the High Court, may specify a Court of Session in each district as a Children's Court for the speedy trial of offences against children (CPCR Act, 2005)

- **3.** Appointment to the NCPCR is by the Central Government on the recommendation of a three-member Selection Committee chaired by the Union Minister for Women and Child Development - the only body on this tab with a statutory selection committee (CPCR Act, 2005)
