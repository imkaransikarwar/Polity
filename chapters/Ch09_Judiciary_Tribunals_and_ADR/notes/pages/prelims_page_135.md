#### Q7 (2009)

**With reference to Lok Adalats, consider the following statements:**

- **1.** An award made by a Lok Adalat is deemed to be a degree of a civil court and no appeal lies against thereto before any court.
- **2.** Matrimonial/Family disputes are not covered under Lok Adalat.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*

- **(a)** 1 only
- **(b)** 2 only
- **(c)** Both 1 and 2
- **(d)** Neither 1 nor 2

> **Answer:** `(a)` · [Prelims Source Page 135](../../pages/prelims/prelims_page_135.pdf)

Tribunals[PRELIMS 2009]

- **1.** Articles 323A and 323B were inserted by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976.

- **2.** Article 323A enables Parliament alone to provide for administrative tribunals for disputes relating to recruitment and conditions of service of public servants of the Union and the States;

- **3.** Article 323B lets Parliament or a State legislature set up tribunals for other matters: taxation, foreign exchange, industrial and labour disputes, land reforms, elections, essential goods, urban property ceilings. Under Article 323A, the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 was enacted, and the CAT began work on 1 November 1985, under PM Rajiv Gandhi (not Lal Bahadur Shastri).

- **4.** Its members are drawn from both the judicial and the administrative streams.

- **5.** In L. Chandra Kumar (1997) the Supreme Court held that judicial review by the High Courts under Articles 226/227 and by the Supreme Court under Article 32 is part of the Basic Structure, so tribunal decisions are subject to review by a Division Bench of the concerned High Court.

- **6.** Orders of the Central Administrative Tribunal and other tribunals are amenable to the writ jurisdiction of the High Court within whose territorial jurisdiction the tribunal is situated (L. Chandra Kumar, 1997); 7. a High Court's power of superintendence under Article 227 extends over all tribunals in its territory, except military tribunals. Lok Adalats[PRELIMS 2010,2009]

- **1.** Given statutory status by Chapter VI (Sections 19-22) of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987.

- **2.** A Lok Adalat may take up both (i) a case pending before a court and (ii) a matter at the pre-litigation stage falling within the jurisdiction of that court - so it is not confined to either.

- **3.** It may settle civil as well as criminal matters, the only bar being offences that are non-compoundable under any law; 4. matrimonial and family disputes are regularly taken up.

- **5.** Its award is deemed to be a decree of a civil court, is final and binding on all parties, and no appeal lies against it before any court.

- **6.** A bench is not composed only of judicial officers - it typically has a serving or retired judicial officer together with a lawyer and a social worker.

- **7.** National Lok Adalats are held on a single day across all courts in the country.

- **8.** Permanent Lok Adalats under Section 22B deal with disputes relating to public utility services (transport, postal, telegraph, power, water supply, insurance, hospitals, sanitation, etc.), with pecuniary jurisdiction up to Rs 1 crore; 9. uniquely, if conciliation fails they may decide the dispute on merits, but they cannot try an offence. Gram Nyayalayas[PRELIMS 2016]

- **1.** The Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008 (in force from 2 October 2009) provides for a Gram Nyayalaya for every Panchayat at the intermediate level, or a group of contiguous intermediate-level Panchayats, with its headquarters at the intermediate Panchayat headquarters.

- **2.** It is presided over by a Nyayadhikari appointed by the State Government in consultation with the High Court, who has the same status, salary and powers as a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class.

- **3.** It is conceived as a mobile court: the Nyayadhikari periodically visits villages and disposes of cases at places other than its headquarters.
