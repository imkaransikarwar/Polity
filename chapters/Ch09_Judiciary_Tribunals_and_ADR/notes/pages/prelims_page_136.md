Legal Aid & Legal Services Authorities[PRELIMS 2020,2013]

- **1.** Article 39A (DPSP, inserted by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976) obliges the State to secure that the operation of the legal system promotes justice on a basis of equal opportunity and to provide free legal aid so that no citizen is denied justice by reason of economic or other disability.

- **2.** The Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (brought into force 9 November 1995) constituted NALSA, with the Chief Justice of India as Patron-in-Chief and a serving Supreme Court judge nominated by the President as Executive Chairman; 3. the Supreme Court Legal Services Committee handles matters before the Supreme Court.

- **4.** NALSA lays down policies and issues guidelines and directions to the State Legal Services Authorities for implementing legal aid programmes and schemes throughout the country and disburses funds to them.

- **5.** Section 12 entitles the following to free and competent legal services: members of SCs and STs; 6. victims of trafficking in human beings or begar (Article 23); 7. women and children; 8. persons with mental illness or disability; 9. victims of mass disaster, ethnic or caste violence, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster; 10. industrial workmen; 11. persons in custody (including in a protective home, juvenile home or psychiatric hospital); and persons whose annual income is below the ceiling prescribed by the State Government (Rs 5,00,000 for cases before the Supreme Court; commonly Rs 3,00,000 in most States, lower in some).

- **12.** Entitlement is thus not automatic for all senior citizens or for all members of a backward class - an income test applies.

- **13.** The State Legal Services Authority is headed by the Chief Justice of the High Court as Patron-in-Chief, with a serving or retired High Court judge as Executive Chairman nominated by the Governor in consultation with the Chief Justice.

- **14.** Every High Court also has a High Court Legal Services Committee for cases before it.

- **15.** District Legal Services Authority in every district, chaired by the District Judge, and a Taluk (Sub-divisional) Legal Services Committee headed by a senior civil judge, deliver legal aid and organise Lok Adalats at the grassroots.
