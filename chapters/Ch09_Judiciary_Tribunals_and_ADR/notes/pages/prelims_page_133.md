## CHAPTER 17: TRIBUNALS, ADR AND LEGAL AID

### THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter

#### 1. REPEATS

#### A. Constitutional Basis of Tribunals

- Articles 323A and 323B were inserted by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 [2009]

- Article 323A: Parliament alone, and only for recruitment and service matters of public servants; Article 323B: Parliament or a State legislature, for a listed set of subjects

- Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985; the Central Administrative Tribunal began on 1 November 1985 under Rajiv Gandhi, not Lal Bahadur Shastri [2009]

- Members are drawn from both the judicial and the administrative streams [2009]

- L. Chandra Kumar (1997): review under Articles 226, 227 and 32 is Basic Structure, so tribunal orders stay reviewable by a Division Bench of the High Court

#### B. Lok Adalats

- Statutory footing in Chapter VI of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 [2009, 2010]

- Takes both pending and pre litigation matters, and civil as well as criminal ones, the only bar being non compoundable offences [2010]

- The award is deemed a decree of a civil court and no appeal lies against it [2009]

- Matrimonial and family disputes are regularly taken up, which is the standard distractor [2009]

- A bench is not composed of judicial officers alone; Permanent Lok Adalats cover public utility services up to Rs 1 crore and may decide on merits [2010]

#### C. Gram Nyayalayas

- Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008, in force from 2 October 2009, one for every intermediate level Panchayat

- Presided over by a Nyayadhikari with the status, salary and powers of a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class, appointed by the State in consultation with the High Court

- A mobile court that sits in villages, with both civil and criminal jurisdiction, so the claim that it hears only civil cases is wrong [2016]

- Not bound by the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, and local social activists may serve as conciliators [2016]

#### D. Legal Aid and the Legal Services Authorities

- Article 39A, a Directive Principle inserted by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, is the source of free legal aid [2013, 2020]

- NALSA under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, with the Chief Justice of India as Patron in Chief, issues guidelines to the State authorities [2013]

- The tiers: State Legal Services Authority under the Chief Justice of the High Court, District authority under the District Judge, and a Taluk committee below it

- Section 12 entitlement list: SCs and STs, victims of trafficking or begar, women and children, persons with mental illness or disability, industrial workmen and persons in custody [2020]

- The income ceiling and the transgender and senior citizen options are the recurring trap [2020]

- Also tested: Zero FIR under the BNSS [2026]
