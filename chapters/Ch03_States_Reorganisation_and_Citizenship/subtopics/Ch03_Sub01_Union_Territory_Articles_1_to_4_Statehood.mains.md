## Chapter 3
Citizenship
CITIZENSHIP
In the Indian constitutional framework, Citizenship is governed by Articles 5–11 (Part II) and the Citizenship
Act, 1955. It represents a person's legal status as a member of the sovereign state, entitling them to all civil
and political rights.
Acquisition and Renunciation

#### A. Acquisition (5 Ways)
1. Birth:
a. Every person born in India on or after 26th January, 1950 but before 1st July, 1987.
b. Every person born in India between 1st July, 1987 and 2nd February, 2004 is a citizen of India given
that either of their parents is a citizen of the country at the time of birth.
c. Person born in India on or after 3rd December, 2004 is a citizen if both parents are Indians or at least
one parent is a citizen and the other is not an illegal migrant at the time of birth.
2. Descent: Born outside India to an Indian parent. Must be registered at an Indian consulate within one
year of birth.
3. Registration:
a. Minor children of persons who are citizens of India.
b. A person of Indian origin who is a resident of any country outside undivided India.
c. A person of Indian origin who has been a resident of India for 7 years before applying for
registration.
d. A person who is married to an Indian citizen and is ordinarily resident for 7 years before applying
for registration.
4. Naturalization: For foreigners resident for 12 years (1+11) who meet Third Schedule qualifications
(language, character).
5. Incorporation of Territory: If a new territory becomes part of India (Eg. Pondicherry, 1962), the
Government notifies its citizens.

#### B. Renunciation and Loss (3 Ways)
1. Renunciation: A voluntary act where a citizen of full age makes a declaration. Minor children also lose
citizenship but can resume it at 18.
2. Termination: Automatic loss if a citizen voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country (Single
Citizenship principle).
3. Deprivation: Compulsory termination by the Government if citizenship was obtained by fraud,
disloyalty to the Constitution, or during war with an enemy.
CAA-NRC: Concepts and Evolution
1. CAA (Citizenship Amendment Act, 2019): Amends the 1955 Act to grant expedited citizenship to
persecuted minorities (Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis, Christians) from Afghanistan,
Bangladesh, and Pakistan who entered India before 31 Dec 2014.
2. NRC (National Register of Citizens): A register of all legal Indian citizens. Mandated by the 2003
Amendment to identify illegal immigrants.
