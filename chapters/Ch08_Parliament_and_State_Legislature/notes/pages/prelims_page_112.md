Rule 267 (Rajya Sabha) is not one of these devices. It suspends a rule so that the listed business of the day can be set aside. (a) House: Rajya Sabha only. The Rajya Sabha has no adjournment motion, so the Opposition there falls back on Rule 267. (b) Decision: by the Chairman, not by a vote; admission is rare (11 times between 1990 and 2016), and notices on Pegasus (2021) and Manipur (2023) were disallowed. (c) Ruling of 4 December 2025 (Chairman C.P. Radhakrishnan): it can be invoked only for an item listed in the day's business; a notice on an unlisted issue is invalid; it cannot be equated with the Lok Sabha adjournment motion.

### ADJACENT DEVICES THAT ARE COMMONLY CONFUSED WITH THE SIX ABOVE

| DEVICE | RULE AND BASIS | FUNCTION | HOW IT DIFFERS FROM THE SIX ABOVE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Confidence Motion | No specific rule. Moved as an ordinary motion under Rule 184. | A government that has been asked to prove its majority moves it and invites the House to express confidence in it. | **1.** Moved by the government, whereas a No Confidence Motion is moved against it. |
| Cut Motions [PRELIMS 2020] | Rules 209 to 212, Lok Sabha. Moved at the stage of voting on Demands for Grants. | Seek to reduce a Demand for Grant. Three types: Policy Cut (reduce to Re 1), Economy Cut (reduce by a specified amount), Token Cut (reduce by Rs 100 to air a specific grievance). | **1.** A financial device tied to the Budget, not a general discussion device.<br>**2.** Available in the Lok Sabha only, since the Rajya Sabha cannot vote on Demands for Grants.<br>**3.** Adoption amounts to a defeat of the government. |
| Privilege Motion | Rule 222 onwards, Lok Sabha. Rule 187 onwards, Rajya Sabha. | Moved against a Minister or member alleged to have committed a breach of privilege of the House by withholding facts or giving wrong information. | **1.** Directed at a breach of privilege, not at a policy failure.<br>**2.** Available in both Houses.<br>**3.** An adjournment motion may not be used to raise a question of privilege. |
| Zero Hour | Not mentioned in the Rules of Procedure. An Indian innovation dating from the 1960s. | The time immediately after Question Hour, used to raise matters of urgent public importance without prior notice of the usual kind. | **1.** Wholly informal and unwritten, unlike every device in the table above.<br>**2.** No ministerial obligation to reply. |
