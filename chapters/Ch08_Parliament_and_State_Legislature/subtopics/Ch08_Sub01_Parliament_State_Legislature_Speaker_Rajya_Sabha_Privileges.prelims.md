## CHAPTER 11
PARLIAMENT VS STATE LEGISLATURE
THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter
1. REPEATS

#### A. Presiding Officers
- The Speaker resigns to the Deputy Speaker and goes by a majority of all the then members [2012, 2025]
- On dissolution the Speaker stays till the new House meets, unlike a State Assembly Speaker [2018, 2025]
- Under Art 96 a Speaker facing removal may speak and vote, yet shall not preside [2024]
- The Deputy Speaker is elected on a date the Speaker fixes, bound by no convention [2022]
- The Rajya Sabha Chairman comes from outside the House, the Council Chairman from within [2013]

#### B. Composition and Membership
- A Legislative Council is capped at one third of the Assembly and floored at 40 [2015]
- Qualifications under Art 84 and Art 173, disqualification under Art 103 and Art 192
- Office of profit, the Leader of the Opposition, and Union Territory representation

#### C. Lok Sabha versus Rajya Sabha
- Only the Lok Sabha carries a no confidence motion; Emergency approval and impeachment are shared [2022]
- The Rajya Sabha acts alone under Art 249 and Art 312 [2012]
- Ministers answer collectively to the Lok Sabha under Art 75(3) [2022]

#### D. Privileges and Anti Defection
- Art 105 and Art 194 privileges stay uncodified, the bribery immunity now withdrawn
- Tenth Schedule grounds and exceptions, decided by the presiding officer
- Also tested: prorogation and dissolution [2024], sessions [2020], the Governor's address and State Assembly rules

- **[2019]** , the Office of Profit Act [2019], treaty law under Art 253 [2013], UTs in the Rajya Sabha [2012], the Art 249
resolution [2016]
2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED
- 2012 to 2016: single answer and two statement sets on Rajya Sabha powers and Councils [2012, 2013, 2015, 2016]
- 2018 to 2022: code sets on presiding officers, sessions and exclusive powers [2018, 2019, 2020, 2022]
- 2024 to 2025: three statement sets on the Speaker and on prorogation [2024, 2025]
3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW
- Presiding officers in edge cases: the Speaker during a removal resolution [2024] and after dissolution [2025]
- Prorogation and dissolution, and on whose advice [2024]
- Exclusive powers of the Lok Sabha [2022]

DETAILED NOTES
Types of Majority in Parliament
1. Simple (functional/working) majority - more than half of the members present and voting.
– This is the default rule under Art 100(1) and is used for ordinary bills, money bills, confidence and no-confidence motions, censure and adjournment motions, and approval of
proclamations under Arts 356 and 360.
2. Absolute majority - more than half of the total membership of the House (e.g. 272 of 543 in the Lok Sabha). In Lok Sabha (545 seats), 273 seats are required for absolute majority.
– It is not by itself prescribed for any parliamentary decision; it matters chiefly in government formation after a general election.
3. Effective majority - more than half of the effective strength, i.e. total membership minus vacancies ('all the then members').
– Used to remove the Deputy Chairman and Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Arts 90(c), 67(b)) and the Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha (Arts 94(c), 96).
4. Special majority - 4 different forms:
(i) Special majority 1: majority of total membership of each House + not less than two thirds of members present and voting
– Constitutional Amendment (Art 368(2))
– Removal of Supreme Court and High Court judges (Art 124(4), Art 217(1)(b))
– Removal of CEC (Art 324(5)) and CAG (Art 148(1)), on the same grounds and in the same manner as a Supreme Court judge
– Approval of a National Emergency (Art 352(6))
(ii) Special majority 2: not less than two thirds of the total membership of each House
Impeachment of the President (Art 61)
(iii) Special majority 3: not less than two thirds of members present and voting in the Rajya Sabha
– Parliament legislating on a State List subject in the national interest (Art 249)
– Creating a new All India Service (Art 312)
– Note: These are the two exclusive powers of the Rajya Sabha. It alone holds them because it is the Council of States, so the States must first agree before the Union moves into
their domain.
(iv) Special majority + State ratification
Federal provisions under the Art 368(2) need the special majority in (i) + at least half the States ratify by simple majority
