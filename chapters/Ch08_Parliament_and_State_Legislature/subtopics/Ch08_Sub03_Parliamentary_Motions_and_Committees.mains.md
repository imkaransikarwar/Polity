Classification:
1. Standing committees: Permanent committees constituted by the Parliament to deal with specific areas
of public policy or administration.
2. Financial Committees: The Public Accounts Committee (PAC), Committee on Public Undertaking,
Estimates Committee.
3. Committees to Enquire: Committee of privileges, Committee of petitions, Ethics committee.
4. Departmental Standing Committees.
5. Committees to Scrutinise and Control.
6. Committees Relating to the Day-to-Day Business of the House.
7. House-Keeping Committees or Service Committees.
8. Ad hoc committees: Ad hoc committees are temporary committees constituted for a specific purpose
and for a limited period of time. Eg. JPC
Significance of Parliamentary Committees:
1. Workload Management for Parliament (principle of division of labour)
2. Checks and Balances: Eg. The Public Accounts Committee (PAC) has played a critical role in examining
financial irregularities, such as the 2G spectrum allocation scam.
3. Facilitating In-Depth Policy Analysis and Improving Legislative Quality - Committee scrutiny of bills
reduces drafting errors and legal loopholes before enactment.
4. Expert Consultation and Evidence-Based Decision-Making - Committees can invite experts, civil
society groups, and industry representatives for consultations.
5. Cross-Party Consensus Building Committees work away from the adversarial floor, enabling bipartisan
cooperation on sensitive issues.
6. Ad-hoc Joint Parliamentary Committees investigate specific financial scandals and institutional
failures. Eg. JPC on Harshad Mehta stock market scam
7. Budgetary Oversight: Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs) analyze budget allocations
and expenditure trends of ministries, ensuring financial prudence.
8. Continuity in Policy Review: Unlike parliamentary sessions, which are time-bound, committees
function throughout the year, ensuring sustained oversight.
9. Bridging Knowledge Gaps Among MPs - Committee discussions and expert briefings equip MPs with
better understanding of specialized and technical matters.
Challenges:
1. Declining Bill Referrals: Eg. Referral rates dropped from 71% (15th LS) to just 17% (17th LS).
2. Non-Binding Recommendations: The government is not legally required to accept committee
suggestions
3. Short Tenure of Members: The one-year
term for members prevents them from
developing deep subject-matter expertise.
4. Lack of discussion: Since they are
recommendatory in nature, reports of
committees are rarely taken up for
discussion in Parliament.
5. Unlike legislative committees in countries
like the US, Indian committees cannot
enforce their recommendations or
summon ministers.
