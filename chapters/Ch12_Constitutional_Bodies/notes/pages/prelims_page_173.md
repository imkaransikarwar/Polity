Matters on which consultation is NOT required (Art 320) UPSC

- **1.** Consultation is compulsory (Art 320(3)) on:

- (a) methods of recruitment to civil services and posts;

- (b) principles for appointments, promotions and transfers, and the suitability of candidates;

- (c) all disciplinary matters, including memorials and petitions;

- (d) claims for legal costs incurred by a civil servant in defending acts done in the discharge of duty;

- (e) claims for an injury pension.

- **2.** The President may also refer any other matter to the UPSC for advice.

- **3.** Three carve-outs:

- (i) Exemption regulations: the President may make regulations specifying matters on which the UPSC need not be consulted, the UPSC (Exemption from Consultation) Regulations (proviso to Art 320(3)).

- (ii) Reservations: the UPSC need not be consulted on the manner of making reservations under Art 16(4) or on giving effect to Art 335 (SC/ST claims to services) (Art 320(4)).

- (iii) Non-civil posts: its remit is confined to civil services and civil posts, so it has no role in appointments to the higher judiciary, the armed forces, or constitutional and statutory offices.

- **4.** Parliamentary control: every exemption regulation must be laid before each House of Parliament for not less than 14 days, and Parliament may modify or repeal it (Art 320(5)). SPSC

- **1.** Same structure at State level: the same compulsory matters apply, and the Governor may refer any other matter for advice.

- **2.** Carve-outs:

- (i) the Governor makes the exemption regulations (proviso to Art 320(3));

- (ii) the exclusion for reservations under Art 16(4) and Art 335 applies identically to SPSCs (Art 320(4));

- (iii) the remit is likewise confined to civil services and posts of the State.

- **3.** Legislative control: exemption regulations must be laid before the State Legislature for not less than 14 days (Art 320(5)). Joint State PSC and UPSC serving a State (Art 315(2)-(4)) UPSC

- **1.** Two or more States may agree to have one Public Service Commission for the group (Art 315(2))

- **2.** Parliament may by law provide for such a Joint State Public Service Commission (JSPSC), but only if the Legislature of each of those States first passes a resolution to that effect

- **3.** A JSPSC is therefore a statutory body created by Parliament, not a constitutional one like the UPSC and SPSCs

- **4.** Its Chairman and Members are appointed by the President (Art 316(1)), but its annual report goes to the Governor of each participating State, who lays it before that State's legislature (Art 323(2))

- **5.** The UPSC may, if requested by the Governor of a State and with the approval of the President, agree to serve all or any of the needs of that State (Art 315(4)) SPSC

- **1.** An SPSC ceases to exist for a State that joins a JSPSC

- **2.** Only two Joint Commissions have ever been notified in practice

- **3.** The usual arrangement for small States/UTs is instead the UPSC serving the State's needs on the Governor's request with the President's approval (Art 315(4)) - how recruitment for several UTs and smaller States has been handled Extension of functions and acting Chairman (Arts 321, 316(1A)) UPSC

- **1.** The additional route by which a Commission's mandate can be enlarged: an Act of Parliament may provide for additional functions to be exercised by the UPSC as respects Union services and also the services of any local authority, other body corporate constituted by law, or public institution (Art 321)

- **2.** An executive order cannot do this

- **3.** If the office of Chairman falls vacant, or the Chairman is unable to act by reason of absence or otherwise, the President appoints one of the other Members to act as Chairman until a regular appointment is made (Art 316(1A))
