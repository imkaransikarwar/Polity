## CHAPTER 21: CONSTITUTIONAL BODIES (2)

### THEME ANALYSIS PYQ pattern for this chapter

#### 1. REPEATS

#### A. Constitutional Versus Statutory Status

- NCSC sits in Art 338 and NCST in Art 338A after the 89th Amendment Act, 2003 split.

- The National Commission for Backward Classes turned constitutional through Art 338B, inserted by the 102nd Amendment Act, 2018.

- The Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities rests on Art 350B, added by the 7th Amendment Act, 1956.

- Attorney General (Art 76) and Advocate General (Art 165) are constitutional offices, not commissions.

- Of the NCBC, NHRC, Law Commission and NCDRC, only the NCBC is a constitutional body [2023].

#### B. Law Officers Inside the Legislature

- The Attorney General may speak and take part in either House but cannot vote [2013].

- Art 88 covers both Houses, joint sittings and any committee he is named to.

- The Solicitor General holds no such right, which sank statement one [2022].

- Resigning with the appointing government is convention, not a constitutional command [2022].

#### C. How Reports Reach the Legislature

- NCSC, NCST and NCBC report to the President, who lays them before Parliament.

- Findings that concern a state go to the Governor for the state legislature.

- Each report carries a memorandum on action taken over rejected recommendations.

#### D. Composition and Civil Court Powers

- Each commission has a Chairperson, a Vice Chairperson and three other members appointed by the President.

- All three hold civil court powers while investigating a complaint.

- Governments must consult them on major policy affecting Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and backward classes.

#### 2. HOW THE FORMAT HAS CHANGED

- 2013: a single fact on the Attorney General's rights [2013]

- 2022: a two statement set on the AG and the Solicitor General [2022]

- 2023: a "how many are constitutional bodies" count across four commissions [2023]

#### 3. WHAT IS BEING ASKED NOW

- Law officers: only the AG is named in Art 88 [2013, 2022]

- Status counting on the commissions (Arts 338, 338A, 338B) against statutory bodies such as the NHRC [2023]

OTHER LAW OFFICERS (SOLICITOR GENERAL / ADDITIONAL ADVOCATE GENERAL)

### SOLICITOR GENERAL

- **1.** The Solicitor General of India and the Additional Solicitors General are not constitutional posts - only the Attorney General is named (Art 76)

- **2.** They are appointed by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet and governed by the Law Officers (Conditions of Service) Rules, 1987; unlike the AG they are given a fixed term (normally three years) instead of holding office during the President's pleasure

- **3.** They assist the AG but are not his legal subordinates

- **4.** Decisively, only the AG carries the right to speak in and take part in the proceedings of either House (Art 88) - the SG and ASGs have no such right

- **5.** There is no Solicitor General of a State

- **6.** The Advocate General is the only constitutional law officer of a State (Art 165)

- **7.** States do appoint Additional Advocates General, Deputy Advocates General, Government Pleaders and Public Prosecutors, but these rest on executive order or ordinary statute (Public Prosecutors under the BNSS, 2023, formerly the CrPC), with no constitutional standing and no right of audience in the Legislature
