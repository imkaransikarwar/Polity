National Commission For SC, ST, BC's
The National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC), Scheduled Tribes (NCST), and Backward Classes
(NCBC) are constitutional/statutory bodies established to safeguard the rights and socio-economic
development of marginalized communities.
Evolution:
Special Officer for SCs and STs
1. Originally, Article 338 of the Indian Constitution provided for the appointment of a Special Officer for
Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs).
2. The Special Officer was designated as the Commissioner for SCs and STs.
3. Primary task was to investigate all matters relating to the constitutional safeguards for the SCs and STs
and to report to the President on their workings.
65th Constitutional Amendment Act of 1990
1. It amended Article 338 of the Indian Constitution and provided for the establishment of a high-level
multi-member National Commission for SCs and STs.
2. This constitutional body replaced the Commissioner for SCs and STs.
89th Constitutional Amendment Act of 2003
1. Amended Article 338 and inserted a new Article 338-A in the Constitution. It added
a. National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC) - Article 338.
b. National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) - Article 338-A.
NCBC: Statutory body (became constitutional via 102nd Amendment Act, 2018, Article 338B).
Powers and Functions:
1. Investigative Power: To monitor all matters relating to safeguards provided for SCs, STs, and BCs under
the Constitution or any other law.
2. Quasi-Judicial Authority: While investigating any matter, the Commissions have the powers of a Civil
Court:
a. Summoning and enforcing attendance of any person.
b. Discovery and production of any document.
c. Receiving evidence on affidavits.
3. Advisory Role: Participating in and advising on the socio-economic development planning of these
classes.
4. Mandatory Consultation: The Union and State governments must consult these Commissions on all
major policy matters affecting these communities.
5. Presents annual and special reports to the President of India on the working of constitutional
safeguards for SCs.
6. Monitors the implementation of safeguards provided under the Constitution and other laws. Eg.
Atrocities Act, 1989,
Challenges:
1. Commissions' recommendations are advisory, not binding.

National Commission for Women
The National Commission for Women (NCW) is the apex statutory body in India established to protect and
promote the interests of women.
Organisational Structure
1. Chairperson: A person committed to the cause of women, nominated by the Central Government.
2. Five Members: Experts in law, management, administration, or social welfare; at least one must be from
SC and ST communities.
3. Member Secretary: An expert in management or an officer from the Civil Services/All-India Services
nominated by the Centre.
4. The Chairperson and every member hold office for a period not exceeding three years.
Major Powers and Functions
1. Review constitutional and legal safeguards for women and recommend remedial legislative measures to
the government. Eg. Proposed amendments to the Dowry Prohibition Act
2. Receiving and processing complaints related to deprivation of women's rights and non-implementation
of protective laws.
3. Suo Motu Cognizance: Can take independent notice of issues involving women's rights violations based
on media reports or other sources.
4. Civil Court Powers: Authorized to summon witnesses, require document discovery, and receive evidence
on affidavits during inquiries
5. Jail Inspections: Inspects jails, remand homes, or other places of custody for women to ensure humane
treatment and reforms.
